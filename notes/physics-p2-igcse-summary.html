0625 (IGCSE)

Last-Minute Revision Summary

  • SUVAT equations apply ONLY for constant acceleration in a straight line
  • v = u + at (no s); s = 0.5(u+v)t (no a); s = ut + 0.5at^2 (no v); v^2 = u^2 + 2as (no t)
  • Displacement-time graph: gradient = velocity; horizontal = stationary
  • Velocity-time graph: gradient = acceleration; area under graph = displacement
  • Acceleration-time graph: area under graph = change in velocity
  • Projectile motion: horizontal and vertical components are independent; time is common to both
  • Maximum range for projectile launched from ground: theta = 45 degrees; max height when vertical velocity = 0
  • Newton's First Law: object remains at rest or constant velocity unless acted on by resultant force
  • Newton's Second Law: F = ma (resultant force causes acceleration in same direction)
  • Newton's Third Law: action and reaction are equal, opposite, and act on DIFFERENT bodies
  • Weight W = mg; normal reaction R is perpendicular to the surface
  • On inclined planes: component parallel = mg sin theta; component perpendicular = mg cos theta
  • Momentum p = mv; conserved in isolated systems; impulse = F delta t = delta p

Key Formulas

v = u + at
s = ut + 0.5at^2
v^2 = u^2 + 2as
F = ma
p = mv
W = mg

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using g = +9.81 m/s^2 when upward is positive -- g always acts downward
  • Confusing distance (scalar) with displacement (vector) -- distance adds, displacement uses vector addition
  • Forgetting that horizontal velocity in projectile motion remains constant (ignoring air resistance)
  • Confusing Newton's Third Law pairs with balanced forces -- third law pairs act on different objects

Quick Exam Tips

  • Always draw a diagram -- even a simple sketch helps assign correct signs to vectors
  • Define your positive direction explicitly at the start of working
  • Choose the SUVAT equation by identifying which variable is missing from the question
  • For v-t graphs: area = displacement, gradient = acceleration -- do not mix these up!
  • In projectile problems, always resolve the initial velocity into horizontal and vertical components first
  • At maximum height of a projectile, vertical velocity is momentarily zero
  • Show all substitution steps; method marks are awarded for correct equation use even with arithmetic errors
  • Check significant figures: usually match the least precise given value
  • Do not forget to include direction when the answer is a vector
