IBB1: Cell Biology

Overview

Cell Biology (Topic 1) is the foundation of IB Biology, covering cell structure, membrane transport, and cell division. It is tested across all exam papers and is central to the IA, where microscope skills and experimental design are assessed.

1.1 Cell Theory

  • All living organisms are composed of cells.
  • The cell is the basic unit of life.
  • All cells come from pre-existing cells.

Exceptions: viruses are not cells; striated muscle fibres and giant algae are multinucleate; aseptate fungal hyphae are multinucleate.

1.2 Prokaryotic vs. Eukaryotic Cells

FeatureProkaryoteEukaryote
NucleusNo true nucleus; nucleoid regionMembrane-bound nucleus
Ribosomes70S80S (70S in mitochondria/chloroplasts)
Membrane-bound organellesAbsentPresent
Cell wallPeptidoglycanCellulose (plants), chitin (fungi)
Size1–10 μm10–100 μm

1.3 Membrane Structure

The fluid mosaic model describes the cell membrane as:

  • A phospholipid bilayer with embedded proteins.
  • Phospholipids have hydrophilic heads and hydrophobic tails.
  • Membrane proteins: channel proteins, carrier proteins, receptor proteins, glycoproteins.
  • Cholesterol maintains membrane fluidity and stability.

HL only

HL students explain membrane fluidity in terms of lipid composition (saturated vs. unsaturated fatty acids) and temperature effects.

1.4 Membrane Transport

Passive Transport

  • Simple diffusion: movement down a concentration gradient; no energy or proteins needed (small, non-polar molecules: O₂, CO₂).
  • Facilitated diffusion: movement down a concentration gradient through channel or carrier proteins (e.g., glucose transport).
  • Osmosis: diffusion of water across a partially permeable membrane from low to high solute concentration.

Active Transport

  • Movement against a concentration gradient; requires ATP and carrier proteins.
  • Example: sodium–potassium pump (Na⁺/K⁺-ATPase).

Endocytosis & Exocytosis

  • Endocytosis: bulk transport into the cell (phagocytosis for solids, pinocytosis for liquids).
  • Exocytosis: bulk transport out of the cell (e.g., secretion of hormones, neurotransmitters).

1.5 Cell Division — Mitosis

Mitosis produces two genetically identical daughter cells for growth, repair, and asexual reproduction.

  • Interphase: DNA replication (S phase), cell growth (G1 and G2).
  • Prophase: chromatin condenses into chromosomes; nuclear envelope breaks down; spindle forms.
  • Metaphase: chromosomes line up at the equator; spindle fibres attach to centromeres.
  • Anaphase: sister chromatids separate and move to opposite poles.
  • Telophase: nuclear envelopes reform; chromosomes decondense; cytokinesis divides cytoplasm.

1.6 Stem Cells

  • Totipotent: can differentiate into any cell type (zygote).
  • Pluripotent: can differentiate into most cell types (embryonic stem cells).
  • Multipotent: can differentiate into a limited range (adult stem cells, e.g., bone marrow).

Ethical considerations surround the use of embryonic stem cells in research.

Common Misconceptions

  • Prokaryotes vs. eukaryotes: Prokaryotes lack membrane-bound organelles, not just a nucleus.
  • Osmosis: Water moves towards higher solute concentration, not towards the solute itself.
  • Mitosis: DNA replication occurs in interphase, not during mitosis.
  • Active transport: Requires ATP and a protein — diffusion does not.

Exam-Specific Information

  • Paper 1: 4–5 MCQs on cell structure, membrane transport, and mitosis stages.
  • Paper 2: Structured questions on comparing prokaryotes/eukaryotes, osmosis experiments, and stem cell ethics.
  • Paper 3: Data analysis of osmosis or enzyme experiments.
  • IA: Osmosis (potato cores), membrane permeability, and cell size investigations are popular.

IBB2: Molecular Biology

Overview

Molecular Biology (Topic 2) covers the structure and function of biological macromolecules, enzyme kinetics, cellular respiration, and photosynthesis. This is one of the most content-heavy topics and is tested across all papers. Metabolic pathways are frequently examined in data-based questions.

2.1 Biological Molecules

Carbohydrates

  • Monosaccharides: glucose, fructose, galactose (C₆H₁₂O₆).
  • Disaccharides: maltose (glucose + glucose), lactose (glucose + galactose), sucrose (glucose + fructose).
  • Polysaccharides: starch (energy storage in plants), glycogen (energy storage in animals), cellulose (structural, plant cell walls).

Glycosidic bonds link monosaccharides. Cellulose has β-1,4 glycosidic bonds with hydrogen bonding between chains.

Lipids

  • Triglycerides: glycerol + 3 fatty acids; ester bonds; energy storage and insulation.
  • Phospholipids: glycerol + 2 fatty acids + phosphate group; forms cell membranes.

Saturated fatty acids have no C=C bonds; unsaturated have one or more C=C bonds (kinked chains).

Proteins

  • Polymers of amino acids linked by peptide bonds.
  • Primary structure: amino acid sequence.
  • Secondary structure: α-helix and β-pleated sheet (hydrogen bonding).
  • Tertiary structure: 3D folding (ionic bonds, disulfide bridges, hydrophobic interactions, hydrogen bonds).
  • Quaternary structure: multiple polypeptide chains (e.g., haemoglobin).

2.2 DNA Structure & Replication

  • DNA is a double helix of antiparallel polynucleotide strands.
  • Each nucleotide = deoxyribose sugar + phosphate group + nitrogenous base (A, T, C, G).
  • Base pairing: A–T (2 hydrogen bonds), C–G (3 hydrogen bonds).
  • Sugar–phosphate backbone forms the structural framework.

DNA Replication

  • Semi-conservative: each new DNA molecule has one old and one new strand.
  • Helicase unwinds the double helix.
  • DNA polymerase synthesises new strands in the 5'→3' direction.
  • Ligase joins Okazaki fragments on the lagging strand.

2.3 Transcription & Translation

Transcription

  • Occurs in the nucleus.
  • RNA polymerase synthesises mRNA from a DNA template.
  • Introns are spliced out in eukaryotes; exons are joined.

Translation

  • Occurs at ribosomes in the cytoplasm.
  • tRNA carries amino acids to the ribosome.
  • Codon–anticodon pairing ensures correct amino acid sequence.
  • Start codon: AUG (methionine); stop codons: UAA, UAG, UGA.

2.4 Enzymes

  • Enzymes are globular proteins that act as biological catalysts.
  • Active site: specific region where substrate binds.
  • Induced fit model: active site changes shape slightly to fit the substrate.

Factors Affecting Enzyme Activity

  • Temperature: activity increases to an optimum, then decreases due to denaturation.
  • pH: each enzyme has an optimum pH; extremes cause denaturation.
  • Substrate concentration: rate increases until all active sites are saturated (V_max).
Competitive inhibition: inhibitor competes for active site; V_max unchanged, K_m increased.
Non-competitive inhibition: inhibitor binds elsewhere; V_max decreased, K_m unchanged.

2.5 Cellular Respiration

  • Glycolysis: glucose → 2 pyruvate; 2 ATP net, 2 NADH; occurs in cytoplasm.
  • Link reaction: pyruvate → acetyl CoA + CO₂ + NADH; occurs in mitochondrial matrix.
  • Krebs cycle: acetyl CoA oxidised; produces NADH, FADH₂, ATP, CO₂; occurs in matrix.
  • Oxidative phosphorylation: electron transport chain + chemiosmosis; produces most ATP; occurs on inner mitochondrial membrane.
Anaerobic respiration in humans: pyruvate → lactate + NAD⁺
Anaerobic respiration in yeast: pyruvate → ethanol + CO₂ + NAD⁺

2.6 Photosynthesis

  • Light-dependent reactions: occur in thylakoid membranes; produce ATP and NADPH; water is split (photolysis) releasing O₂.
  • Light-independent reactions (Calvin cycle): occur in stroma; CO₂ fixed by RuBisCO into GP, then reduced to G3P using ATP and NADPH; RuBP regenerated.
6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ (requires light and chlorophyll)

Common Misconceptions

  • DNA replication: Occurs before mitosis (in interphase), not during mitosis.
  • Enzyme denaturation: Denaturation destroys the active site shape; it does not break peptide bonds.
  • Photosynthesis: Produces glucose and oxygen from CO₂ and water; oxygen comes from water, not CO₂.
  • Aerobic vs. anaerobic: Anaerobic respiration still uses glycolysis; the difference is in the fate of pyruvate.

Exam-Specific Information

  • Paper 1: 5–6 MCQs on DNA structure, enzyme kinetics, and metabolic pathways.
  • Paper 2: Structured questions on replication, transcription/translation, enzyme experiments, and respiration/photosynthesis.
  • Paper 3: Data analysis of enzyme activity experiments.
  • IA: Enzyme activity (catalase, amylase), respiration rate, and photosynthesis rate experiments are very popular.

IBB3: Genetics

Overview

Genetics (Topic 3) covers inheritance patterns, meiosis, genetic modification, and biotechnology. This topic requires understanding of both theoretical principles and practical applications. Genetic crosses and pedigree analysis are common in Paper 1 and Paper 2.

3.1 Genes & Chromosomes

  • Gene: a heritable factor consisting of a length of DNA that influences a specific characteristic.
  • Allele: one specific form of a gene, differing by base sequence.
  • Locus: specific position of a gene on a chromosome.
  • Genome: the whole genetic information of an organism.

Humans have 23 pairs of chromosomes (46 total). One pair = sex chromosomes (XX female, XY male); 22 pairs = autosomes.

3.2 Meiosis

Meiosis produces haploid gametes (n) from diploid cells (2n), introducing genetic variation.

  • Meiosis I: homologous chromosomes separate (reduction division).
  • Meiosis II: sister chromatids separate (equational division, similar to mitosis).

Sources of Genetic Variation

  • Crossing over: exchange of genetic material between non-sister chromatids in prophase I.
  • Independent assortment: random orientation of homologous pairs at metaphase I.
  • Random fertilisation: any sperm can fertilise any egg.

3.3 Inheritance

Monohybrid Cross

Cross involving one gene. Use Punnett squares to predict offspring ratios.

Genotypic ratio (heterozygous × heterozygous): 1:2:1 (AA:Aa:aa)
Phenotypic ratio (complete dominance): 3:1

Dihybrid Cross

Cross involving two genes. Expected phenotypic ratio for heterozygous × heterozygous = 9:3:3:1.

Non-Mendelian Inheritance

  • Incomplete dominance: heterozygote has intermediate phenotype (e.g., pink snapdragons).
  • Codominance: both alleles expressed (e.g., ABO blood groups).
  • Multiple alleles: more than two alleles for a gene (e.g., ABO: Iᴬ, Iᴮ, i).
  • Sex linkage: gene on a sex chromosome (e.g., red-green colour blindness, haemophilia — X-linked recessive).

Worked Example: Sex Linkage

Question: A carrier female for haemophilia (XᴴXʰ) marries a normal male (XᴴY). What is the probability their son will have haemophilia?

Solution:

Sons inherit Y from father and X from mother.
Mother's gametes: Xᴴ or Xʰ (50% each).
Probability son has haemophilia = 50% (receives Xʰ from mother).

3.4 Genetic Modification

  • Plasmids: small circular DNA used as vectors in bacteria.
  • Restriction enzymes: cut DNA at specific recognition sites, producing sticky or blunt ends.
  • DNA ligase: joins DNA fragments.
  • Promoter: DNA sequence that initiates transcription.

Applications: insulin production, golden rice (vitamin A), herbicide-resistant crops, gene therapy.

3.5 Biotechnology

  • PCR: amplifies DNA sequences using primers, DNA polymerase, and thermal cycling.
  • Gel electrophoresis: separates DNA fragments by size; smaller fragments travel further.
  • DNA profiling: used in forensics, paternity testing, and evolutionary studies.
  • Cloning: somatic cell nuclear transfer (e.g., Dolly the sheep).

Common Misconceptions

  • Meiosis vs. mitosis: Meiosis produces genetically different haploid cells; mitosis produces identical diploid cells.
  • Dominant vs. common: A dominant allele is not necessarily more common in a population.
  • Sex-linked inheritance: Males cannot be carriers for X-linked recessive traits — they are either affected or unaffected.
  • Genetic modification: GM does not always involve adding genes from different species — it can also involve gene editing (e.g., CRISPR).

Exam-Specific Information

  • Paper 1: 4–5 MCQs on inheritance patterns, pedigree analysis, and genetic terminology.
  • Paper 2: Structured questions on genetic crosses, meiosis, genetic modification, and biotechnology.
  • Paper 3: Data analysis of gel electrophoresis or breeding experiments.
  • IA: Genetics simulations, PCR analysis, and breeding experiments are possible IA topics.

Last-Minute Summary

  • Gene = DNA segment; allele = variant of a gene.
  • Meiosis: crossing over + independent assortment → genetic variation.
  • Monohybrid ratio: 3:1; Dihybrid ratio: 9:3:3:1.

IBB4: Ecology

Detailed Notes

1. Ecosystems

  • Food chains/webs: Energy flow; trophic levels
  • Energy transfer: ~10% between levels

2. Nutrient Cycles

  • Carbon cycle: Photosynthesis, respiration, decomposition, combustion
  • Nitrogen cycle: Nitrogen fixation, nitrification, denitrification

3. Conservation

  • Sustainable management of resources

Last-Minute Summary

  • 10% energy transfer between trophic levels
  • Carbon cycle: photosynthesis, respiration, combustion
  • Nitrogen cycle: fixation, nitrification, denitrification

Exam Tips

  • Draw pyramid of energy — always upright
  • Pyramid of biomass usually upright; pyramid of numbers can be inverted
  • Nitrogen-fixing bacteria convert N₂ → ammonia/nitrites
  • Denitrifying bacteria return nitrogen to atmosphere as N₂

IBB5: Evolution

Detailed Notes

1. Natural Selection

  • Variation → competition → survival of fittest → reproduction → inheritance

2. Speciation

  • Geographic isolation → different selection pressures → reproductive isolation

3. Classification

  • Domain, Kingdom, Phylum, Class, Order, Family, Genus, Species

Last-Minute Summary

  • Natural selection: variation → competition → survival → reproduction
  • Speciation needs isolation
  • Classification: Domain → Kingdom → Phylum → Class → Order → Family → Genus → Species

Exam Tips

  • Allopatric speciation: geographic barrier
  • Sympatric speciation: same area (e.g. polyploidy in plants)
  • Binomial nomenclature: Genus species (italicised or underlined)
  • Three domains: Bacteria, Archaea, Eukarya

IBB6: Human Physiology

Detailed Notes

1. Gas Exchange

  • Alveoli: Large surface area, thin walls, good blood supply

2. Respiration

  • Aerobic: Glucose + O₂ → CO₂ + H₂O + ATP
  • Anaerobic (animals): Glucose → lactate + ATP
  • Anaerobic (yeast): Glucose → ethanol + CO₂ + ATP

Last-Minute Summary

  • Alveoli: large SA, thin walls
  • Aerobic: uses O₂
  • Anaerobic: no O₂; lactate in animals, ethanol in yeast

Exam Tips

  • Compare alveolus and villus — both have large SA, thin walls, good blood supply
  • Oxygen debt occurs after anaerobic respiration in muscles
  • Yeast fermentation produces ethanol and CO₂
  • Aerobic respiration produces much more ATP than anaerobic
