Biological Molecules

Last-Minute Revision · 9700 (A-Level)

Last-Minute Revision Summary

  • Carbohydrates: monosaccharides (glucose α and β, fructose, galactose), disaccharides (maltose, sucrose, lactose), polysaccharides (starch, glycogen, cellulose)
  • α-glucose → starch and glycogen; β-glucose → cellulose
  • Glycosidic bonds form in condensation; break in hydrolysis
  • Starch: amylose (helix) + amylopectin (branched); compact, insoluble storage
  • Glycogen: highly branched; rapid glucose release; stored in liver and muscle
  • Cellulose: straight chains, H-bonds → microfibrils; structural support
  • Triglycerides: glycerol + 3 fatty acids; ester bonds; energy storage
  • Phospholipids: glycerol + 2 fatty acids + phosphate; hydrophilic head, hydrophobic tails; membrane structure
  • Saturated fats: no C=C, solid at room temp; unsaturated: C=C, liquid at room temp
  • Proteins: amino acids joined by peptide bonds; primary (sequence), secondary (α-helix, β-sheet), tertiary (3D folding), quaternary (multiple subunits)
  • Collagen: fibrous, triple helix, high tensile strength; Haemoglobin: globular, 4 subunits, transports O₂
  • Water: polar, excellent solvent, high specific heat capacity, cohesive and adhesive
  • Tests: Benedict's (reducing sugar → brick red), iodine (starch → blue-black), Biuret (protein → mauve), emulsion (lipid → white)

Quick Exam Tips

  • For non-reducing sugars: hydrolyse with dilute acid first, then neutralise before Benedict's
  • Iodine test = blue-black (not just 'blue'); Biuret = mauve/purple (not pink)
  • Always link protein structure to function: collagen = strength; haemoglobin = O₂ transport
  • Lipids yield more energy per gram (~37 kJ/g) than carbohydrates (~16 kJ/g)
  • Distinguish α-glucose from β-glucose by the position of -OH on carbon-1
