🏢 Unit 1 — Business & Its Environment

▼

🔑 The Economic Problem

Unlimited wants + Limited resources = Scarcity. Every choice has an opportunity cost — the next best alternative you give up.

⚖️ Economic Systems

Free market: Private ownership, profit motive, consumer sovereignty (e.g., Singapore). Planned: Government controls everything (e.g., North Korea). Mixed: Most real-world economies combine both.

🎯 Business Objectives

Profit maximisation, sales maximisation, survival, satisficing (doing "enough"), CSR. Objectives change as businesses grow — a startup focuses on survival; a multinational on shareholder returns.

Eval tip: Satisficing is realistic but lacks ambition

👥 Stakeholders

Internal: Employees, managers, shareholders. External: Government, suppliers, customers, community, pressure groups. Conflicts arise — shareholders want dividends; employees want higher wages.

📈 External Growth

Horizontal integration (same industry, same stage — e.g., Facebook buying Instagram). Vertical: backward (closer to raw materials) or forward (closer to consumer). Conglomerate: unrelated diversification.

🌍 Case Study: Disney Acquires Fox

Disney's $71.3bn acquisition of 21st Century Fox (2019) was horizontal integration — combining two entertainment giants to dominate streaming content and reduce competition.

📢 Unit 2 — Marketing

▼

🎨 The Marketing Mix (4Ps + 3Ps)

Product (features, branding, packaging), Price (strategies below), Place (distribution channels), Promotion (advertising, PR, sales). For services add: People, Process, Physical Evidence.

💰 Pricing Strategies

Penetration: Low price to enter market (Netflix). Skimming: High price early adopters (Apple iPhone). Psychological: £9.99 not £10. Promotional: Loss leaders to attract customers.

📊 Boston Matrix

Stars (high growth, high share — invest), Cash Cows (low growth, high share — milk for cash), Question Marks (high growth, low share — risky), Dogs (low growth, low share — divest).

Exam trap: Don't keep Dogs hoping they'll improve

🔄 Product Life Cycle

Introduction → Growth → Maturity → Decline. At Introduction: heavy promotion, skimming/penetration. At Maturity: extension strategies (new flavours, new markets). At Decline: harvest or divest.

💡
Did you know? Coca-Cola uses product extension to keep its 130-year-old brand in the "Maturity" stage — Zero Sugar, Cherry, Vanilla, limited editions keep sales growing.

📱 Digital Marketing

SEO (organic search ranking), PPC (pay-per-click ads), Influencer marketing (trusted voices), Content marketing (blogs, videos), Email marketing (highest ROI of any channel).

💵 Unit 3 — Finance & Accounting

▼

🏦 Sources of Finance

Internal: Retained profit (no interest, no dilution), sale of assets. External: Share capital (permanent, dilutes control), loans (interest but keep control), overdrafts (flexible, expensive), debentures (long-term loans), venture capital (high growth potential), crowdfunding (validation + funding).

📉 Cash Flow Problems & Solutions

Causes: Overtrading, late payments, seasonal demand, overstocking. Solutions: Debt factoring (sell receivables), sale and leaseback (sell assets, rent them back), overdrafts, improving credit control.

📊 Key Financial Statements

Income Statement: Revenue − Cost of Sales = Gross Profit. Gross Profit − Expenses = Operating Profit. Operating Profit − Interest & Tax = Net Profit.
Statement of Financial Position: Assets = Equity + Liabilities.

🔍 Ratio Analysis

Profitability: GPM, NPM, ROCE. Liquidity: Current ratio (1.5–2.0 ideal), Acid test (1.0+ ideal). Efficiency: Inventory days, Receivables days, Payables days. Gearing: Debt/Equity ratio — high gearing = high risk but potentially high return.

Exam tip: Always compare ratios over time or against competitors
⚠️ Case Study: Thomas Cook Collapse (2019)

Thomas Cook had £1.7bn debt (high gearing) and negative cash flow for years. Despite selling assets, they couldn't meet obligations. Lesson: High gearing is dangerous in volatile industries.

💹 Investment Appraisal

Payback: How long to recover initial investment? Simple but ignores time value of money. NPV: Discount future cash flows — if positive, accept. ARR: Average annual profit ÷ average investment. IRR: Discount rate where NPV = 0.

💡
Did you know? Amazon didn't turn a profit for 7 years (1997–2003) because it reinvested everything into growth. Investors accepted this because of the company's cash flow and market share trajectory.

⚙️ Unit 4 — Operations Management

▼

🏭 Methods of Production

Job: One-off, customised (bespoke furniture). Batch: Groups of identical items (bread, clothing). Flow: Continuous production (oil refining, soft drinks). Cell production: Teams responsible for complete units (Toyota).

🎯 Lean Production

JIT: Materials arrive exactly when needed — reduces waste but risky if supply chain fails. Kaizen: Continuous improvement from all employees. TQM: Quality is everyone's responsibility, not just inspectors. Kanban: Visual signals control production flow.

📐 Economies of Scale

Purchasing: Bulk-buy discounts. Technical: Indivisibility of equipment. Managerial: Specialists improve efficiency. Financial: Better interest rates. Marketing: Spreading fixed costs over more units. Risk-bearing: Diversification across products/markets.

🚗 Case Study: Toyota Production System

Toyota invented Lean Manufacturing after WWII when resources were scarce. Workers can stop the entire production line (Andon cord) if they spot defects. Result: Higher quality, less waste, lower costs — now copied worldwide.

🌱 Sustainability

Triple Bottom Line: People (social impact), Planet (environmental), Profit (financial). Circular economy: Design out waste — products are reused, repaired, remanufactured, recycled.

👤 Unit 5 — People in Organisations

▼

🏗️ Organisational Structure

Tall (hierarchical): Many layers, narrow span of control, clear promotion paths but slow communication. Flat: Few layers, wide span, faster decisions but managers overstretched. Matrix: Dual reporting (project + functional) — flexible but confusing.

🧠 Motivation Theories

Taylor: Pay by output — efficient but dehumanising. Maslow: Hierarchy from physiological → safety → social → esteem → self-actualisation. Herzberg: Hygiene factors (prevent dissatisfaction) vs Motivators (create satisfaction). Mayo: Social factors matter more than money.

Eval tip: Herzberg is most useful for managers — fix hygiene first, then add motivators

👔 Leadership Styles

Autocratic: Decisions alone — fast but demotivating. Democratic: Consults team — better decisions but slower. Laissez-faire: Hands-off — works for experts, fails for novices. Situational: Adapt style to circumstances (Tannenbaum & Schmidt continuum).

💡
Did you know? Google studied 180 teams and found psychological safety (feeling safe to take risks) was the #1 predictor of team success — more than talent, structure, or leadership style.

🌐 Unit 6 — Strategic Management (A2)

▼

🎯 Strategic Analysis Tools

SWOT: Internal Strengths/Weaknesses + External Opportunities/Threats. PESTLE: Macro environment scan. Porter's Five Forces: Threat of new entrants, bargaining power of suppliers, bargaining power of buyers, threat of substitutes, competitive rivalry.

📈 Ansoff Matrix

Market Penetration (existing product, existing market — safest). Product Development (new product, existing market). Market Development (existing product, new market). Diversification (new product, new market — riskiest).

🔄 Managing Change

Lewin's model: Unfreeze → Change → Refreeze. Kotter's 8 steps: Create urgency, form coalition, create vision, communicate, empower, generate short-term wins, consolidate, anchor in culture. Resistance comes from fear, habit, loss of status, misunderstanding.

🌍 Globalisation

Drivers: Technology, trade liberalisation, MNCs, transport improvements. Impact: Access to larger markets but increased competition. Glocalisation: Think global, act local (McDonald's adapts menus per country).

🍔 Case Study: McDonald's Glocalisation

McDonald's sells McSpicy Paneer in India (no beef), Teriyaki McBurger in Japan, and McArabia in the Middle East. Same brand, locally adapted products — the essence of glocalisation.
