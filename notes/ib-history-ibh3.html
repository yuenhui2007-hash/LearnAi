Cold War

IB DP History HL/SL — Prescribed Subject 3

Overview

The Cold War (Prescribed Subject 3 / World History Topic 12) examines the ideological, political, and military confrontation between the USA and USSR from 1947 to 1991. It covers origins, escalation, détente, and the end of the Cold War, along with proxy wars and their impact on nations.

1. Origins of the Cold War (1945–1949)

  • Ideological differences: capitalism vs. communism; democracy vs. one-party state.
  • Wartime tensions: disagreements over Poland, Germany, and the atomic bomb.
  • Long Telegram (1946): Kennan's analysis of Soviet expansionism.
  • Truman Doctrine (1947): US commitment to contain communism.
  • Marshall Plan (1948): US economic aid to rebuild Western Europe.
  • Berlin Blockade (1948–49): Soviet blockade of West Berlin; overcome by airlift.
  • NATO (1949): Western military alliance.

2. Escalation (1950–1962)

  • Korean War (1950–53): first proxy war; ended in stalemate at 38th parallel.
  • Warsaw Pact (1955): Soviet military alliance in Eastern Europe.
  • Hungarian Uprising (1956): crushed by Soviet tanks; West did not intervene.
  • U-2 Crisis (1960): US spy plane shot down over USSR.
  • Berlin Wall (1961): physical division of Berlin; symbol of Cold War division.
  • Cuban Missile Crisis (1962): closest to nuclear war; Khrushchev backed down; led to détente.

3. Détente (1963–1979)

  • Hotline (1963): direct communication between Washington and Moscow.
  • Test Ban Treaty (1963): limited nuclear testing.
  • SALT I (1972): limited strategic arms.
  • Helsinki Accords (1975): recognition of post-WWII borders; human rights provisions.
  • SALT II (1979): further arms limitations (not ratified by US Senate).

4. End of the Cold War (1980–1991)

  • Reagan's military buildup: SDI ("Star Wars") — economic pressure on USSR.
  • Gorbachev's reforms: Glasnost (openness) and Perestroika (restructuring).
  • Revolutions of 1989: fall of communist regimes in Eastern Europe.
  • Fall of Berlin Wall (1989): symbolic end of division.
  • German reunification (1990): end of post-WWII order.
  • Dissolution of USSR (1991): end of Cold War.

5. Proxy Wars & Impact on Nations

  • Korea: division at 38th parallel; ongoing tension.
  • Vietnam: US containment policy failed; 58,000 US deaths; domino theory discredited.
  • Afghanistan (1979–89): Soviet invasion; US supported mujahideen; USSR's "Vietnam."
  • Angola, Nicaragua, Congo: superpower involvement in civil conflicts.

Common Misconceptions

  • Cuban Missile Crisis: It was not a US "victory" in a traditional sense — both sides made concessions (US removed missiles from Turkey secretly).
  • Détente: It did not end the Cold War — tensions resumed in the late 1970s.
  • Gorbachev's intentions: He wanted to reform communism, not destroy it.

  • Paper 2: Essay questions on causes, practices, and effects of Cold War.

  • Analyse the reasons for the collapse of détente in the late 1970s.

Last-Minute Summary

  • Origins: ideology, wartime tensions, Truman Doctrine, Marshall Plan, Berlin Blockade, NATO.
  • Escalation: Korea, Hungary, U-2, Berlin Wall, Cuban Missile Crisis.
  • Détente: Test Ban, SALT, Helsinki — reduced tensions but did not end Cold War.
  • End: Reagan's pressure, Gorbachev's reforms, 1989 revolutions, USSR dissolution.
  • Proxy wars: Korea, Vietnam, Afghanistan — devastating for host nations.

Exam Tips

  • For Paper 1, always use OPVL when evaluating sources.
  • For Paper 2, plan your essay with a clear thesis and balanced evidence.
  • Use specific dates and names — "the late 1940s" is weaker than "1947."
  • Consider multiple perspectives (US, USSR, non-aligned nations).
  • Evaluate significance — do not just list events.
