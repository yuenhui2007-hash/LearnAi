Introduction to Economics

IB DP Economics HL/SL — Topic 1

Overview

Introduction to Economics (Topic 1) establishes the foundational concepts of scarcity, choice, and opportunity cost. It also introduces the methodological framework of economics, including the distinction between positive and normative statements. This topic underpins all subsequent economic analysis and is tested in Paper 1 (essays) and Paper 2 (data response).

1.1 Scarcity, Choice & Opportunity Cost

Economics is the study of how individuals, firms, and societies allocate scarce resources to satisfy unlimited wants.

Key Definitions

  • Scarcity: the fundamental economic problem that resources are limited relative to unlimited wants.
  • Choice: because of scarcity, decisions must be made about how to allocate resources.
  • Opportunity cost: the value of the next best alternative foregone when a choice is made.
  • Free good: unlimited in supply with no opportunity cost (e.g., air).
  • Economic good: scarce and has an opportunity cost.

Worked Example 1: Opportunity Cost

Question: A government has $1 billion to spend. It can build a hospital (estimated benefit: 800 QALYs) or a highway (estimated benefit: 600 QALYs). What is the opportunity cost of building the hospital?

Solution:

Opportunity cost = 600 QALYs (the benefit of the next best alternative, the highway).

1.2 Factors of Production

  • Land: natural resources (e.g., oil, forests, water).
  • Labor: human effort, both physical and mental.
  • Capital: manufactured goods used to produce other goods (e.g., machinery, factories).
  • Enterprise (Entrepreneurship): the ability to combine the other factors and take risks.

Each factor earns a corresponding reward: land → rent, labor → wages, capital → interest, enterprise → profit.

1.3 Production Possibility Curve (PPC)

The PPC shows the maximum combinations of two goods an economy can produce using all resources efficiently.

  • Points on the curve: productive efficiency.
  • Points inside the curve: inefficiency or unemployment.
  • Points outside the curve: unattainable with current resources.
Opportunity cost of producing more of one good = quantity of the other good given up.
Law of increasing opportunity cost: PPC is bowed outwards because resources are not perfectly transferable.

Shifts in the PPC

  • Outward shift: economic growth (more resources, better technology, improved labour productivity).
  • Inward shift: economic decline (war, natural disaster, emigration).

1.4 Economic Systems

  • Free market economy: resources allocated by price mechanism and private ownership. Advantages: efficiency, consumer sovereignty, innovation. Disadvantages: inequality, public goods underprovided, externalities.
  • Planned (command) economy: government makes all economic decisions. Advantages: reduced inequality, provision of public goods. Disadvantages: inefficiency, lack of innovation, shortages/surpluses.
  • Mixed economy: combines market and government intervention. Most modern economies are mixed.

1.5 Positive vs. Normative Statements

  • Positive: objective, fact-based, testable (e.g., "An increase in the minimum wage reduces employment by 2%").
  • Normative: subjective, value-based, prescriptive (e.g., "The minimum wage should be increased to reduce poverty").

IB examiners expect students to distinguish between these in evaluation questions.

Common Misconceptions

  • Scarcity: Scarcity is not the same as shortage. Scarcity is permanent; shortages are temporary market conditions.
  • Opportunity cost: It is the next best alternative, not all alternatives foregone.
  • Capital: In economics, capital means physical capital (machinery), not financial capital (money).
  • PPC: Movement along the PPC shows a reallocation of resources; a shift shows a change in productive capacity.

Exam-Specific Information

  • Paper 1 (Part a): 10-mark questions often ask for definitions and diagrams (e.g., "Explain the concept of opportunity cost using a PPC diagram").
  • Paper 1 (Part b): 15-mark evaluation questions may ask whether market or planned economies are preferable.
  • Paper 2: Data response questions may require calculation of opportunity cost from production data.
  • IA: Internal Assessment commentary often involves identifying opportunity costs in real-world policy decisions.

Practice Questions

  • Explain why scarcity forces individuals and societies to make choices.
  • Draw a PPC and explain what happens to the curve if a new technology improves agricultural productivity only.
  • Distinguish between positive and normative economics, giving two examples of each.
  • Evaluate the advantages and disadvantages of a free market economic system.

Last-Minute Summary

  • Economics studies scarcity, choice, and resource allocation.
  • Opportunity cost = next best alternative foregone.
  • Factors of production: land, labour, capital, enterprise.
  • PPC shows maximum output combinations; bowed out due to increasing opportunity cost.
  • Free market vs. planned vs. mixed economies — know advantages and disadvantages.
  • Positive = testable/factual; Normative = value-based/opinion.

Exam Tips

  • Always define key terms in your opening sentence.
  • Draw accurate, labelled diagrams — they earn significant marks.
  • Use real-world examples to support theoretical explanations.
  • In evaluation questions, consider short-run vs. long-run, stakeholders, and assumptions.
  • Include "It depends on..." in 15-mark evaluations.
