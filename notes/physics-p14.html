A2 Level

Astrophysics

9702 (A-Level) · 0625 (IGCSE)

Detailed Notes

1. Astronomical Distances

Astronomical distances are so vast that special units are needed:

  • Astronomical Unit (AU): Mean Earth-Sun distance ≈ 1.50 × 10¹¹ m. Used for distances within the solar system.
  • Light-year (ly): Distance light travels in one year ≈ 9.46 × 10¹⁵ m. Used for stellar distances.
  • Parsec (pc): Distance at which 1 AU subtends an angle of 1 arcsecond ≈ 3.26 ly ≈ 3.09 × 10¹⁶ m. Used in professional astronomy.

📖 Key Definition

Parsec (pc): The distance at which a star would have a parallax angle of 1 arcsecond. 1 pc = 3.086 × 10¹⁶ m = 3.26 ly.

2. Stellar Luminosity and the Stefan-Boltzmann Law

A star's luminosity L is the total power it radiates. For a star approximated as a black body:

L = 4πr²σT⁴

where r = stellar radius, T = surface temperature (in Kelvin), and σ = Stefan-Boltzmann constant = 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ W/(m²·K⁴).

Luminosity depends strongly on temperature (T⁴) and on radius squared. A small, hot star can be more luminous than a large, cool star.

3. Wien's Displacement Law

The peak wavelength of a black body's spectrum is inversely proportional to its temperature:

λ_max T = 2.898 × 10⁻³ m·K

Hotter stars emit more strongly at shorter (bluer) wavelengths. Cooler stars peak at longer (redder) wavelengths.

📝 Worked Example — Wien's Law

Question: The Sun has a surface temperature of 5800 K. Calculate the peak wavelength of its emission.

Solution:

λ_max = 2.898 × 10⁻³ / 5800 = 5.0 × 10⁻⁷ m (500 nm, in the green-yellow visible region)

4. Doppler Effect for Light

When a light source moves relative to an observer, the observed wavelength changes:

Δλ/λ ≈ v/c (for v << c)
  • Redshift: Source moving away → observed λ increases (spectral lines shift toward red).
  • Blueshift: Source moving toward observer → observed λ decreases.
Redshift of distant galaxies provided the first observational evidence for the expansion of the universe. Edwin Hubble discovered that more distant galaxies have greater redshift.

5. Hubble's Law

The recession velocity of a galaxy is proportional to its distance from us:

v = H₀d

where H₀ ≈ 70 km/(s·Mpc) is the Hubble constant. This law implies that the universe is expanding uniformly — space itself is stretching.

📝 Worked Example — Hubble's Law

Question: A galaxy is observed to have a redshift corresponding to a recession velocity of 2.1 × 10⁴ km/s. Calculate its distance from Earth. (H₀ = 70 km/(s·Mpc))

Solution:

d = v/H₀ = 2.1 × 10⁴ / 70 = 300 Mpc

6. The Big Bang Theory

The Big Bang theory states that the universe began from an extremely hot, dense state approximately 13.8 billion years ago and has been expanding ever since. Key evidence includes:

  • Cosmic Microwave Background (CMB): Relic radiation from ~380,000 years after the Big Bang, now cooled to ~2.7 K. Discovered by Penzias and Wilson in 1965.
  • Redshift of galaxies: Hubble's observation that distant galaxies are receding.
  • Abundance of light elements: The observed ratios of hydrogen, helium, and lithium match Big Bang nucleosynthesis predictions.
The age of the universe can be estimated from the Hubble constant: t ≈ 1/H₀ ≈ 14 billion years. This is consistent with other independent measurements.

7. Stellar Evolution

Stars follow a lifecycle determined by their mass:

  • Main sequence: Star fuses hydrogen to helium in its core. The Sun has been on the main sequence for ~4.6 billion years and will remain for another ~5 billion years.
  • Red giant: Core hydrogen exhausted. Core contracts and heats; outer layers expand and cool. Helium fusion begins (triple-alpha process).
  • White dwarf (low-mass stars, M < 8M☉): Electron degeneracy pressure supports the star. No fusion. Gradually cools over billions of years.
  • Supernova (high-mass stars, M > 8M☉): Star explodes catastrophically. May leave a neutron star (supported by neutron degeneracy pressure) or a black hole (gravitational collapse).

⚠️ Common Misconception

Students often think that a red giant is hotter than the Sun because it is "giant." In fact, red giants are cooler (hence red) but much larger, making them more luminous overall. Temperature and luminosity are distinct properties.

📝 Worked Example — Luminosity Comparison

Question: Star A has radius 2R☉ and temperature 6000 K. Star B has radius 0.5R☉ and temperature 12000 K. Compare their luminosities.

Solution:

L ∝ r²T⁴

L_A/L_B = (2/0.5)² × (6000/12000)⁴ = 16 × (0.5)⁴ = 16 × 0.0625 = 1

The stars have equal luminosity! Star B is hotter but much smaller.

🎯 Exam-Style Question

Question: (a) State Hubble's law. (b) A galaxy at a distance of 150 Mpc has a recession velocity of 1.05 × 10⁴ km/s. Calculate a value for the Hubble constant. (c) Use your answer to estimate the age of the universe in years. [5 marks]

Mark Scheme

• (a) Recession velocity is proportional to distance / v = H₀d (1 mark)

• (b) H₀ = v/d = 1.05 × 10⁴/150 = 70 km/(s·Mpc) (1 mark)

• (c) t ≈ 1/H₀ = 1/(70 × 10³/(3.086 × 10²²)) = 4.41 × 10¹⁷ s (1 mark)

• t = 4.41 × 10¹⁷ / (365 × 24 × 3600) = 1.4 × 10¹⁰ years (accept 1.3–1.5 × 10¹⁰) (2 marks — 1 method, 1 answer)

🎯 Exam-Style Question

Question: A star has a radius of 7.0 × 10⁸ m and a surface temperature of 5500 K. Calculate its luminosity. (σ = 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ W/(m²·K⁴)) [2 marks]

Mark Scheme

• L = 4πr²σT⁴ = 4π × (7.0 × 10⁸)² × 5.67 × 10⁻⁸ × (5500)⁴ (1 mark)

• L = 3.2 × 10²⁶ W (accept 3.1–3.3 × 10²⁶ W) (1 mark)

Last-Minute Summary

  • 1 AU = 1.50 × 10¹¹ m; 1 ly = 9.46 × 10¹⁵ m; 1 pc = 3.26 ly = 3.09 × 10¹⁶ m
  • L = 4πr²σT⁴ (Stefan-Boltzmann law)
  • Wien: λ_max T = 2.898 × 10⁻³ m·K
  • Doppler: Δλ/λ ≈ v/c; Redshift = moving away
  • Hubble: v = H₀d; H₀ ≈ 70 km/(s·Mpc)
  • Big Bang evidence: CMB (~2.7 K), redshift, light element abundance
  • Age of universe ≈ 1/H₀ ≈ 14 billion years
  • Star life: main sequence → red giant → white dwarf (low mass) or supernova → neutron star/black hole (high mass)

Exam Tips

  • Always convert temperatures to Kelvin before using Stefan-Boltzmann or Wien's law.
  • Remember that luminosity depends on r² AND T⁴ — temperature has a much stronger effect.
  • For Doppler shift, use Δλ/λ = v/c. Redshift means v is positive (receding).
  • Hubble's law applies to galaxies, not individual stars within a galaxy.
  • The CMB is evidence for the Big Bang because it is the cooled remnant of the early hot universe.
  • When comparing stars, set up ratios to cancel common constants.
