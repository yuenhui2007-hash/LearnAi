📘 Subject Context

Coordinate Geometry of Circles & Parabolas is a core topic in IGCSE Geography. It combines theoretical knowledge with practical skills in map reading, data interpretation, and fieldwork analysis.

IGCSE Geography tests knowledge, skills, and application. Paper 1 tests physical and human geography topics; Paper 2 tests geographical skills including map work and data analysis; Paper 3 (optional) tests fieldwork and coursework.

Coordinate Geometry of Circles & Parabolas

Geography G9

📘 IGCSE Content

1.1 Distance and Midpoint

Distance between (x₁, y₁) and (x₂, y₂): d = √[(x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)²] Midpoint: ((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2)

1.2 Gradient (Slope)

m = (y₂ − y₁) / (x₂ − x₁)
  • Parallel lines: m₁ = m₂
  • Perpendicular lines: m₁ × m₂ = −1

1.3 Equation of a Straight Line

Gradient-intercept form: y = mx + c Point-gradient form: y − y₁ = m(x − x₁) General form: ax + by + c = 0

1.4 Worked Example — Line Equations

Problem: Find the equation of the line through (2, 3) perpendicular to 2x + 3y = 6.

Solution:

Gradient of given line: 2x + 3y = 6 → y = −⅔x + 2, so m = −⅔

Perpendicular gradient: m⊥ = 3/2

Equation: y − 3 = (3/2)(x − 2)

y = (3/2)x − 3 + 3 = (3/2)x

Or: 3x − 2y = 0

1.5 Circle Equation (IGCSE Extended)

Centre (a, b), radius r: (x − a)² + (y − b)² = r²

Worked Example: Find centre and radius of x² + y² − 4x + 6y − 12 = 0.

Complete squares: (x² − 4x + 4) + (y² + 6y + 9) = 12 + 4 + 9 = 25

(x − 2)² + (y + 3)² = 25

Centre: (2, −3), Radius: 5

1.6 Parabola y = ax² + bx + c

  • If a > 0: U-shaped, minimum point
  • If a < 0: ∩-shaped, maximum point
  • Vertex at x = −b/(2a)
  • y-intercept at (0, c)

📗 AS Level Content

2.1 Tangents and Normals to Curves

For curve y = f(x), the gradient at x = a is f′(a).

Tangent at (a, f(a)): y − f(a) = f′(a)(x − a) Normal at (a, f(a)): y − f(a) = −(1/f′(a))(x − a)

2.2 Parabola in Vertex Form

y = a(x − h)² + k Vertex: (h, k) Axis of symmetry: x = h

2.3 Intersection of Line and Curve

Substitute the line equation into the curve equation to get a quadratic. The discriminant determines the number of intersections:

  • b² − 4ac > 0: two intersections (secant)
  • b² − 4ac = 0: one intersection (tangent)
  • b² − 4ac < 0: no intersections

2.4 Chord of Contact and Polar

For circle x² + y² = r² and external point (x₁, y₁), the chord of contact (line joining points of contact of tangents) is:

xx₁ + yy₁ = r²

This is also called the polar of the point with respect to the circle.

2.5 Parametric Equations

Circle: x = a + r cos θ, y = b + r sin θ

Parabola y² = 4ax: x = at², y = 2at

📙 A2 Level Content

3.1 General Conic Sections

The general second-degree equation:

Ax² + Bxy + Cy² + Dx + Ey + F = 0

Discriminant Δ = B² − 4AC determines type:

  • Δ < 0: Ellipse (or circle if A = C, B = 0)
  • Δ = 0: Parabola
  • Δ > 0: Hyperbola

3.2 Focus-Directrix Properties

Parabola: PS = PM (e = 1) Ellipse: PS = e × PM (e < 1) Hyperbola: PS = e × PM (e > 1)

where S is focus, M is foot of perpendicular to directrix.

3.3 Tangent and Normal using Parametric Form

For parabola y² = 4ax with parametric point (at², 2at):

Tangent: ty = x + at² Normal: y + tx = 2at + at³

The point of intersection of tangents at t₁ and t₂ is (at₁t₂, a(t₁+t₂)).

3.4 Director Circle and Orthoptic Locus

The locus of points from which perpendicular tangents can be drawn:

  • Circle x² + y² = r²: director circle is x² + y² = 2r²
  • Parabola y² = 4ax: directrix x = −a

3.5 Confocal Conics

Conics sharing the same foci are called confocal. Ellipses and hyperbolas can form orthogonal families of confocal conics, used in physics (e.g., electrostatics, gravitational fields).

📐 Worked Example

Question: Find the interior angle of a regular decagon.

Solution:
n = 10
Interior angle = (n − 2) × 180° / n
= (10 − 2) × 180° / 10 = 8 × 18° = 144°
Answer: 144°
Confusing interior and exterior angles. Exterior angles of ANY polygon always sum to 360°.
For regular polygons: interior + exterior = 180°. Use this to check your answers.

⚡ Quick Recall

  • Interior angle sum = (n − 2) × 180°
  • Exterior angle sum = 360°
  • Regular polygon interior = (n−2)×180°/n
  • Pythagoras: a² + b² = c²

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Mixing up push factors (reasons to leave) and pull factors (reasons to move to) in migration studies.
  • Mixing up erosion and weathering — erosion involves movement; weathering is in situ breakdown.
  • Forgetting to describe the trend in a graph before explaining it — description must come first.
  • Using "LEDC" and "MEDC" without defining them or recognising that these categories are simplifications.
  • Forgetting to use map evidence when answering map-based questions — quote grid references and features.

✅ Key Points to Remember

  • Know the characteristics and challenges of urbanisation in different parts of the world.
  • Be able to read and interpret OS maps — including grid references, contours, and scale.
  • Understand sustainable development and be able to evaluate different approaches to resource management.
  • Know the causes and consequences of population change, including the Demographic Transition Model.
  • Understand the factors affecting climate and be able to explain them using the energy balance model.

Geographical Skills: g9

Geography combines knowledge of physical and human processes with practical skills in map reading, data analysis, and fieldwork. Both aspects are examined, so balanced preparation is essential.

Map Skills Checklist
  • Four-figure and six-figure grid references
  • Compass bearings and directions
  • Measuring straight-line and winding distances
  • Interpreting contour patterns and relief
  • Identifying land use and settlement patterns
  • Calculating gradients
Gradient Calculation

Formula: Gradient = Vertical Interval / Horizontal Equivalent

Example: If the vertical interval between two points is 100 m and the horizontal distance is 2 km (2000 m):

Gradient = 100 / 2000 = 1/20 = 1:20

This can also be expressed as a ratio (1:20) or percentage (5%).

In data response questions, always describe the pattern before explaining it. Quote specific figures from graphs and maps to support your analysis.

Worked Example 1: Interpreting a Climate Graph

Problem

Describe the climate shown in a graph where temperatures range from 15°C (July) to 28°C (January) and rainfall is concentrated in summer months (December–March) with a winter dry season.

Solution

This climate is tropical monsoon/savanna (Aw):

  • High temperatures year-round (15–28°C), indicating a tropical location
  • Summer wet season with concentrated rainfall
  • Winter dry season (drought conditions)
  • Located in regions like northern Australia, parts of India, or Brazilian Highlands

Vegetation would be tropical grassland (savanna) with scattered trees adapted to seasonal drought.

Worked Example 2: River Landform Analysis

Problem

Explain the formation of an ox-bow lake.

Solution
  1. Lateral erosion: On the outside of a meander bend, water flows faster and erodes the river bank.
  2. Deposition: On the inside of the bend, water flows slower and deposits sediment, forming a point bar.
  3. Meander migration: The meander becomes more pronounced and the neck narrows.
  4. Cut-through: During a flood, the river breaks through the narrow neck, creating a straighter channel.
  5. Isolation: Deposition seals off the old meander loop, forming an ox-bow lake.

Worked Example 3: Population Pyramid Analysis

Problem

A population pyramid has a wide base, steep sides, and a narrow top. Describe the population structure and suggest two implications for the country's development.

Solution

Structure: High birth rate, high death rate, short life expectancy, high dependency ratio (many young dependents). This is typical of a country in Stage 1 or early Stage 2 of the Demographic Transition Model.

Implications:

  1. Pressure on services: High demand for schools, healthcare, and childcare. The working population must support many dependents.
  2. Potential for future growth: Large youthful population means future workforce, but only if education and employment opportunities are provided.

Practice Questions

Question 1

Describe and explain three factors that influence the climate of a coastal location.

Answer: (1) Sea breezes — land heats faster than sea, creating onshore winds during the day. (2) Maritime influence — oceans moderate temperatures (cooler summers, warmer winters). (3) Prevailing winds — onshore winds bring moisture; offshore winds bring dry conditions.

Question 2

Explain why volcanic eruptions can have both positive and negative effects on people living nearby.

Answer: Negative: loss of life, destruction of property, ash clouds affecting health and aviation, lahars. Positive: fertile volcanic soils for agriculture, geothermal energy, tourism revenue, creation of new land.

Question 3

Using a map extract (not provided), describe the relief and drainage patterns of the area shown and suggest how these have influenced settlement.

Answer: Relief — describe contour patterns (steep/gentle slopes, valleys, ridges). Drainage — pattern (dendritic, trellis), direction of flow, presence of rivers/lakes. Settlement — typically on flat land near water, avoiding steep slopes and flood plains.

🎯 Examination Tips

  • For "explain" questions, give reasons — link back to geographical processes and concepts.
  • In fieldwork questions, describe your methodology clearly and explain why you chose those methods.
  • Evaluate your fieldwork — acknowledge limitations and suggest improvements.
  • For map questions, always quote specific evidence — grid references, compass directions, distances.
  • Check the command word — "describe" and "explain" require very different answers.
