📘 Subject Context

Understanding Inflation & Unemployment is central to IGCSE Economics. This topic links microeconomic and macroeconomic concepts and is frequently tested in data response and essay-style questions.

IGCSE Economics requires both knowledge and application. Paper 1 (Multiple Choice) tests definitions and concepts; Paper 2 (Structured Questions) tests analysis and evaluation. Always use real-world examples where possible.

Inflation & Unemployment

9708

Detailed Notes

1. Inflation

Sustained rise in general price level. CPI measures inflation. Causes: demand-pull, cost-push, monetary.

2. Costs of Inflation

Menu costs, shoe-leather costs, redistribution, uncertainty.

3. Unemployment

  • Frictional: between jobs.
  • Structural: skills mismatch.
  • Cyclical: recession.
  • Seasonal: seasonal industries.

4. Phillips Curve

Short-run: inverse relationship between inflation and unemployment. Long-run: vertical at natural rate.

Real-World Applications

  • Price controls: Maximum prices (rent controls) and minimum prices (minimum wage)
  • Taxes: Direct (income tax) vs indirect (VAT, excise duties)
  • Subsidies: Producer subsidies (shift supply right) vs consumer subsidies
  • Externalities: Positive (education, vaccination) vs negative (pollution, congestion)

Market Failure & Government Intervention

  • Public goods: Non-excludable and non-rival (defence, street lighting)
  • Merit goods: Under-consumed due to information failure (education, healthcare)
  • Demerit goods: Over-consumed due to information failure (cigarettes, alcohol)
  • Government responses: Legislation, taxation, subsidies, provision, regulation

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Confusing public goods with merit goods — public goods are non-excludable and non-rivalrous.
  • Confusing balance of trade deficit with a government budget deficit — they are different concepts.
  • Forgetting that price elasticity of demand is always negative — economists often ignore the sign.
  • Mixing up direct and indirect taxes — direct taxes are on income/profit; indirect taxes are on spending.
  • Confusing a movement along a demand curve with a shift of the demand curve — price changes cause movements; other factors cause shifts.

✅ Key Points to Remember

  • Be able to evaluate government policies using criteria like effectiveness, time lags, and unintended consequences.
  • Learn the advantages and disadvantages of free trade and protectionism.
  • Know the factors that shift supply and demand curves — memorise these as lists.
  • Know the causes and consequences of inflation, unemployment, and balance of payments deficits.
  • Always draw supply and demand diagrams accurately — label axes, curves, equilibrium, and shifts.

Economic Analysis: e8

Economics requires the integration of theoretical models with real-world application. Examination questions test your ability to use diagrams, apply concepts to scenarios, and evaluate policies and outcomes.

Effective Economic Writing
  1. Define key terms: Start with precise definitions to demonstrate knowledge.
  2. Use diagrams: Accurately drawn and labelled diagrams earn marks directly.
  3. Apply to context: Always relate your answer to the country, market, or scenario given.
  4. Evaluate: Consider both advantages and disadvantages before reaching a judgement.
Evaluation Framework

When evaluating economic policies or theories, consider:

  • Effectiveness: Will the policy achieve its intended goal?
  • Time lags: How long before the policy takes effect?
  • Unintended consequences: What negative side effects might occur?
  • Costs: What are the financial, social, or political costs?
  • Alternatives: Would another policy be more suitable?
Evaluation is the highest-order skill in economics. Examiners reward balanced arguments that lead to a reasoned conclusion, not just lists of pros and cons.

Worked Example 1: Calculating Price Elasticity of Demand

Problem

When the price of a good increases from $10 to $12, quantity demanded falls from 100 to 80 units. Calculate the price elasticity of demand (PED).

Solution

% change in price = (12 − 10) / 10 × 100 = 20%

% change in quantity = (80 − 100) / 100 × 100 = −20%

PED = −20% / 20% = −1 (or 1 in absolute terms)

Since |PED| = 1, demand is unit elastic.

Worked Example 2: Calculating GDP Growth

Problem

A country's GDP was $500 billion in 2022 and $525 billion in 2023. Calculate the annual GDP growth rate.

Solution

Growth rate = (525 − 500) / 500 × 100 = 25/500 × 100 = 5%

Worked Example 3: Market Equilibrium Analysis

Problem

The demand and supply equations for a market are: Qd = 100 − 2P and Qs = 20 + 3P. Find the equilibrium price and quantity.

Solution

At equilibrium: Qd = Qs

100 − 2P = 20 + 3P

80 = 5P → P = $16

Q = 100 − 2(16) = 68 units

Practice Questions

Question 1

A government imposes a specific tax of $2 per unit on a good. The original supply equation is Qs = 10 + 2P. Write the new supply equation and explain how the tax affects the equilibrium price and quantity.

Answer: New Qs = 10 + 2(P − 2) = 6 + 2P. Equilibrium price increases, equilibrium quantity decreases. Consumer and producer surplus both decrease; government gains tax revenue.

Question 2

Explain two causes of demand-pull inflation and evaluate one policy a government could use to reduce it.

Answer: Causes: increased consumer spending, expansionary fiscal/monetary policy, depreciation of currency. Policy: contractionary monetary policy (raise interest rates) — reduces borrowing and spending but may cause unemployment and reduce economic growth.

Question 3

A country has a balance of trade deficit of $50 billion and receives $30 billion in net income from abroad. Calculate the current account balance and explain two measures the government could take to reduce a current account deficit.

Answer: Current account = −50 + 30 = −$20 billion (deficit). Measures: depreciation of currency, supply-side policies, protectionist policies, contractionary fiscal policy.

🎯 Examination Tips

  • Use connective phrases like "however", "on the other hand", "therefore" to develop arguments.
  • Structure essay answers with clear paragraphs, each making one main point.
  • For evaluation questions, consider both sides before reaching a reasoned judgement.
  • In data response questions, refer to the data provided — quote figures to support your analysis.
  • Use real-world examples — e.g., "as seen in the UK in 2022 when inflation reached 11%".
