IGCSE AS A2

The Origins of World War I, 1871–1914

history-h1

📘 IGCSE Content

The Alliance System

  • Triple Alliance (1882): Germany, Austria-Hungary, Italy — Bismarck's diplomatic creation to isolate France
  • Triple Entente (1907): Britain, France, Russia — formed in response to German expansion
  • Significance: Turned regional disputes into continental conflicts; guaranteed escalation

Militarism

  • Arms race: Germany's naval expansion challenged British supremacy; Dreadnought crisis 1909
  • Schlieffen Plan: German strategy for two-front war — attack France through Belgium first
  • Conscription: All major powers had large standing armies by 1914

Imperialism

  • Moroccan Crises (1905, 1911): Germany challenged French influence; heightened tensions
  • Balkan Wars (1912–13): Weakened Ottoman Empire; Serbia emerged stronger

The July Crisis 1914

  • 28 June: Archduke Franz Ferdinand assassinated in Sarajevo by Gavrilo Princip (Black Hand)
  • 23 July: Austria-Hungary issued impossible ultimatum to Serbia
  • 28 July: Austria declared war on Serbia
  • 1 August: Germany declared war on Russia
  • 3 August: Germany declared war on France; invaded Belgium
  • 4 August: Britain declared war on Germany (guarantee to Belgium, 1839)

📗 AS Level Content

Historiographical Debates

  • Fischer Thesis (1961): Germany deliberately planned war; "War Council" December 1912 as evidence of intent
  • Counter-arguments: Clark (Sleepwalkers) — all powers share blame; crisis mismanagement not deliberate planning
  • Structural vs intentionalist: Were long-term forces or short-term decisions more important?

Source Evaluation

  • Grey's speech to Parliament (3 Aug 1914): Justification for British entry
  • Bethmann-Hollweg's "blank cheque": Evidence of German encouragement to Austria

📙 A2 Level Content

Thematic Synthesis

  • German responsibility: Weltpolitik, naval expansion, support for Austria — but other powers made choices too
  • Role of nationalism: Pan-Slavism vs Pan-Germanism; Serbian nationalism as trigger
  • Systemic failure: Alliance system + mobilisation timetables made war difficult to stop once started

Essay Frameworks

  • "Germany was solely responsible": Evaluate Fischer evidence vs structural factors
  • "Short-term causes were more important": July Crisis analysis vs long-term militarism

📜 Worked Example

Question: To what extent was Germany responsible for WWI?

Essay Plan:
FOR: Blank cheque, Schlieffen Plan, naval expansion, Fischer thesis
AGAINST: Austrian ultimatum, Russian mobilisation, alliance system, Clark "sleepwalkers"
Conclusion: Significant responsibility but not sole blame
Writing narrative instead of analysis. History essays need CAUSATION — explain WHY and SIGNIFICANCE.
Always use SPECIFIC EVIDENCE — dates, names, statistics. "On 28 June 1914" not "in 1914".

⚡ Quick Recall

  • Long-term: Militarism, Alliances, Imperialism, Nationalism
  • Short-term: Assassination of Franz Ferdinand
  • Treaty of Versailles: War guilt, reparations, territorial losses
  • Cold War: Containment, Truman Doctrine, Marshall Plan

Source Analysis Framework

  • Content: What does the source say? What information does it provide?
  • Provenance: Who wrote it? When? Why? What is their perspective?
  • Reliability: Is it trustworthy? What biases might exist?
  • Utility: How useful is it for a specific enquiry?

Essay Structure

  • Introduction: Define key terms, set out argument/line of reasoning
  • Paragraphs: Point → Evidence → Explanation → Link back to question
  • Synthesis: Compare factors, weigh importance, show interconnections
  • Conclusion: Judgment based on evidence, avoid introducing new points
Always answer the EXACT question asked. Underline key words ("To what extent", "How far", "Most important") and address them directly.
