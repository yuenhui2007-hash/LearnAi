Atomic Structure

Syllabus reference: Cambridge 9701 / 0620 — Topic 1

The Atom

An atom consists of a central nucleus containing protons and neutrons, with electrons orbiting in energy levels (shells).

  • Proton — relative charge +1, relative mass 1, located in nucleus
  • Neutron — relative charge 0, relative mass 1, located in nucleus
  • Electron — relative charge -1, relative mass 1/1840, located in shells

Key Terms

  • Atomic number (Z) — number of protons in the nucleus
  • Mass number (A) — total number of protons + neutrons
  • Isotopes — atoms of the same element with different numbers of neutrons
  • Relative atomic mass (Ar) — weighted mean mass of an atom relative to 1/12 of a carbon-12 atom
Number of neutrons = Mass number (A) − Atomic number (Z)
Number of electrons (in neutral atom) = Number of protons = Atomic number (Z)

Electronic Configuration

Electrons fill shells according to energy levels:

  • 1st shell — max 2 electrons
  • 2nd shell — max 8 electrons
  • 3rd shell — max 8 electrons (IGCSE) / 18 electrons (A-Level)

Mass Spectrometry (A-Level)

A mass spectrometer measures the mass-to-charge ratio (m/z) of ions to determine relative atomic masses and isotopic abundances.

Exam Tips

  • Always write the full electronic configuration for A-Level answers.
  • Remember that isotopes have the same chemical properties but different physical properties.
  • In mass spectra, the molecular ion peak gives the relative molecular mass.
