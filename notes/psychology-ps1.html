Research Methods

Syllabus reference: Cambridge 9990 — Paper 1 & 2

Types of Research

  • Experiment — IV manipulated, DV measured, controls extraneous variables. High internal validity.
  • Observation — behaviour recorded. Can be naturalistic (real setting) or controlled (structured setting).
  • Self-report — questionnaires and interviews. Include closed and open questions.
  • Case study — in-depth study of one person/group. Rich detail but low generalisability.
  • Correlation — examines relationship between two variables. Cannot infer causation.

Sampling Methods

  • Opportunity — use whoever is available. Quick but biased.
  • Random — every member of target population has equal chance. Unbiased but time-consuming.
  • Stratified — subgroups represented proportionally. Representative.
  • Volunteer / Self-selected — participants choose to take part. May attract certain personalities.

Experimental Designs

  • Independent measures — different participants in each condition. No order effects but participant variables.
  • Repeated measures — same participants in all conditions. Controls participant variables but order effects.
  • Matched pairs — participants matched on key variables. Balanced but hard to match perfectly.

Ethics

  • Informed consent — participants must know they are in a study
  • Right to withdraw — at any time without penalty
  • Confidentiality — data anonymised
  • Protection from harm — physical and psychological
  • Deception — only if justified and debriefed
  • Debriefing — full explanation after study

Data Analysis

  • Measures of central tendency — mean, median, mode
  • Measures of dispersion — range, standard deviation
  • Inferential statistics — chi-squared, Mann-Whitney U, Wilcoxon, Spearman's rho

Exam Tips

  • Always evaluate methodology — strengths and weaknesses.
  • Link ethical issues to specific studies where possible.
  • Know when to use each statistical test.
