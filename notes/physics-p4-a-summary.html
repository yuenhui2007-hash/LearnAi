Work, Energy & Power

9702 (A-Level)

Last-Minute Revision Summary

  • Work done W = Fs cos theta -- zero work when force is perpendicular to displacement
  • Kinetic energy Ek = 0.5mv^2; gravitational potential energy Ep = mgh; elastic Ee = 0.5kx^2
  • Principle of conservation of energy: total energy constant in a closed system
  • In real systems, account for energy dissipated by resistive forces (friction, air resistance)
  • Power P = W/t = E/t = Fv -- rate of energy transfer, measured in watts (W)
  • Efficiency = (useful output / total input) x 100%; always less than 100%
  • Work-energy theorem: work done by resultant force = change in kinetic energy
  • For variable force: work done = area under F-s graph = integral F ds
  • Maximum KE from elastic potential: 0.5kx^2 = 0.5mv^2

Key Formulas

W = Fs cos theta
Ek = 0.5mv^2
Ep = mgh
Ee = 0.5kx^2
P = W/t = Fv
Efficiency = (P_out/P_in) x 100%

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Confusing power (rate, W) with energy (total, J)
  • Forgetting to include work done against resistive forces in energy conservation problems
  • Using h as total path length instead of vertical height for gravitational PE
  • Assuming 100% efficiency in real machines without justification

Quick Exam Tips

  • Read the question carefully -- identify exactly what is asked (work, energy, power, or efficiency)
  • Show all working -- method marks are often awarded even if the final answer is wrong
  • Check units -- J for energy, W for power, N for force
  • When using conservation of energy, list all forms at start and end including work against resistive forces
  • For efficiency, always state whether you used power or energy -- both give same result
  • Remember P = Fv is useful for vehicles moving at constant velocity (power = drag force x speed)
