📋 Accounting (9706)

Complete A-Level Syllabus Notes — Summarised

📊 Quick Formula Sheet

Gross Profit = Net Sales − Cost of Sales
Net Profit = Gross Profit − Operating Expenses
Current Ratio = Current Assets / Current Liabilities
Acid Test = (CA − Inventory) / CL
ROCE = (Operating Profit / Capital Employed) × 100
Inventory Days = (Avg Inventory / COS) × 365
Receivables Days = (Receivables / Revenue) × 365
Payables Days = (Payables / Credit Purchases) × 365
Gearing = (NCL / Capital Employed) × 100
Interest Cover = Operating Profit / Interest Payable
EPS = Profit after Tax / Number of Ordinary Shares
Contribution = Selling Price − Variable Cost
Break-even = Fixed Costs / Contribution per unit
NPV = Σ [Cash Flow_t / (1 + r)^t]

Unit 1 — Financial Accounting (AS)

  • Accounting Cycle: Source documents → Books of prime entry → Ledgers → Trial balance → Financial statements
  • Double Entry: Every debit has a corresponding credit; Assets = Capital + Liabilities
  • Books of Prime Entry: Sales Day Book, Purchases Day Book, Cash Book, Journal, Petty Cash Book
  • Ledger Accounts: T-accounts; balancing (balance c/d and b/d)
  • Trial Balance: Lists all debit and credit balances; checks arithmetical accuracy only
  • Correction of Errors: Suspense account for TB imbalances; types: omission, commission, principle, original entry, reversal, compensating, duplication
  • Bank Reconciliation: Reconcile cash book with bank statement; unpresented cheques, uncredited deposits, bank charges, standing orders, dishonoured cheques
  • Control Accounts: Sales Ledger Control (reconciles debtors) and Purchases Ledger Control (reconciles creditors)
  • Capital vs Revenue: Capital expenditure = long-term assets; Revenue expenditure = day-to-day costs

Unit 2 — Financial Statements (AS)

  • Sole Trader: Income Statement + Statement of Financial Position; adjustments for accruals, prepayments, depreciation, bad debts
  • Partnership: Appropriation account (interest on capital, salaries, residual profit shared in ratio); Current accounts vs Fixed capital
  • Limited Companies: Share capital (authorised, issued, called-up, paid-up); Types of shares (ordinary, preference); Reserves (share premium, revaluation, general, retained earnings); Debentures
  • Statement of Changes in Equity (SOCE): Tracks all equity movements across the year
  • Manufacturing Accounts: Direct materials + Direct labour + Factory overheads = Total manufacturing cost; transfers to Income Statement
  • Incomplete Records: Markup vs margin; Statement of affairs method; control account approach for missing figures
  • Clubs & Societies: Receipts and payments account; Income and expenditure account; accumulated fund

Unit 3 — Analysis & Communication (AS)

  • Profitability Ratios: Gross profit margin, Net profit margin, ROCE, ROE
  • Liquidity Ratios: Current ratio (ideal 1.5–2.0), Acid test (ideal 1.0+)
  • Efficiency Ratios: Inventory turnover/days, Receivables days, Payables days
  • Gearing Ratios: Gearing %, Interest cover
  • Investor Ratios: EPS, P/E ratio, Dividend yield, Dividend cover
  • Cash Flow Statements (IAS 7): Operating, Investing, Financing activities; reconciling cash movements
  • Users of Accounts: Investors, creditors, lenders, employees, managers, government, customers, public
  • Limitations: Historical cost, not forward-looking, subject to policies, excludes non-financial factors

Unit 4 — Cost & Management Accounting (A2)

  • Cost Classification: By element (materials, labour, overheads); by behaviour (fixed, variable, semi-variable); by traceability (direct vs indirect); by function
  • Costing Methods: Job costing (individual jobs), Batch costing (batches of identical items), Process costing (by department/process), Service costing
  • Overhead Absorption: Three stages: Allocation → Apportionment → Absorption; OAR = Budgeted overheads / Budgeted activity; under/over-absorption adjustments
  • Marginal Costing: Contribution = SP − VC; Break-even = FC / Contribution; Margin of safety; C/S ratio
  • Absorption vs Marginal: Fixed overheads treated as product cost vs period cost; profit differences when inventory changes
  • Budgeting: Sales, production, materials, labour, overhead, cash budgets; flexible budgets
  • Standard Costing: Setting standards (ideal, attainable, current); Variance analysis (price, usage, rate, efficiency, expenditure, volume)
  • Investment Appraisal: Payback, ARR, NPV (discounting), IRR; annuity factors for equal cash flows
