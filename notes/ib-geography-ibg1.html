Changing Population

IB DP Geography HL/SL — Option A / Core

Overview

Changing Population explores global population trends, demographic transition, fertility and mortality patterns, migration, and the challenges posed by ageing populations. This topic is examined in Paper 1 (short answers and essays) and Paper 2 (geographic perspectives). Understanding population pyramids and the Demographic Transition Model (DTM) is essential.

1.1 Global Population Trends

  • World population reached 8 billion in 2022.
  • Growth is uneven: rapid in sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia; slowing or declining in Europe and East Asia.
  • Population momentum: even if fertility falls, population continues growing due to youthful age structure.
Natural increase rate (NIR) = crude birth rate (CBR) − crude death rate (CDR)
Doubling time ≈ 70 / growth rate (%)

1.2 Demographic Transition Model (DTM)

StageCBRCDRNIRExample
1 (High stationary)HighHighLow/zeroNo countries today
2 (Early expanding)HighFallingHighNiger, Angola
3 (Late expanding)FallingLowModerateIndia, Brazil
4 (Low stationary)LowLowLow/zeroUSA, Australia
5 (Declining)Very lowLowNegativeJapan, Germany

Limitations of DTM

  • Originally based on European experience — may not apply to all countries.
  • Does not account for migration.
  • Some countries (e.g., China) moved through stages rapidly due to policy intervention.

1.3 Fertility & Mortality

Fertility

  • Total Fertility Rate (TFR): average number of children per woman.
  • Replacement level: approximately 2.1 (accounting for infant mortality).
  • Factors affecting fertility: education (especially female), employment, urbanisation, contraception access, cultural/religious norms, government policy.

Mortality

  • Infant Mortality Rate (IMR): deaths under 1 year per 1000 live births.
  • Life expectancy: affected by healthcare, sanitation, nutrition, education, and conflict.

1.4 Migration

  • Voluntary: economic migration, education, family reunification.
  • Forced: refugees, asylum seekers, internally displaced persons (IDPs).
  • Lee's Push-Pull Model: push factors (unemployment, conflict) and pull factors (jobs, safety, education).

Impacts of Migration

  • Origin country: remittances, brain drain, reduced labour supply, demographic imbalance.
  • Destination country: labour supply, cultural diversity, strain on services, social tensions.

1.5 Ageing Populations

  • Caused by low fertility and increased life expectancy.
  • Challenges: increased dependency ratio, pension costs, healthcare demand, labour shortages.
  • Responses: pro-natalist policies, raising retirement age, immigration, automation.
Dependency ratio = (dependents / working-age population) × 100
Old-age dependency ratio = (population 65+ / population 15–64) × 100

Common Misconceptions

  • DTM universality: Not all countries follow the DTM exactly — policy and culture can accelerate or delay transitions.
  • Migration and refugees: Refugees have specific legal status under the 1951 Convention; not all migrants are refugees.
  • Population pyramids: A broad base does not always mean rapid growth — check the scale and actual numbers.

Exam-Specific Information

  • Paper 1: Short-answer questions on population pyramids, DTM stages, and migration data.
  • Paper 2: 10-mark and 15-mark essays on population policies, migration impacts, or ageing populations.
  • IA: Population change fieldwork or secondary data analysis is possible.

Practice Questions

  • Describe and explain the shape of a population pyramid for a country in Stage 2 of the DTM.
  • Explain two push factors and two pull factors affecting international migration.
  • Evaluate the effectiveness of government policies in managing an ageing population.
  • Discuss the social and economic impacts of high rates of emigration on a country's development.

Last-Minute Summary

  • DTM: 5 stages from high CBR/CDR to low/negative growth.
  • TFR replacement level ≈ 2.1; affected by education, policy, culture.
  • Migration: voluntary vs. forced; push-pull model; Lee's model.
  • Ageing populations: dependency ratio rises; challenges for pensions and healthcare.
  • Population pyramids show age-sex structure and hint at DTM stage.

Exam Tips

  • Always define key terms (e.g., TFR, NIR, dependency ratio).
  • Use specific country examples with data where possible.
  • For essay questions, include both positive and negative impacts.
  • Draw and label population pyramids accurately.
  • In evaluation, consider short-term vs. long-term and different stakeholder perspectives.
