A2 Level

Deformation of Solids

9702 (A-Level) · 0625 (IGCSE)

Last-Minute Revision Summary

  • Stress sigma = F/A (Pa); Strain epsilon = delta L/L (no units); Young modulus E = sigma/epsilon (Pa)
  • Young modulus is a material property -- independent of sample dimensions
  • Hooke's Law: F = kx (valid up to limit of proportionality)
  • Limit of proportionality: graph stops being straight (Hooke's Law fails)
  • Elastic limit: maximum stress before permanent deformation occurs
  • Elastic deformation: returns to original shape; Plastic: permanent deformation
  • Strain energy = 0.5Fx = 0.5kx^2 = area under force-extension graph
  • Energy per unit volume = 0.5 sigma epsilon = sigma^2/(2E) = 0.5E epsilon^2
  • Young modulus experiment: measure L, d, F, delta L; plot F vs delta L; E = gradient x L/A

Key Formulas

sigma = F/A
epsilon = delta L/L
E = sigma/epsilon = FL/(A delta L)
F = kx
E_strain = 0.5Fx = 0.5kx^2

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Confusing limit of proportionality with elastic limit
  • Giving strain units -- it is dimensionless
  • Using total length instead of extension in strain calculations
  • Forgetting to convert diameter to radius or area properly

Quick Exam Tips

  • Always convert mm^2 to m^2 when calculating area: 1 mm^2 = 10^-6 m^2
  • When using a micrometer, check for zero error and apply correction
  • Strain is dimensionless -- never give it units
  • On a stress-strain graph, gradient in linear region = Young modulus
  • Distinguish carefully between limit of proportionality and elastic limit
  • For strain energy, use extension (not total length)
  • When stretched beyond elastic limit, unloading curve lies below loading curve (energy dissipated)
