Organic Chemistry Fundamentals

Organic chemistry is the study of carbon compounds. Carbon's unique ability to form strong covalent bonds with itself and other elements gives rise to an enormous diversity of compounds.

📘 Key Definitions

Homologous series: A family of compounds with the same functional group, each successive member differing by CH₂.

Functional group: An atom or group of atoms responsible for the characteristic reactions of a compound.

Structural isomerism: Compounds with the same molecular formula but different structural formulae.

Stereoisomerism: Compounds with the same structural formula but different spatial arrangement of atoms.

Types of Structural Isomerism

TypeDescriptionExample
Chain isomerismDifferent carbon skeletons (branched vs straight)Butane vs methylpropane
Position isomerismFunctional group in different positionsPropan-1-ol vs propan-2-ol
Functional group isomerismDifferent functional groupsPropanal (aldehyde) vs propanone (ketone)

Types of Stereoisomerism

TypeDescriptionExample
Cis-trans (geometric)Different arrangement around a C=C double bondcis-but-2-ene vs trans-but-2-ene
Optical isomerismNon-superimposable mirror images (enantiomers)2-hydroxypropanoic acid (lactic acid)
Optical isomerism occurs when a carbon atom (chiral centre) is bonded to four different groups. Enantiomers rotate plane-polarised light in opposite directions: dextrorotatory (+) and laevorotatory (−). A racemic mixture contains equal amounts of both enantiomers and shows no optical activity.

Alkanes — Structure and Reactions

Alkanes are saturated hydrocarbons with the general formula CₙH₂ₙ₊₂. They contain only C-C and C-H single bonds. They are relatively unreactive due to the strong, non-polar C-C and C-H bonds.

Reactions of Alkanes

Free Radical Substitution — Mechanism

Initiation: Cl₂ → 2Cl•     (homolytic fission by UV light)

Propagation:

CH₄ + Cl• → CH₃• + HCl

CH₃• + Cl₂ → CH₃Cl + Cl•

Termination:

Cl• + Cl• → Cl₂

CH₃• + Cl• → CH₃Cl

CH₃• + CH₃• → C₂H₆

Free radical substitution produces a mixture of products (mono-, di-, tri-substituted). Further substitution is difficult to control. UV light is required for initiation.
Worked Example: Combustion of Alkanes

Complete combustion: CH₄ + 2O₂ → CO₂ + 2H₂O

Incomplete combustion: 2CH₄ + 3O₂ → 2CO + 4H₂O

Incomplete combustion produces toxic CO and particulates (soot, C).

Alkenes — Structure and Reactions

Alkenes are unsaturated hydrocarbons containing at least one C=C double bond. The general formula is CₙH₂ₙ. They are more reactive than alkanes due to the electron-rich π bond.

Tests for Unsaturation

• Bromine water (orange) → decolourises with alkenes (forms colourless dibromoalkane)
• Acidified KMnO₄ (purple) → decolourises with alkenes (forms diol)

Reactions of Alkenes

Electrophilic Addition Mechanisms

📘 Key Definitions

Electrophile: An electron-deficient species that accepts an electron pair.

Carbocation: A positively charged carbon intermediate.

Markovnikov's rule: In the addition of HX to an unsymmetrical alkene, the H attaches to the carbon with more H atoms already attached.

Electrophilic Addition of HBr to Propene — Mechanism

Step 1: The π bond attacks H⁺ (electrophile)

CH₃-CH=CH₂ + H⁺ → CH₃-CH⁺-CH₃ (secondary carbocation — more stable)

Note: CH₃-CH₂-CH₂⁺ (primary) is less stable and forms in smaller amounts.

Step 2: Br⁻ attacks the carbocation

CH₃-CH⁺-CH₃ + Br⁻ → CH₃-CHBr-CH₃ (major product)

Minor product: CH₃-CH₂-CH₂Br

Carbocation Stability:

Tertiary (3°) > Secondary (2°) > Primary (1°) > Methyl

This is due to positive inductive effect (+I) of alkyl groups — they push electron density toward the positively charged carbon, stabilising it.
Electrophilic Addition of Br₂ to Ethene — Mechanism

Step 1: The π bond attacks Br₂, polarising the Br-Br bond

A cyclic bromonium ion intermediate forms.

Step 2: Br⁻ attacks from the opposite side (anti addition)

The product is 1,2-dibromoethane.

The bromonium ion intermediate explains why anti addition occurs — the Br⁻ must attack from the opposite side of the ring, leading to trans products in cyclic systems.

Polymerisation

Addition Polymerisation

Alkenes can polymerise to form long-chain polymers. The double bond opens up and monomers link together.

n CH₂=CH₂ → −[CH₂-CH₂]ₙ−     (polyethene)
n CH₂=CHCl → −[CH₂-CHCl]ₙ−     (polychloroethene / PVC)
n CH₂=CH(C₆H₅) → −[CH₂-CH(C₆H₅)]ₙ−     (polystyrene)
Addition polymers are non-biodegradable because their strong C-C bonds resist breakdown. This causes environmental problems with plastic waste. Recycling and biodegradable alternatives are important solutions.

Disposal of Plastics

  • Recycling: Melting and remoulding (thermoplastics only)
  • Incineration: Burning for energy; produces CO₂ and potentially toxic gases (HCl from PVC)
  • Landfill: Non-biodegradable plastics persist for hundreds of years
  • Biodegradable plastics: Polylactic acid (PLA) and other polymers with ester linkages

Exam-Style Questions

📝 Exam Question 1

(a) Define homologous series. [2]

(b) Draw the structural isomers of C₄H₁₀. [2]

(c) Explain why but-1-ene shows cis-trans isomerism but but-2-ene does not. [2]

Mark Scheme:
(a) Family of organic compounds with same functional group (1), successive members differ by CH₂ (1).
(b) Butane (straight chain) and methylpropane (branched) (2).
(c) But-1-ene: each C of the double bond has two different groups / CH₂=CHCH₂CH₃ has CH₂ and H on one carbon (1). But-2-ene has CH₃ and H on each carbon of the double bond, allowing cis-trans (1). Actually but-2-ene DOES show cis-trans; but-1-ene does NOT because one carbon has two H atoms.
📝 Exam Question 2

Propene reacts with HBr.

(a) Name the major product and explain why it is the major product. [3]

(b) Draw the mechanism for the formation of the major product. [4]

Mark Scheme:
(a) 2-bromopropane (1). Markovnikov's rule / H adds to C with more H atoms (1). Forms more stable secondary carbocation (1).
(b) Curly arrow from C=C to H of HBr (1). Correct carbocation intermediate (1). Curly arrow from Br⁻ to C⁺ (1). Correct product shown (1).

📝 Quick Summary

  • Alkanes: CₙH₂ₙ₊₂, saturated, free radical substitution with halogens
  • Alkenes: CₙH₂ₙ, unsaturated, electrophilic addition reactions
  • Markovnikov's rule: H adds to C with more H atoms
  • Test for unsaturation: Br₂ water or acidified KMnO₄ decolourises
  • Free radical substitution: initiation, propagation, termination
    • • Electrophilic addition: forms carbocation intermediate; more stable carbocation favoured
  • Addition polymerisation: opens C=C bond to form long chains
  • Structural isomerism: chain, position, functional group
  • Stereoisomerism: cis-trans (geometric), optical (enantiomers)

💡 Exam Tips

  • Always draw curly arrows showing movement of electron PAIRS
  • Show the correct partial charges on polar bonds in mechanisms
  • Carbocation stability: tertiary > secondary > primary
  • Free radical mechanisms use fishhook arrows (single electrons)
  • Remember Markovnikov's rule for unsymmetrical alkenes
  • Polymer repeating units are written with bonds extending outside brackets
