Periodicity

IB DP Chemistry HL/SL — Topic 3

Overview

Periodicity (Topic 3) examines trends in the periodic table and the properties of elements in groups and periods. Understanding these patterns allows you to predict chemical behaviour and is tested across all papers. Group 1, Group 17, and transition metals are particularly important.

3.1 The Periodic Table

  • Arranged by increasing atomic number.
  • Groups: vertical columns — elements have similar chemical properties due to same number of valence electrons.
  • Periods: horizontal rows — correspond to the outermost electron shell being filled.
  • Blocks: s-block (groups 1–2), p-block (groups 13–18), d-block (transition metals), f-block (lanthanides/actinides).

3.2 Periodic Trends

Atomic Radius

  • Across a period: decreases — increasing nuclear charge pulls electrons closer.
  • Down a group: increases — additional electron shells outweigh increased nuclear charge.

Ionic Radius

  • Cations are smaller than their parent atoms (lost electron shell or increased effective nuclear charge).
  • Anions are larger than their parent atoms (increased electron–electron repulsion).

Ionisation Energy

  • Across a period: generally increases (higher nuclear charge, smaller radius).
  • Down a group: decreases (larger radius, more shielding).
  • Dips: Group 2 → Group 13 (p electron is higher in energy and slightly shielded); Group 15 → Group 16 (electron pairing repulsion in p subshell).

Electronegativity

Ability of an atom to attract bonding electrons in a covalent bond.

  • Across a period: increases (higher nuclear charge, smaller atomic radius).
  • Down a group: decreases (larger radius, more shielding).
  • Fluorine is the most electronegative element (Pauling scale = 4.0).

Electron Affinity

Energy change when an electron is added to a gaseous atom. More negative = more exothermic = more favourable.

3.3 Properties of Period 3 Elements

Oxides

  • Na₂O, MgO: basic oxides — react with acids to form salts and water.
  • Al₂O₃: amphoteric oxide — reacts with both acids and bases.
  • SiO₂, P₄O₁₀, SO₃: acidic oxides — react with bases to form salts and water.

Chlorides

  • NaCl, MgCl₂: ionic chlorides — conduct when molten/aqueous, neutral pH in solution.
  • AlCl₃: covalent network (dimer Al₂Cl₆) — reacts with water to form acidic solution.
  • SiCl₄, PCl₅: covalent molecular — hydrolyse in water to form acidic solutions and white fumes (HCl).

3.4 Group 1: Alkali Metals

  • Low density, low melting points, soft metals.
  • Reactivity increases down the group — outer electron is further from nucleus and more shielded.
  • Reaction with water: 2M + 2H₂O → 2MOH + H₂ (vigorous, exothermic).

3.5 Group 17: Halogens

  • Reactivity decreases down the group — harder to gain an electron as atomic radius increases.
  • Displacement reactions: more reactive halogen displaces less reactive halogen from its salt solution.
  • Colours: F₂ (pale yellow gas), Cl₂ (green-yellow gas), Br₂ (red-brown liquid), I₂ (grey solid / purple vapour).

3.6 Transition Metals [HL only]

  • Variable oxidation states (e.g., Fe²⁺/Fe³⁺, Cu⁺/Cu²⁺).
  • Coloured compounds due to d–d transitions.
  • Catalytic activity (e.g., Fe in Haber process, V₂O₅ in Contact process, MnO₂ in decomposition of H₂O₂).
  • Formation of complex ions with ligands (e.g., [Cu(H₂O)₆]²⁺ is blue).

HL Exam Focus

May 2024 HL Paper 2: Coloured complex ions and explanation using d-orbital splitting in octahedral fields.

Common Misconceptions

  • Atomic radius: Do not confuse atomic radius with ionic radius — trends differ for cations and anions.
  • Electronegativity: It applies to atoms in bonds, not isolated atoms.
  • Group trends: Reactivity of metals increases down a group; reactivity of non-metals decreases down a group.
  • Amphoteric: Al₂O₃ and Al(OH)₃ are amphoteric — they react with both acids and bases.

Exam-Specific Information

  • IA: Trends in reactivity, halogen displacement, and transition metal complexes are common.

Practice Questions

  • Explain why the atomic radius of chlorine is smaller than that of sodium.
  • Predict the pH of aqueous solutions of NaCl, MgCl₂, AlCl₃, and SiCl₄. Explain your reasoning.
  • Write an equation for the reaction of chlorine with sodium bromide solution and explain the trend in reactivity.
  • HL: Explain why [Fe(H₂O)₆]³⁺ is coloured while [Zn(H₂O)₆]²⁺ is colourless.

Last-Minute Summary

  • Atomic radius decreases across a period, increases down a group.
  • Ionisation energy increases across a period, decreases down a group (with dips at Groups 13 and 16).
  • Electronegativity increases across a period, decreases down a group.
  • Metal oxides are basic; non-metal oxides are acidic; Al₂O₃ is amphoteric.
  • Group 1 reactivity increases down the group; Group 17 reactivity decreases down the group.
  • HL: Transition metals have variable oxidation states, coloured compounds, and catalytic activity.

Exam Tips

  • Read each question carefully and identify the command term before answering.
  • Show all working with units — method marks are generous in IB Chemistry.
  • Give final answers to 2–3 significant figures unless specified otherwise.
  • In "explain" questions, always mention nuclear charge, atomic radius, and shielding.
  • For trend questions, use the data booklet values where available.
