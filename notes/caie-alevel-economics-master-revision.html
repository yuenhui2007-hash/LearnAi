Basic Economic Ideas

Detailed Notes

1. Scarcity, Choice, Opportunity Cost

Scarcity = limited resources vs unlimited wants. Choice = allocation decisions. Opportunity cost = next best alternative foregone.

2. Production Possibility Curve (PPC)

Shows maximum output combinations. Points on curve = efficient. Inside = inefficient. Outside = unattainable. Shift outward = economic growth.

3. Economic Systems

  • Free market: Private ownership, price mechanism, profit motive.
  • Planned: Government ownership, central planning.
  • Mixed: Both private and public sectors.

4. Positive vs Normative

Positive = objective, fact-based ("what is"). Normative = subjective, value-based ("what ought to be").

Real-World Applications

  • Price controls: Maximum prices (rent controls) and minimum prices (minimum wage)
  • Taxes: Direct (income tax) vs indirect (VAT, excise duties)
  • Subsidies: Producer subsidies (shift supply right) vs consumer subsidies
  • Externalities: Positive (education, vaccination) vs negative (pollution, congestion)

Market Failure & Government Intervention

  • Public goods: Non-excludable and non-rival (defence, street lighting)
  • Merit goods: Under-consumed due to information failure (education, healthcare)
  • Demerit goods: Over-consumed due to information failure (cigarettes, alcohol)
  • Government responses: Legislation, taxation, subsidies, provision, regulation

5. Price Elasticity of Demand (PED)

  • Formula: PED = (% change in quantity demanded) / (% change in price)
  • Elastic (PED > 1): Quantity changes more than price — luxuries, many substitutes
  • Inelastic (PED < 1): Quantity changes less than price — necessities, few substitutes
  • Unit elastic (PED = 1): % change in quantity = % change in price
  • Perfectly inelastic (PED = 0): Quantity doesn't change at all — essential medicines
  • Factors affecting PED: Number of substitutes, necessity vs luxury, proportion of income, time period

📈 Worked Example

Question: When price rises from $4 to $5, quantity demanded falls from 200 to 160 units. Calculate PED.

%ΔQ = (160−200)/200 × 100 = −20%
%ΔP = (5−4)/4 × 100 = +25%
PED = |−20/25| = 0.8 (inelastic)

6. Government Intervention — Price Controls

Last-Minute Summary

  • Scarcity → choice → opportunity cost
  • PPC: efficient on curve, inefficient inside, unattainable outside
  • Free market = private; Planned = government; Mixed = both
  • Positive = fact; Normative = opinion

Price System

Detailed Notes

1. Demand

Quantity consumers willing and able to buy at various prices. Law of demand: price↑ → quantity demanded↓ (ceteris paribus).

2. Supply

Quantity producers willing and able to sell. Law of supply: price↑ → quantity supplied↑.

3. Equilibrium

Where demand = supply. Excess demand (shortage) → price rises. Excess supply (surplus) → price falls.

4. Elasticity

PED = (%ΔQd)/(%ΔP)
PES = (%ΔQs)/(%ΔP)
YED = (%ΔQd)/(%ΔY)
XED = (%ΔQa)/(%ΔPb)
  • PED > 1: elastic; < 1: inelastic; = 1: unitary; = 0: perfectly inelastic; = ∞: perfectly elastic.

5. Consumer and Producer Surplus

Consumer surplus = willingness to pay - actual price. Producer surplus = actual price - willingness to sell.

Real-World Applications

  • Price controls: Maximum prices (rent controls) and minimum prices (minimum wage)
  • Taxes: Direct (income tax) vs indirect (VAT, excise duties)
  • Subsidies: Producer subsidies (shift supply right) vs consumer subsidies
  • Externalities: Positive (education, vaccination) vs negative (pollution, congestion)

Market Failure & Government Intervention

  • Public goods: Non-excludable and non-rival (defence, street lighting)
  • Merit goods: Under-consumed due to information failure (education, healthcare)
  • Demerit goods: Over-consumed due to information failure (cigarettes, alcohol)
  • Government responses: Legislation, taxation, subsidies, provision, regulation

Last-Minute Summary

  • Demand: price↑ qty↓; Supply: price↑ qty↑
  • Equilibrium: Qd = Qs
  • PED = %ΔQd/%ΔP; >1 elastic, <1 inelastic
  • Consumer surplus = WTP - price

Government Intervention

Detailed Notes

1. Maximum Price (Price Ceiling)

Set below equilibrium. Causes shortage (excess demand). Rationing, queues, black markets.

2. Minimum Price (Price Floor)

Set above equilibrium. Causes surplus (excess supply). Government must buy surplus.

3. Indirect Taxes

Specific (per unit) or ad valorem (% of price). Shifts supply curve up. Tax incidence depends on elasticity.

4. Subsidies

Payment to producers. Shifts supply down. Increases quantity, decreases price.

5. Buffer Stock Schemes

Buy when price low (store), sell when price high. Stabilises prices.

Real-World Applications

  • Price controls: Maximum prices (rent controls) and minimum prices (minimum wage)
  • Taxes: Direct (income tax) vs indirect (VAT, excise duties)
  • Subsidies: Producer subsidies (shift supply right) vs consumer subsidies
  • Externalities: Positive (education, vaccination) vs negative (pollution, congestion)

Market Failure & Government Intervention

  • Public goods: Non-excludable and non-rival (defence, street lighting)
  • Merit goods: Under-consumed due to information failure (education, healthcare)
  • Demerit goods: Over-consumed due to information failure (cigarettes, alcohol)
  • Government responses: Legislation, taxation, subsidies, provision, regulation

Last-Minute Summary

  • Price ceiling < equilibrium → shortage
  • Price floor > equilibrium → surplus
  • Tax: shifts supply up; Subsidy: shifts supply down
  • Buffer stocks: buy low, sell high

International Trade

Detailed Notes

1. Absolute and Comparative Advantage

Absolute = produce more with same resources. Comparative = lower opportunity cost. Specialise and trade based on comparative advantage.

2. Protectionism

  • Tariffs: taxes on imports.
  • Quotas: quantity limits.
  • Subsidies: domestic producers.
  • Embargoes: complete ban.

3. Balance of Payments

Current account + capital account + financial account = 0. Current account = trade in goods + services + income + transfers.

4. Exchange Rates

Fixed vs floating. Appreciation = currency stronger. Depreciation = currency weaker.

Real-World Applications

  • Price controls: Maximum prices (rent controls) and minimum prices (minimum wage)
  • Taxes: Direct (income tax) vs indirect (VAT, excise duties)
  • Subsidies: Producer subsidies (shift supply right) vs consumer subsidies
  • Externalities: Positive (education, vaccination) vs negative (pollution, congestion)

Market Failure & Government Intervention

  • Public goods: Non-excludable and non-rival (defence, street lighting)
  • Merit goods: Under-consumed due to information failure (education, healthcare)
  • Demerit goods: Over-consumed due to information failure (cigarettes, alcohol)
  • Government responses: Legislation, taxation, subsidies, provision, regulation

Last-Minute Summary

  • Comparative advantage = lower opportunity cost
  • Protectionism: tariffs, quotas, subsidies, embargoes
  • Current account = goods + services + income + transfers
  • Appreciation = stronger; Depreciation = weaker

National Income

Detailed Notes

1. Circular Flow

Income = Output = Expenditure. Leakages (S, T, M) = Injections (I, G, X).

2. GDP vs GNP

  • GDP = total output within country.
  • GNP = GDP + net property income from abroad.

3. Real vs Nominal

Real GDP adjusted for inflation. Nominal GDP at current prices.

4. Limitations

Doesn't measure informal economy, inequality, environmental costs, quality of life.

Real-World Applications

  • Price controls: Maximum prices (rent controls) and minimum prices (minimum wage)
  • Taxes: Direct (income tax) vs indirect (VAT, excise duties)

Market Failure & Government Intervention

  • Demerit goods: Over-consumed due to information failure (cigarettes, alcohol)
  • Government responses: Legislation, taxation, subsidies, provision, regulation

Last-Minute Summary

  • Income = Output = Expenditure
  • Leakages = Injections
  • Real GDP adjusted for inflation

Balance of Payments

Detailed Notes

1. Fiscal Policy

Government spending and taxation. Expansionary: spend more, tax less. Contractionary: spend less, tax more.

2. Monetary Policy

Interest rates, money supply. Expansionary: lower rates. Contractionary: raise rates.

3. Supply-Side Policies

Improve productive capacity. Education, training, privatisation, deregulation.

Real-World Applications

Economic Development

Detailed Notes

1. Inflation

Sustained rise in general price level. CPI measures inflation. Causes: demand-pull, cost-push, monetary.

2. Costs of Inflation

Menu costs, shoe-leather costs, redistribution, uncertainty.

3. Unemployment

  • Frictional: between jobs.
  • Structural: skills mismatch.
  • Cyclical: recession.
  • Seasonal: seasonal industries.

4. Phillips Curve

Short-run: inverse relationship between inflation and unemployment. Long-run: vertical at natural rate.

Real-World Applications

  • Price controls: Maximum prices (rent controls) and minimum prices (minimum wage)
  • Taxes: Direct (income tax) vs indirect (VAT, excise duties)
  • Subsidies: Producer subsidies (shift supply right) vs consumer subsidies
  • Externalities: Positive (education, vaccination) vs negative (pollution, congestion)

Market Failure & Government Intervention

  • Public goods: Non-excludable and non-rival (defence, street lighting)
  • Merit goods: Under-consumed due to information failure (education, healthcare)
  • Demerit goods: Over-consumed due to information failure (cigarettes, alcohol)
  • Government responses: Legislation, taxation, subsidies, provision, regulation

Last-Minute Summary

  • Inflation: sustained price rise; CPI measurement
  • Types: demand-pull, cost-push
  • Unemployment: frictional, structural, cyclical, seasonal
  • Phillips curve: short-run trade-off, long-run vertical

Supply-Side Policy

Detailed Notes

1. Costs

  • Fixed costs: don't vary with output.
  • Variable costs: vary with output.
  • Total cost = FC + VC.
  • Marginal cost = ΔTC/ΔQ.

2. Revenue

  • Total revenue = price × quantity.
  • Marginal revenue = ΔTR/ΔQ.

3. Market Structures

  • Perfect competition: Many firms, identical products, free entry, price taker.
  • Monopoly: One firm, barriers to entry, price maker.
  • Oligopoly: Few firms, interdependent, barriers to entry.
  • Monopolistic competition: Many firms, differentiated products.

4. Profit Maximisation

MC = MR

Real-World Applications

  • Price controls: Maximum prices (rent controls) and minimum prices (minimum wage)
  • Taxes: Direct (income tax) vs indirect (VAT, excise duties)
  • Subsidies: Producer subsidies (shift supply right) vs consumer subsidies
  • Externalities: Positive (education, vaccination) vs negative (pollution, congestion)

Market Failure & Government Intervention

  • Public goods: Non-excludable and non-rival (defence, street lighting)
  • Merit goods: Under-consumed due to information failure (education, healthcare)
  • Demerit goods: Over-consumed due to information failure (cigarettes, alcohol)
  • Government responses: Legislation, taxation, subsidies, provision, regulation

Last-Minute Summary

  • TC = FC + VC; MC = ΔTC/ΔQ
  • Perfect competition: price taker, many firms
  • Monopoly: price maker, one firm
  • Oligopoly: few firms, interdependent
  • Profit max: MC = MR
