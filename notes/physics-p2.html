Kinematics & Dynamics

9702 (A-Level) · 0625 (IGCSE)

Detailed Notes

1. Kinematics

Kinematics is the study of motion without considering the forces causing it.

  • Displacement (s) — distance moved in a specified direction (vector).
  • Velocity (v) — rate of change of displacement (vector).
  • Speed — rate of change of distance (scalar).
  • Acceleration (a) — rate of change of velocity (vector).
s = ut + ½at²
v = u + at
v² = u² + 2as
s = ½(u + v)t

These equations apply only for uniform acceleration in a straight line.

2. Graphs of Motion

  • Displacement-time graph: gradient = velocity.
  • Velocity-time graph: gradient = acceleration; area under graph = displacement.
  • Acceleration-time graph: area under graph = change in velocity.

3. Dynamics

Newton's Laws of Motion:

  • First Law: A body remains at rest or moves with constant velocity unless acted upon by a resultant force.
  • Second Law: F = ma. The resultant force is proportional to the rate of change of momentum.
  • Third Law: When body A exerts a force on body B, body B exerts an equal and opposite force on body A.
Momentum: p = mv
Impulse: FΔt = Δ(mv)
Conservation of momentum: total momentum before = total momentum after (in a closed system)

4. Projectile Motion

  • Horizontal motion: constant velocity (aₓ = 0).
  • Vertical motion: constant acceleration due to gravity (aᵧ = g ≈ 9.81 m/s²).
  • Horizontal and vertical motions are independent.

Additional Applications

  • Real-world context: Engineering applications, medical physics, environmental monitoring
  • Experimental methods: Common apparatus, measurement techniques, error analysis
  • SI units in practice: Prefix usage in scientific and everyday contexts

Common Exam Question Types

7. Projectile Motion — Full Analysis

  • Horizontal: aₓ = 0, vₓ = u cos θ (constant), sₓ = ut cos θ
  • Vertical: aᵧ = −g, vᵧ = u sin θ − gt, sᵧ = ut sin θ − ½gt²
  • Time of flight: T = 2u sin θ / g (when landing at same height)
  • Max height: H = u² sin²θ / 2g (at t = u sin θ / g)
  • Range: R = u² sin 2θ / g (max at θ = 45°)

Last-Minute Summary

  • 4 SUVAT equations for uniform acceleration: v=u+at, s=ut+½at², v²=u²+2as, s=½(u+v)t
  • Displacement-time gradient = velocity; Velocity-time gradient = acceleration, area = displacement
  • Newton's 1st: body at rest stays at rest unless resultant force acts
  • Newton's 2nd: F = ma
  • Newton's 3rd: action = reaction (equal and opposite)
  • Momentum p = mv; conserved in closed system
  • Impulse = FΔt = change in momentum
  • Projectiles: horizontal = constant velocity, vertical = constant acceleration (g)

📐 Worked Example

Question: A ball is thrown vertically upwards at 20 m/s. Calculate the maximum height reached. (g = 9.81 m/s²)

Solution:
At max height, v = 0. Using v² = u² + 2as:
0 = 20² + 2(−9.81)s
0 = 400 − 19.62s
s = 400 / 19.62 = 20.4 m
Answer: 20.4 m
Using g = +9.81 m/s² when the object is moving upwards — g always acts downwards, so use −9.81 m/s² for upward motion.
Always draw a diagram and define your positive direction before solving kinematics problems.

⚡ Quick Recall

  • v = u + at (no displacement)
  • s = ut + ½at² (no final velocity)
  • v² = u² + 2as (no time)
  • s = ½(u+v)t (no acceleration)
  • All apply only for constant acceleration
