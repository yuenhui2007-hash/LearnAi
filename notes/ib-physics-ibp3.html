Thermal Physics

IB DP Physics HL/SL — Topic 3

Overview

Thermal Physics (Topic 3) bridges macroscopic thermodynamics and microscopic kinetic theory. It is examined across all papers and connects to energy production (Topic 8). Understanding the first law of thermodynamics and the behaviour of ideal gases is essential for both SL and HL students.

3.1 Temperature & Internal Energy

Temperature is a measure of the average random kinetic energy of the particles in a substance. It is measured in kelvin (K).

T(K) = T(°C) + 273.15

Internal energy (U) is the total potential and random kinetic energy of the molecules of a substance. For an ideal gas, internal energy depends only on temperature.

Key Distinction

Temperature is a macroscopic property related to average kinetic energy. Internal energy includes both kinetic and potential energy of all particles.

3.2 Specific Heat Capacity & Latent Heat

Specific heat capacity (c) is the energy required to raise the temperature of 1 kg of a substance by 1 K.

Q = mcΔT
Q = energy (J), m = mass (kg), c = specific heat capacity (J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹), ΔT = temperature change (K)

Specific latent heat (L) is the energy required to change the phase of 1 kg of a substance without changing its temperature.

Q = mL
L = specific latent heat (J kg⁻¹)

Worked Example 1: Heating and Phase Change

Question: Calculate the energy needed to convert 0.50 kg of ice at −10°C to water at 20°C. (c_ice = 2.1 × 10³ J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹, c_water = 4.2 × 10³ J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹, L_fusion = 3.3 × 10⁵ J kg⁻¹)

Solution:

Heating ice: Q₁ = (0.50)(2.1 × 10³)(10) = 1.05 × 10⁴ J
Melting: Q₂ = (0.50)(3.3 × 10⁵) = 1.65 × 10⁵ J
Heating water: Q₃ = (0.50)(4.2 × 10³)(20) = 4.2 × 10⁴ J
Total Q = 2.18 × 10⁵ J ≈ 2.2 × 10⁵ J

3.3 Kinetic Theory of Gases

The kinetic model assumes gas molecules are point masses in random motion, with elastic collisions and negligible intermolecular forces except during collisions.

Pressure and Molecular Speed

p = ⅓ρ〈c²〉 = ⅓(Nm/V)〈c²〉
where 〈c²〉 = mean-square speed, ρ = density, N = number of molecules, m = molecular mass

Kinetic Energy and Temperature

½m〈c²〉 = (3/2)kT
where k = Boltzmann constant = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹

The average translational kinetic energy of a molecule is directly proportional to absolute temperature.

Root-Mean-Square Speed

c_rms = √(3kT/m) = √(3RT/M)
where R = 8.31 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹, M = molar mass (kg mol⁻¹)

3.4 Laws of Thermodynamics

First Law of Thermodynamics

ΔU = Q − W
ΔU = change in internal energy, Q = heat added to system, W = work done by system

Sign convention: Q > 0 when heat enters the system; W > 0 when the system does work on surroundings.

Second Law of Thermodynamics

Heat cannot spontaneously flow from a colder body to a hotter body. The total entropy of an isolated system never decreases.

ΔS ≥ Q/T (for reversible processes, ΔS = Q/T)

Processes

  • Isothermal: ΔT = 0, so ΔU = 0, therefore Q = W.
  • Adiabatic: Q = 0, so ΔU = −W.
  • Isochoric: W = 0, so ΔU = Q.
  • Isobaric: p = constant, W = pΔV.

3.5 Ideal Gas Equation

pV = nRT = NkT
p = pressure (Pa), V = volume (m³), n = number of moles, N = number of molecules
R = 8.31 J mol⁻¹ K⁻¹, k = 1.38 × 10⁻²³ J K⁻¹

Worked Example 2: Ideal Gas

Question: 2.0 mol of an ideal gas occupies 5.0 × 10⁻² m³ at 300 K. Calculate the pressure.

Solution:

p = nRT/V = (2.0)(8.31)(300)/(5.0 × 10⁻²) = 9.97 × 10⁴ Pa ≈ 1.0 × 10⁵ Pa

Common Misconceptions

  • Heat vs. temperature: Heat is energy transfer; temperature is a measure of average kinetic energy.
  • Internal energy of ideal gas: Depends only on temperature, not volume or pressure.
  • Latent heat: Occurs at constant temperature — students often incorrectly add a temperature change.
  • First law signs: Some textbooks use ΔU = Q + W (work done on system). IB uses ΔU = Q − W (work done by system).

Practice Questions

  • A 0.20 kg aluminium block at 100°C is placed in 0.50 kg water at 20°C. Find the final temperature. (c_al = 900 J kg⁻¹ K⁻¹)
  • Calculate the rms speed of oxygen molecules (M = 32 g mol⁻¹) at 300 K.
  • An ideal gas expands isothermally, doing 500 J of work. How much heat enters the gas?
  • Sketch a p-V diagram for an adiabatic expansion and label the axes.

Last-Minute Summary

  • Q = mcΔT for temperature changes; Q = mL for phase changes (no ΔT).
  • Average KE per molecule ∝ T (kelvin).
  • First law: ΔU = Q − W (IB convention).
  • Isothermal: ΔU = 0; Adiabatic: Q = 0; Isochoric: W = 0.
  • pV = nRT — always use absolute temperature (K) and SI units (Pa, m³).
