Capacitance & Electromagnetism

9702 (A-Level) · 0625 (IGCSE)

Last-Minute Revision Summary

  • Proton number Z = protons; Nucleon number A = protons + neutrons; N = A - Z
  • Alpha = He-4 nucleus (stopped by paper, very high ionisation); Beta = fast electron (stopped by Al, moderate); Gamma = EM (stopped by thick lead, low ionisation)
  • Alpha decay: Z decreases by 2, A decreases by 4; Beta decay: Z increases by 1, A same (n to p + e- + anti-nu)
  • Gamma: no change in Z or A -- just energy loss
  • Half-life: N = N0 (0.5)^(t/t_half); Activity A = lambda N
  • E = mc^2; 1 u = 931.5 MeV; mass defect converted to kinetic energy
  • Fission: heavy nucleus splits + neutrons + energy; chain reaction possible
  • Fusion: light nuclei combine + energy; requires ~10^7 K to overcome electrostatic repulsion

Key Formulas

N = N0 (0.5)^(t/t_half)
A = lambda N
E = mc^2
1 u = 931.5 MeV

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Forgetting to balance both Z and A in decay equations
  • Thinking beta decay is an electron from electron shells -- it is created in the nucleus
  • Confusing half-life as time for all nuclei to decay -- it is time for HALF to decay
  • Using E = mc^2 directly with atomic mass units instead of converting via 931.5 MeV/u

Quick Exam Tips

  • Always balance both Z and A in nuclear equations
  • Gamma emission does not change Z or A -- only reduces nucleus energy
  • In half-life calculations, check if t/t_half is whole number; if not, use N = N0 e^(-lambda t)
  • For mass-energy, convert u to MeV using 1 u = 931.5 MeV -- much faster than E = mc^2
  • In fission, mention neutrons released can trigger further fissions (chain reaction)
  • Fusion requires high temperature to overcome electrostatic repulsion
