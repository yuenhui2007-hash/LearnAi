IGCSE

📘 Subject Context

Differentiation is a challenging but rewarding topic in IGCSE Additional Mathematics. This subject bridges the gap between IGCSE Mathematics and A-Level, requiring strong algebraic skills and logical reasoning.

IGCSE Additional Mathematics tests pure mathematical skills across two papers. Both papers require calculators and test knowledge of functions, quadratics, indices, surds, factors, simultaneous equations, logarithms, trigonometry, and calculus. Working must be shown clearly.

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Confusing the chain rule, product rule, and quotient rule for differentiation.
  • Forgetting the restrictions on logarithms — logₐ(b) is only defined for a > 0, a ≠ 1, and b > 0.
  • Confusing the rules of indices — especially negative and fractional indices.
  • Making arithmetic errors with fractions — find common denominators carefully.
  • Using the wrong trigonometric identity — memorise all standard identities thoroughly.

✅ Key Points to Remember

  • Know the shapes and key features of standard functions: linear, quadratic, cubic, exponential, logarithmic, trigonometric.
  • Be able to apply differentiation to find gradients, tangents, normals, stationary points, and rates of change.
  • Be able to solve simultaneous equations algebraically and graphically.
  • Master algebraic manipulation — this is the foundation of all Additional Mathematics.
  • Understand the concept of a function, including domain, range, one-to-one functions, and inverse functions.

Advanced Problem Solving: maths-am12

Additional Mathematics bridges IGCSE Mathematics and A-Level. The questions are more demanding and often require combining multiple techniques in a single problem.

Key Competencies
  1. Algebraic fluency: Manipulate complex expressions with confidence.
  2. Graphical insight: Sketch and interpret graphs of various functions.
  3. Calculus foundations: Apply differentiation and integration to solve problems.
  4. Trigonometric proficiency: Solve equations and prove identities using standard results.
Strategy for Multi-Step Problems

Step 1: Analyse the given information and identify the end goal.

Step 2: Break the problem into smaller, manageable parts.

Step 3: Apply the appropriate technique to each part.

Step 4: Connect the results to reach the final answer.

Step 5: Verify your solution by checking special cases or working backwards.

Additional Mathematics questions often have elegant solutions that become apparent only after careful analysis. If your calculation becomes excessively complicated, reconsider your approach.

Worked Example 1: Solving Trigonometric Equations

Problem

Solve 2sin²x + 3cosx = 0 for 0° ≤ x ≤ 360°.

Solution

Using sin²x = 1 − cos²x:

2(1 − cos²x) + 3cosx = 0

2 − 2cos²x + 3cosx = 0

2cos²x − 3cosx − 2 = 0

Let u = cosx: 2u² − 3u − 2 = 0

(2u + 1)(u − 2) = 0

u = −½ or u = 2 (rejected as cosx ≤ 1)

cosx = −½ → x = 120°, 240°

Worked Example 2: Using the Binomial Theorem

Problem

Find the coefficient of x³ in the expansion of (2 − 3x)⁵.

Solution

General term: C(5,r) × 2^(5−r) × (−3x)^r

For x³: r = 3

Coefficient = C(5,3) × 2² × (−3)³ = 10 × 4 × (−27) = −1080

Worked Example 3: Differentiation and Stationary Points

Problem

Find the coordinates and nature of the stationary points of y = x³ − 6x² + 9x + 2.

Solution

dy/dx = 3x² − 12x + 9 = 0

x² − 4x + 3 = 0 → (x − 1)(x − 3) = 0 → x = 1 or x = 3

When x = 1: y = 1 − 6 + 9 + 2 = 6 → (1, 6)

When x = 3: y = 27 − 54 + 27 + 2 = 2 → (3, 2)

d²y/dx² = 6x − 12

At x = 1: d²y/dx² = −6 < 0 → maximum at (1, 6)

At x = 3: d²y/dx² = 6 > 0 → minimum at (3, 2)

Practice Questions

Question 1

Solve the equation log₂(x) + log₂(x − 2) = 3.

Answer: x = 4 (x = −2 is rejected)

Question 2

A curve has equation y = (2x + 1)/(x − 3). Find dy/dx and the equation of the normal at the point where x = 4.

Answer: dy/dx = −7/(x−3)²; normal: y = 7x − 26

Question 3

Find ∫(3x² + 4/x²) dx, giving your answer in the form ax³ + b/x + c.

Answer: x³ − 4/x + c

🎯 Examination Tips

  • Always show clear, logical working — method marks are awarded at multiple stages.
  • Use the quadratic formula when factorising is not obvious — it is given in the formula sheet.
  • For trigonometric equations, find all solutions within the given range — there may be more than one.
  • For graph sketching questions, identify intercepts, turning points, and asymptotes.
  • Check your algebra by substituting values back into the original equation.
