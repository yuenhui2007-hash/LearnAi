Developmental Psychology

9990 — Attachment, Cognitive Development, and Aging

IGCSE Content

Developmental psychology is not part of the IGCSE syllabus. It is introduced at AS Level under 9990.

AS Level Content

Attachment Theory (Bowlby)

  • Attachment: Strong emotional bond between infant and caregiver
  • Critical period: First 2.5 years essential for attachment formation
  • Monotropy: Infant forms one special attachment (usually to mother)
  • Internal working model: Mental template of relationships formed from early attachment; influences later relationships
  • Secure base: Attachment figure provides safety from which to explore

Ainsworth's Strange Situation (1970)

Procedure: Mother and infant in room; stranger enters; mother leaves; mother returns; stranger leaves; mother leaves; stranger returns; mother returns.

Attachment types:
- Secure (Type B): ~65%. Distressed when mother leaves, happy when she returns. Explores confidently.
- Insecure-Avoidant (Type A): ~20%. Little distress at separation; ignores mother on return. Independent but emotionally distant.
- Insecure-Resistant/Ambivalent (Type C): ~10–15%. Very distressed at separation; angry/difficult to comfort on return. Clingy but resistant.
- Disorganised (Type D): Added later. Inconsistent behaviour; no clear strategy. Often associated with abuse/neglect.

Piaget's Theory of Cognitive Development

Sensorimotor (0–2 years):
- Learn through senses and motor actions
- Object permanence develops (~8 months)

Pre-operational (2–7 years):
- Symbolic thinking, language development
- Egocentrism: difficulty seeing others' perspectives
- Animism: attributing life to inanimate objects
- Conservation: fail to understand quantity stays same despite appearance change

Concrete Operational (7–11 years):
- Logical thinking about concrete events
- Conservation understood
- Less egocentric
- Seriation and classification skills

Formal Operational (11+ years):
- Abstract reasoning, hypothetical thinking
- Systematic problem-solving
- Metacognition (thinking about thinking)

Piaget's Key Concepts

  • Schema: Mental framework for organising knowledge
  • Assimilation: Fitting new info into existing schema
  • Accommodation: Changing schema to fit new info
  • Equilibration: Balance between assimilation and accommodation drives development

A2 Level Content

Criticisms of Piaget

  • Underestimated children: Baillargeon — infants show object permanence earlier using violation-of-expectation
  • Stages too rigid: Development is more continuous than stage-like
  • Sample bias: Based on own children and Swiss children — cultural differences ignored
  • Competence vs Performance: Children may have ability but not show it in Piaget's tasks

Vygotsky's Sociocultural Theory

  • Development driven by social interaction, not just individual discovery
  • Zone of Proximal Development (ZPD): Gap between what child can do alone and with help
  • Scaffolding: Support from more knowledgeable person, gradually withdrawn
  • Language as tool for thought — internal speech guides cognition

Effects of Deprivation and Privation

  • Deprivation: Attachment formed then lost/separated → may recover if good care follows
  • Privation: No attachment formed in critical period → more severe, longer-lasting effects
  • Genie (Curtiss, 1977): Extreme privation case — limited recovery despite intervention
  • Romanian orphans (Rutter): Early adoption led to recovery; later adoption = worse outcomes

Aging and Cognitive Change

  • Fluid intelligence (problem-solving, processing speed) declines with age
  • Crystallised intelligence (knowledge, vocabulary) stable or improves
  • Working memory capacity decreases
  • Semantic memory relatively preserved; episodic memory more affected

Last-Minute Summary

  • Bowlby: attachment = critical period, monotropy, internal working model, secure base
  • Ainsworth: Secure (B), Avoidant (A), Resistant (C), Disorganised (D)
  • Piaget's stages: Sensorimotor → Pre-operational → Concrete Operational → Formal Operational
  • Piaget: schema, assimilation, accommodation, equilibration
  • Vygotsky: social interaction drives development; ZPD, scaffolding, internal speech
  • Deprivation = attachment lost; Privation = no attachment formed
  • Aging: fluid intelligence declines; crystallised intelligence stable
  • Evaluate all theories: methodology, sample bias, cultural bias, alternative explanations
