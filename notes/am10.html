Permutations & Combinations

0606

IGCSE Content

1. Factorial Notation

n! = n \times (n-1) \times (n-2) \times ... \times 2 \times 1 \quad \text{with } 0! = 1

2. Permutations (Order Matters)

^nP_r = \frac{n!}{(n-r)!}

Example: In how many ways can 3 people be arranged in a row from 5? ⁵P₃ = 5!/(5-3)! = 60

3. Combinations (Order Does Not Matter)

^nC_r = \binom{n}{r} = \frac{n!}{r!(n-r)!}

Example: In how many ways can a committee of 3 be chosen from 5? ⁵C₃ = 5!/(3!2!) = 10

4. Arrangements with Repetition

\text{Number of arrangements of } n \text{ items with repeats} = \frac{n!}{n_1! \cdot n_2! \cdot ... \cdot n_k!}

Where n₁, n₂, ... are the counts of identical items.

5. Common Problem Types

  • Committee problems: use combinations
  • Arrangement in a line: use permutations
  • Circular arrangements: (n-1)!
  • Restrictions: count total and subtract forbidden cases, or count valid cases directly

AS & A2 Content

Permutations and combinations form the basis of probability distributions (binomial, hypergeometric) and are used in hypothesis testing.

Last-Minute Summary

  • Permutation: order matters. ⁿPᵣ = n!/(n-r)!.
  • Combination: order doesn't matter. ⁿCᵣ = n!/[r!(n-r)!].
  • Repetitions: divide by factorials of identical items.
  • Circular: (n-1)!.
  • At least/at most: consider complementary counting.
  • Restrictions: place restricted items first, then fill the rest.
