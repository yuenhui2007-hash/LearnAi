Emerging Technologies & AI

Topic i10 — Future Tech, Machine Learning & Ethical AI

📘 IGCSE Content

1.1 What is Artificial Intelligence?

Artificial Intelligence (AI) is the simulation of human intelligence by machines. It involves systems that can perform tasks normally requiring human cognition: visual perception, speech recognition, decision-making, language translation.

  • Narrow/Weak AI: Designed for a specific task (e.g., chess engine, voice assistant, spam filter). Cannot generalize beyond its domain. All current AI is narrow AI.
  • General/Strong AI: Hypothetical AI with human-like general intelligence across all domains. Does not yet exist.
  • Superintelligence: AI surpassing human intelligence in virtually every field. Speculative; subject of debate among researchers (Bostrom, Kurzweil).

1.2 AI Applications

  • Virtual assistants: Siri, Alexa, Google Assistant — natural language processing, speech recognition, task automation.
  • Recommendation systems: Netflix, Spotify, Amazon — collaborative filtering, content-based filtering.
  • Computer vision: Facial recognition (unlocking phones, security), medical imaging (tumour detection), autonomous vehicles (object detection).
  • Natural Language Processing (NLP): Machine translation (Google Translate), sentiment analysis, chatbots, text summarization.
  • Game playing: Deep Blue (chess), AlphaGo (Go), AlphaStar (StarCraft II) — demonstrated superhuman performance in complex games.

1.3 Robotics & Automation

  • Industrial robots: Automated assembly lines, welding, painting. Increase precision and speed; operate in hazardous environments.
  • Service robots: Vacuum cleaners (Roomba), lawn mowers, delivery drones, warehouse robots (Amazon Kiva).
  • Medical robots: Surgical assistants (da Vinci), rehabilitation exoskeletons, robotic prosthetics.
  • Autonomous vehicles: Self-driving cars (Tesla Autopilot, Waymo) use sensors (LIDAR, cameras, radar), GPS, and AI to navigate.

1.4 Internet of Things (IoT)

Network of physical devices embedded with sensors, software, and connectivity to exchange data.

  • Smart home: Thermostats (Nest), lighting (Philips Hue), security cameras, smart locks, voice-controlled appliances.
  • Smart cities: Traffic monitoring, intelligent street lighting, waste management sensors, air quality monitors.
  • Wearables: Fitness trackers (Fitbit), smartwatches (Apple Watch), health monitors.
  • Industrial IoT (IIoT): Predictive maintenance, supply chain tracking, quality monitoring.

📗 AS Level Content

2.1 Machine Learning (ML)

A subset of AI where systems learn from data rather than being explicitly programmed.

  • Supervised learning: Model trained on labelled data (input + correct output). Learns to map inputs to outputs.
    • Classification: Predict discrete categories (spam/not spam, disease/no disease). Algorithms: Decision Trees, Random Forest, SVM, Neural Networks.
    • Regression: Predict continuous values (house price, temperature). Algorithms: Linear Regression, Polynomial Regression.
  • Unsupervised learning: Model finds patterns in unlabelled data.
    • Clustering: Group similar data points (customer segmentation). Algorithms: K-Means, Hierarchical Clustering.
    • Dimensionality reduction: Reduce features while preserving information (PCA).
  • Reinforcement learning: Agent learns by interacting with environment, receiving rewards/penalties. Used in game playing, robotics, recommendation systems.

2.2 Neural Networks & Deep Learning

  • Artificial Neural Network (ANN): Inspired by biological neurons. Layers of interconnected nodes (neurons).
    • Input layer: Receives raw data features.
    • Hidden layers: Process and transform data. More layers = "deep" learning.
    • Output layer: Produces final prediction.
  • Training process:
    1. Forward pass: input flows through network, producing output.
    2. Calculate loss: difference between prediction and actual (e.g., Mean Squared Error, Cross-Entropy).
    3. Backpropagation: calculate gradient of loss with respect to each weight.
    4. Gradient descent: adjust weights to minimize loss. Learning rate controls step size.
  • Overfitting: Model memorizes training data but fails on new data. Solutions: more training data, regularization (L1/L2), dropout, early stopping, cross-validation.
  • Underfitting: Model too simple to capture patterns. Solutions: more complex model, more features, longer training.

2.3 Natural Language Processing (NLP) Deep Dive

  • Tokenization: Splitting text into words or subwords.
  • Word embeddings: Represent words as dense vectors capturing semantic meaning (Word2Vec, GloVe). Similar words have similar vectors.
  • Transformers: Architecture enabling parallel processing and attention mechanisms. "Attention is All You Need" (Vaswani et al., 2017).
  • Large Language Models (LLMs): GPT-4, Claude, LLaMA. Trained on massive text corpora. Capabilities: generation, summarization, translation, reasoning, code generation. Limitations: hallucination, bias, lack of true understanding.

2.4 Computer Vision Techniques

  • Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs): Specialized for image data. Convolutional layers detect features (edges, textures, shapes). Pooling layers reduce dimensionality. Popular architectures: LeNet, AlexNet, VGG, ResNet, EfficientNet.
  • Object detection: YOLO (You Only Look Once), R-CNN. Identifies and locates multiple objects in images.
  • Image segmentation: Pixel-level classification. Semantic segmentation (class per pixel) vs Instance segmentation (individual object masks).
  • Generative models: GANs (Generative Adversarial Networks — generator vs discriminator), Diffusion Models (DALL-E, Stable Diffusion, Midjourney).

📙 A2 Level Content

3.1 Advanced AI Concepts

  • Federated Learning: Train models across decentralized devices without centralizing raw data. Privacy-preserving. Used by Google Gboard, Apple Siri.
  • Transfer Learning: Pre-train on large dataset, fine-tune on smaller specific dataset. Dramatically reduces training data requirements.
  • AutoML: Automated machine learning — algorithms select models, tune hyperparameters, engineer features automatically.
  • Neuromorphic Computing: Hardware mimicking brain architecture (spiking neural networks). Extremely energy-efficient for edge AI (Intel Loihi, IBM TrueNorth).
  • Quantum Machine Learning: Using quantum computers to accelerate certain ML tasks. Promising for optimization and linear algebra but still experimental.

3.2 Ethical AI & Governance

  • AI Bias: Training data reflects historical biases → discriminatory outcomes. Examples: facial recognition less accurate for darker skin; hiring algorithms penalizing women; predictive policing targeting minorities.
    • Mitigation: Diverse training data, bias auditing, fairness constraints, explainable models, diverse development teams.
  • Explainability (XAI): Understanding why AI made a decision. Critical for healthcare, finance, legal. Techniques: SHAP, LIME, attention visualization.
  • AI Governance frameworks:
    • EU AI Act (2024): Risk-based regulation — prohibited, high-risk, limited-risk, minimal-risk categories.
    • US Executive Order on AI (2023): Safety testing, standards, watermarking AI-generated content.
    • UN recommendations: Human rights-based approach to AI.
  • Autonomous weapons: Lethal autonomous weapons systems (LAWS). Campaign to Stop Killer Robots. Debate: deterrence vs loss of human control, accountability gap.

3.3 Blockchain & Decentralized Technologies

  • Blockchain: Distributed, immutable ledger. Blocks chained via cryptographic hashes. Consensus mechanisms: Proof of Work (energy-intensive, Bitcoin), Proof of Stake (energy-efficient, Ethereum 2.0).
  • Smart contracts: Self-executing code on blockchain (Ethereum). Automatically enforce agreements without intermediaries.
  • NFTs (Non-Fungible Tokens): Unique digital ownership certificates. Used for art, collectibles, gaming items. Controversial due to speculation and environmental concerns.
  • DeFi (Decentralized Finance): Financial services (lending, borrowing, trading) on blockchain without traditional banks. High risk, regulatory uncertainty.
  • Use cases beyond crypto: Supply chain transparency, voting systems, digital identity, healthcare records, intellectual property.

3.4 Biotechnology & Human Enhancement

  • Brain-Computer Interfaces (BCIs): Direct communication between brain and computer. Neuralink, Synchron. Applications: paralysis treatment, prosthetic control, eventually cognitive enhancement.
  • CRISPR & gene editing: AI accelerates genetic research and drug discovery. Ethical concerns: designer babies, genetic discrimination, unintended mutations.
  • Wearable & implantable tech: Smart contact lenses, continuous glucose monitors, implantable RFID chips. Privacy and bodily autonomy concerns.

3.5 Evaluating Emerging Technologies

When assessing new technologies, consider the STEEPLE framework:

  • Social: Impact on communities, inequality, digital divide, employment.
  • Technological: Maturity, reliability, scalability, interoperability.
  • Economic: Cost, ROI, market disruption, new business models.
  • Environmental: Energy consumption, e-waste, resource extraction, carbon footprint.
  • Political: Regulation, geopolitical competition, state surveillance.
  • Legal: Liability, intellectual property, compliance, jurisdiction.
  • Ethical: Autonomy, privacy, fairness, transparency, human dignity.
