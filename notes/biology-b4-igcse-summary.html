Cell Membranes & Transport

Last-Minute Revision · 0610 (IGCSE)

Last-Minute Revision Summary

  • Fluid mosaic: phospholipid bilayer + intrinsic/extrinsic proteins + cholesterol + glycoproteins/glycolipids
  • Phospholipids: hydrophilic heads face out, hydrophobic tails face in
  • Cholesterol: maintains membrane fluidity; reduces permeability to water
  • Simple diffusion: small non-polar molecules (O₂, CO₂) pass through bilayer down gradient
  • Facilitated diffusion: channel proteins (ions, water) or carrier proteins (glucose); down gradient; no ATP
  • Osmosis: water moves from high Ψ to low Ψ across partially permeable membrane
  • Pure water Ψ = 0; solutes lower Ψ (make more negative)
  • Animal cells: hypotonic → lysis; isotonic → normal; hypertonic → crenation
  • Plant cells: hypotonic → turgid; isotonic → flaccid; hypertonic → plasmolysis
  • Active transport: against gradient; needs ATP and carrier proteins; e.g., Na⁺/K⁺ pump
  • Endocytosis: phagocytosis (solids), pinocytosis (liquids); exocytosis: bulk export
  • SA:V ratio decreases as size increases; large organisms need specialised exchange surfaces

Quick Exam Tips

  • Use 'water potential' NOT 'water concentration' when explaining osmosis
  • Distinguish clearly: diffusion (any molecule, down gradient), osmosis (water only), facilitated diffusion (needs protein, down gradient, no ATP), active transport (needs protein, against gradient, needs ATP)
  • Casparian strip blocks apoplast pathway, forcing selective membrane uptake
  • Phagocytosis = solids; pinocytosis = liquids; both need ATP
  • For SA:V calculations, show all working and use consistent units
