Atomic, Nuclear & Particle Physics

IB DP Physics HL/SL — Topic 7

Overview

Atomic, Nuclear and Particle Physics (Topic 7) explores the structure of matter from atoms to quarks. This topic is conceptually demanding and highly tested in Paper 1 (MCQ) and Paper 2 (structured). HL students encounter additional material on the Standard Model and exchange particles. Strong understanding of conservation laws is essential.

7.1 Atomic Structure

Rutherford Scattering

  • Alpha particles fired at thin gold foil.
  • Most passed through → atom is mostly empty space.
  • Some deflected at large angles → concentrated positive charge (nucleus).

Bohr Model

  • Electrons orbit in discrete energy levels.
  • Energy is emitted/absorbed as photons when electrons transition between levels.
E = hf = hc/λ
where h = 6.63 × 10⁻³⁴ J s

Worked Example 1: Photon Energy

Question: Calculate the wavelength of a photon emitted when an electron transitions from −1.5 eV to −3.4 eV in a hydrogen atom.

Solution:

ΔE = (−1.5) − (−3.4) = 1.9 eV = 1.9 × 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ = 3.04 × 10⁻¹⁹ J
λ = hc/ΔE = (6.63 × 10⁻³⁴)(3.0 × 10⁸)/(3.04 × 10⁻¹⁹) = 6.5 × 10⁻⁷ m = 650 nm

7.2 Radioactive Decay

Types of Radiation

  • Alpha (α): helium nucleus (²⁴He), +2 charge, high ionisation, low penetration.
  • Beta (β): electron (β⁻) or positron (β⁺), moderate ionisation and penetration.
  • Gamma (γ): electromagnetic radiation, no charge, low ionisation, high penetration.

Decay Equations

α decay: ᴬZX → ᴬ⁻⁴Z⁻₂Y + ⁴₂He
β⁻ decay: ᴬZX → ᴬZ₊₁Y + ⁰₋₁e + ν̄ₑ
β⁺ decay: ᴬZX → ᴬZ₋₁Y + ⁰₊₁e + νₑ

Half-Life

N = N₀(½)ⁿ where n = number of half-lives
Activity A = λN, where λ = decay constant
t½ = ln(2)/λ ≈ 0.693/λ

Worked Example 2: Half-Life

Question: A sample has an initial activity of 800 Bq and a half-life of 5.0 days. Calculate the activity after 20 days.

Solution:

n = 20/5 = 4 half-lives
A = 800 × (½)⁴ = 800/16 = 50 Bq

7.3 Nuclear Reactions

Mass Defect and Binding Energy

Mass defect Δm = (sum of nucleon masses) − (nuclear mass)
Binding energy E = Δmc²
Binding energy per nucleon indicates nuclear stability — peaks at iron-56 (⁵⁶Fe).

Fission and Fusion

  • Fission: heavy nucleus splits into smaller nuclei, releasing energy. Used in nuclear power reactors.
  • Fusion: light nuclei combine to form a heavier nucleus, releasing energy. Powers stars; requires extremely high temperatures.

7.4 Fundamental Particles [HL only]

Quarks

  • Up (u): charge +⅔e
  • Down (d): charge −⅓e
  • Charm (c): charge +⅔e
  • Strange (s): charge −⅓e
  • Top (t): charge +⅔e
  • Bottom (b): charge −⅓e

Hadrons

  • Baryons: three quarks (e.g., proton = uud, neutron = udd).
  • Mesons: quark–antiquark pair (e.g., π⁺ = uđ).

Leptons

  • Electron (e⁻), muon (μ⁻), tau (τ⁻) and their neutrinos (νₑ, ν_μ, ν_τ).
  • Lepton number is conserved in all interactions.

Exchange Particles

  • Photon (γ): electromagnetic force.
  • W⁺, W⁻, Z⁰: weak nuclear force.
  • Gluon (g): strong nuclear force.
  • Graviton (hypothetical): gravitational force.

Conservation Laws

In all particle interactions, the following are conserved:

  • Energy and momentum
  • Charge
  • Baryon number
  • Lepton number (separately for each generation)

HL Exam Focus

May 2024 HL Paper 2: Determine quark composition from conservation of charge and baryon number in a particle interaction.

Common Misconceptions

  • Half-life: It is the time for half the nuclei to decay, not half the activity (though for large samples these are equivalent).
  • Binding energy: It is the energy required to separate a nucleus into its nucleons, not the energy stored in the nucleus.
  • β decay: The electron emitted is created at the moment of decay, not pre-existing in the nucleus.
  • Quark confinement: Individual quarks cannot be isolated — they are always found in hadrons.

Exam-Specific Information

  • Paper 2: Structured questions on binding energy, fission/fusion, and HL particle interactions.

  • IA: Half-life experiments and background radiation measurements are popular.

  • HL: A proton collides with a neutron to produce a deuteron and a pion. Write the quark equation and show that baryon number is conserved.

Last-Minute Summary

  • E = hf = hc/λ for photon energy from atomic transitions.
  • α: ⁴₂He; β⁻: ⁰₋₁e + ν̄; β⁺: ⁰₊₁e + ν.
  • Half-life: N = N₀(½)ⁿ; t½ = ln(2)/λ.
  • Binding energy = Δmc²; highest per nucleon at Fe-56.
  • Fission: heavy → light; Fusion: light → heavy.
  • HL: Proton = uud, Neutron = udd; conserve charge, baryon number, lepton number.

Exam Tips

  • Read each question carefully and identify the command term before answering.
  • Show all working for calculation questions — method marks are generous in IB Physics.
  • When asked to "calculate," always include units and correct significant figures.
  • In "explain" questions, link your answer back to the physics principle.
  • For decay equations, check conservation of nucleon number (A) and proton number (Z).
