Geometry & Trigonometry

IB DP Mathematics: Analysis & Approaches HL/SL — Topic 3

Overview

Geometry and Trigonometry (Topic 3) covers angle measurement, trigonometric functions, identities, equations, and geometric applications. HL students also study 3D geometry and vector methods. This topic is heavily tested in Paper 1 and Paper 2.

3.1 Radians & the Unit Circle

π radians = 180°
Arc length: s = rθ
Sector area: A = ½r²θ
where θ is in radians

3.2 Trigonometric Functions

y = a sin(b(x − c)) + d
a = amplitude
Period = 2π/b
c = horizontal shift (phase)
d = vertical shift

3.3 Trigonometric Identities

sin²θ + cos²θ = 1
1 + tan²θ = sec²θ
1 + cot²θ = cosec²θ

sin(A ± B) = sin A cos B ± cos A sin B
cos(A ± B) = cos A cos B ∓ sin A sin B
tan(A ± B) = (tan A ± tan B)/(1 ∓ tan A tan B)

sin 2A = 2 sin A cos A
cos 2A = cos²A − sin²A = 2cos²A − 1 = 1 − 2sin²A

3.4 Solving Trigonometric Equations

Find all solutions in a given interval using the unit circle or CAST diagram.

Worked Example 1: Trig Equation

Question: Solve 2 sin x = 1 for 0° ≤ x ≤ 360°.

Solution:

sin x = 0.5
x = 30°, 150° (sine positive in 1st and 2nd quadrants)

3.5 Sine & Cosine Rules

Sine rule: a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C
Cosine rule: a² = b² + c² − 2bc cos A
Area = ½ab sin C

3.6 3D Geometry [HL only]

  • Angles between lines and planes.
  • Distance from point to plane.
  • Vector equations of lines and planes.

HL Exam Focus

May 2024 HL Paper 2: Angle between two planes using normal vectors.

Common Misconceptions

  • Radians vs. degrees: Always check whether the question specifies degrees or radians.
  • Inverse sine: sin⁻¹ gives the principal value only — there may be a second solution in [0, 2π].
  • Period: The period of sin(bx) is 2π/b, not 2πb.
  • 3D geometry: The angle between a line and a plane is the complement of the angle between the line and the normal.

Last-Minute Summary

  • π rad = 180°; arc length = rθ; sector area = ½r²θ.
  • Amplitude = |a|; Period = 2π/b.
  • sin²θ + cos²θ = 1; double angle formulas.
  • Solve trig equations using unit circle/CAST — check all quadrants.
  • Sine rule: a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C.
  • Cosine rule: a² = b² + c² − 2bc cos A.

Exam Tips

  • Read each question carefully and identify the command term before answering.
  • Show all working — method marks are generous in IB Mathematics.
  • Give exact answers unless asked for decimal approximations.
  • Check whether angles are in degrees or radians.
  • For trig equations, find all solutions in the given interval.
