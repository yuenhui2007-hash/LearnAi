Group 2 — The Alkaline Earth Metals

Group 2 elements (Be, Mg, Ca, Sr, Ba, Ra) have two electrons in their outer shell. They are reactive metals that form +2 ions by losing both valence electrons. Their reactivity increases down the group as the outer electrons are further from the nucleus and more shielded.

📘 Key Definitions

Alkaline earth metals: The elements in Group 2 of the Periodic Table.

First ionisation energy: Energy required to remove the first electron from a gaseous atom.

Second ionisation energy: Energy required to remove the second electron from a gaseous +1 ion.

Group 2 Reactions and Trends

Reaction with Water

Reactivity increases down the group. Beryllium does not react; magnesium reacts slowly with steam; calcium, strontium, and barium react increasingly vigorously with cold water.

Mg + H₂O(g) → MgO + H₂     (steam, requires heating)
Ca + 2H₂O(l) → Ca(OH)₂ + H₂     (cold water, moderate)
Ba + 2H₂O(l) → Ba(OH)₂ + H₂     (cold water, vigorous)

Reaction with Dilute Acids

Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂
Ca + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂

Reaction with Oxygen

2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO (bright white flame)
2Ca + O₂ → 2CaO

Solubility of Group 2 Hydroxides and Sulfates

CompoundSolubility TrendExplanation

Sulfates MSO₄Solubility decreases down groupLattice energy decreases slower than hydration energy; larger cations less able to polarise sulfate ion
Worked Example: Identifying Group 2 Ions

A white solid dissolves in water to give a solution with pH 13. When sulfuric acid is added, a white precipitate forms. Identify the Group 2 ion.

Solution: High pH indicates a soluble hydroxide → likely Ca(OH)₂, Sr(OH)₂, or Ba(OH)₂. White precipitate with H₂SO₄ is the sulfate. BaSO₄ is very insoluble and used in medical imaging. SrSO₄ is also insoluble. CaSO₄ is slightly soluble.

Ba(OH)₂ is the most soluble Group 2 hydroxide. CaSO₄ is sparingly soluble (used in plaster of Paris). BaSO₄ is completely insoluble (used in barium meals for X-rays).

Group 17 — The Halogens

Group 17 elements (F, Cl, Br, I, At, Ts) have seven valence electrons. They are reactive non-metals that form −1 ions by gaining one electron. Their reactivity decreases down the group as the added electron is further from the nucleus.

Trends down Group 17: • Atomic radius increases • Electronegativity decreases • Melting/boiling points increase (stronger van der Waals forces with more electrons) • Reactivity decreases (harder to gain an electron) • Oxidising power decreases (less positive E°)

Halogen Displacement Reactions

A more reactive halogen (higher E°) will displace a less reactive halogen from its halide solution. This demonstrates the decreasing oxidising power down the group.

Cl₂ + 2NaBr → 2NaCl + Br₂     (orange solution / brown organic layer)
Cl₂ + 2NaI → 2NaCl + I₂     (brown solution / purple organic layer)
Br₂ + 2NaI → 2NaBr + I₂     (brown solution / purple organic layer)

But: Br₂ + NaCl → NO REACTION (Br less reactive than Cl)
Test for Halide Ions
  1. Add dilute nitric acid to acidify the solution (removes carbonate/hydroxide interference).
  2. Add silver nitrate solution (AgNO₃).
  3. Observe precipitate colour: Cl⁻ = white, Br⁻ = cream, I⁻ = yellow.
  4. Add dilute ammonia: AgCl dissolves; AgBr and AgI do not.
  5. Add concentrated ammonia: AgBr dissolves; AgI does not.
Worked Example: Identifying Halides

A solution gives a cream precipitate with AgNO₃ that dissolves in concentrated ammonia but not dilute ammonia. Identify the halide.

Solution: Cream precipitate = AgBr. AgBr dissolves in conc. NH₃ but not dilute NH₃ → the halide is Br⁻.

Hydrogen Halides and Halide Tests

Properties of Hydrogen Halides

PropertyHFHClHBrHI
Bond strengthVery strongStrongModerateWeak
Thermal stabilityVery stableStableLess stableDecomposes easily
Acid strengthWeak acidStrong acidStrong acidStrong acid
Reducing abilityVery weakWeakModerateStrong
HF is a weak acid because the H-F bond is very strong and does not dissociate easily. HCl, HBr, and HI are strong acids because bond strength decreases down the group, making dissociation easier.

Reaction of Halides with Concentrated Sulfuric Acid

NaCl + H₂SO₄ → NaHSO₄ + HCl     (no redox — HCl not reducing enough)

NaBr + H₂SO₄ → NaHSO₄ + HBr
2HBr + H₂SO₄ → Br₂ + SO₂ + 2H₂O     (redox — HBr reduces H₂SO₄)

NaI + H₂SO₄ → NaHSO₄ + HI
8HI + H₂SO₄ → 4I₂ + H₂S + 4H₂O     (HI is strong reducing agent)
Worked Example: Explaining Trends

Explain why HI is a stronger reducing agent than HCl.

Solution: The H-I bond is weaker than the H-Cl bond (I is larger, bond longer). The I⁻ ion is larger, so its outer electrons are further from the nucleus and more easily lost. I⁻ is more easily oxidised to I₂ than Cl⁻ is to Cl₂.

Exam-Style Questions

📝 Exam Question 1

(a) Explain why barium is more reactive than magnesium. [3]

(b) Write an equation for the reaction of calcium with water. [1]

(c) Explain why the solubility of Group 2 sulfates decreases down the group. [2]

Mark Scheme:
(a) Ba has more electron shells / larger atomic radius (1). Outer electrons are further from nucleus / more shielding (1). Less nuclear attraction for outer electrons / easier to remove electrons (1).
(b) Ca + 2H₂O → Ca(OH)₂ + H₂ (1).
(c) Lattice energy and hydration energy both decrease (1). Lattice energy decreases less than hydration energy / hydration energy decreases more (1), making ΔH(solution) less favourable.
📝 Exam Question 2

(a) Describe what you would observe when chlorine water is added to aqueous potassium bromide. [2]

(b) Write an ionic equation for the reaction. [1]

(c) Explain why this reaction occurs in terms of electrode potentials. [2]

Mark Scheme:
(a) Colourless solution turns orange / yellow-orange / brown (1). Orange-brown organic layer if extracted (1).
(b) Cl₂ + 2Br⁻ → 2Cl⁻ + Br₂ (1).
(c) E°(Cl₂/Cl⁻) = +1.36 V > E°(Br₂/Br⁻) = +1.07 V (1). Cl₂ is the stronger oxidising agent / more readily reduced / E°cell = +0.29 V > 0 (1).

📝 Quick Summary

  • Group 2 reactivity increases down group; form +2 ions
  • Reactions: with water (increasing vigour), acids (H₂ gas), oxygen (oxides)
  • Hydroxide solubility increases down group; sulfate solubility decreases
  • Group 17 reactivity decreases down group; form −1 ions
  • Halogen displacement: more reactive halogen displaces less reactive
  • AgNO₃ test: Cl⁻ = white (sol. dil. NH₃), Br⁻ = cream (sol. conc. NH₃), I⁻ = yellow (insol.)
  • Reducing power of HX increases down group; HF weak acid, rest strong

💡 Exam Tips

  • Remember the trends: reactivity up/down depends on ease of electron loss/gain
  • Always mention atomic radius, shielding, and nuclear attraction
  • For halide tests, add dilute HNO₃ first to remove interfering ions
  • When explaining solubility trends, compare lattice and hydration energies
  • Use E° values to justify displacement reactions quantitatively
