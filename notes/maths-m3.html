Straight Line Equations

The equation of a straight line can be written in several forms. Understanding each form and when to use it is essential.

Forms of a Straight Line

FormEquationBest Used When
Gradient-Intercepty = mx + cGradient and y-intercept known
Point-Gradienty − y₁ = m(x − x₁)Gradient and one point known
Two-Point Formy − y₁y₂ − y₁ = x − x₁x₂ − x₁Two points known
General Formax + by + c = 0Standard representation
Gradient m = y₂ − y₁x₂ − x₁
Worked Example

Find the equation of the line through (2, 5) and (4, 11).

m = 11 − 54 − 2 = 3

y − 5 = 3(x − 2) → y = 3x − 1

Parallel and Perpendicular Lines

  • Parallel: Same gradient (m₁ = m₂)
  • Perpendicular: Product of gradients = −1 (m₁ × m₂ = −1)
Worked Example

Find the equation of the line through (1, 2) perpendicular to y = 2x + 3.

m = 2, so perpendicular gradient = −1/2

y − 2 = −12(x − 1) → y = −12x + 52

Key Point: The gradient of a line perpendicular to y = mx + c is −1/m. Always check that m ≠ 0 first.

Midpoint & Distance

Midpoint: (x₁ + x₂2, y₁ + y₂2)
Distance: √[(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²]
Worked Example

Find the midpoint and distance between A(1, 3) and B(5, 9).

Midpoint = (1+52, 3+92) = (3, 6)

Distance = √[(5−1)² + (9−3)²] = √[16 + 36] = √52 = 2√13

Exam-Style Question

Question: The points A(−1, 2) and B(3, 8) are opposite vertices of a square. Find the coordinates of the other two vertices. [5]

Solution: Centre = (1, 5), diagonal length = √(16+36) = √52, side = √26. Gradient AB = 6/4 = 3/2. Perpendicular gradient = −2/3. Other vertices at distance √26 from centre along perpendicular direction: (4, 3) and (−2, 7)

Circle Equations

A circle is the set of all points equidistant from a fixed centre.

(x − a)² + (y − b)² = r²
Centre (a, b), radius r

General Form

x² + y² + 2gx + 2fy + c = 0 has centre (−g, −f) and radius √(g² + f² − c)

Worked Example

Find centre and radius: x² + y² − 6x + 4y − 12 = 0

Centre: (3, −2). Radius: √(9 + 4 + 12) = √25 = 5

Tangent to a Circle

A tangent is perpendicular to the radius at the point of contact.

Worked Example

Find the equation of the tangent to (x−2)² + (y−1)² = 25 at (5, 5).

Gradient of radius = 5−15−2 = 43. Tangent gradient = −34

y − 5 = −34(x − 5) → 3x + 4y = 35

Key Point: To find where a line intersects a circle, substitute the line equation into the circle equation and solve the resulting quadratic. The discriminant tells you: Δ > 0 (two points), Δ = 0 (tangent), Δ < 0 (no intersection).

Parametric & Locus Problems

A parametric equation expresses x and y in terms of a third variable (parameter).

Worked Example

Eliminate the parameter t: x = 2t, y = t² + 1

t = x/2 → y = (x/2)² + 1 = x²4 + 1

Locus

A locus is a set of points satisfying a given condition.

Worked Example

Find the locus of points equidistant from A(1, 2) and B(5, 4).

Using PA² = PB²: (x−1)² + (y−2)² = (x−5)² + (y−4)²

Expand: x²−2x+1+y²−4y+4 = x²−10x+25+y²−8y+16

Simplify: 8x + 4y = 36 or 2x + y = 9 (the perpendicular bisector of AB)

📝 Quick Summary

  • Gradient m = (y₂ − y₁)/(x₂ − x₁)
  • Parallel lines: m₁ = m₂; Perpendicular: m₁ × m₂ = −1
  • Circle: (x − a)² + (y − b)² = r²
  • Distance = √[(x₂ − x₁)² + (y₂ − y₁)²]
  • Midpoint = ((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2)

💡 Exam Tips

  • Always draw a diagram — it helps visualise the problem.
  • Remember: perpendicular gradients multiply to −1.
  • For circle problems, complete the square to find centre and radius.
  • Check your final answer by substituting a point back in.
  • When finding intersections, use the discriminant to determine the number of solutions.
