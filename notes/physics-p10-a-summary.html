Electric & Gravitational Fields

9702 (A-Level) · 0625 (IGCSE)

Last-Minute Revision Summary

  • Force on wire: F = BIL sin theta (Fleming's left-hand rule: Field, Current, Motion)
  • Force on moving charge: F = BQv sin theta
  • Magnetic flux density B = F/IL (T) -- defined from force on current-carrying conductor
  • Circular motion in B-field: r = mv/(BQ), T = 2 pi m/(BQ) -- period independent of velocity!
  • Magnetic flux Phi = BA (Wb); Flux linkage = N Phi
  • Faraday's Law: epsilon = -N(d Phi/dt) -- e.m.f. proportional to rate of change of flux linkage
  • Lenz's Law: induced current opposes the change producing it (conservation of energy)
  • Transformer: Vs/Vp = Ns/Np; ideal: Vp Ip = Vs Is
  • Transformers require a.c. -- no changing flux with d.c.

Key Formulas

F = BIL sin theta
F = BQv sin theta
r = mv/(BQ)
epsilon = -N(d Phi/dt)
Vs/Vp = Ns/Np

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using Fleming's wrong hand for motor vs generator effect
  • Thinking transformers work with d.c.
  • Forgetting Lenz's Law negative sign in Faraday's Law
  • Confusing flux (Phi = BA) with flux density (B)

Quick Exam Tips

  • Fleming's LEFT hand for force/motor effect; RIGHT hand for induction/generator effect
  • For circular motion: BQv = mv^2/r therefore r = mv/(BQ)
  • Period of circular motion in B-field is independent of velocity -- key for cyclotrons
  • In transformers, use turns ratio for voltage and inverse ratio for current (ideal)
  • When calculating induced e.m.f., use magnitude of change; add direction from Lenz's Law separately
  • Transformers require a.c. -- never say they work with d.c.
