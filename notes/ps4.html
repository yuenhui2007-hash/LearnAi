Social Psychology

9990 — Relationships, Conformity, Obedience, and Attitudes

IGCSE Content

Social psychology is not part of the IGCSE syllabus. It is introduced at AS Level under 9990.

AS Level Content

Conformity

Changing behaviour or beliefs to align with group norms.

  • Normative social influence: Conform to be liked/accepted (public compliance, private disagreement)
  • Informational social influence: Conform because we believe others are correct (genuine acceptance)

Asch (1951) — Line Judgement Study

  • Participants judged line lengths; confederates gave wrong answers
  • Findings: 75% conformed at least once; average conformity rate ~33%
  • Factors increasing conformity: group size (up to 3), unanimity, task difficulty, low self-esteem
  • Factors decreasing conformity: ally (even one dissenter reduces conformity dramatically)

Obedience

Following orders from an authority figure.

Milgram (1963) — Shock Experiment

  • Participants believed they were giving electric shocks to a "learner"
  • 65% gave the maximum 450V shock
  • Variations:
    - Proximity: obedience dropped when experimenter left room
    - Location: obedience dropped in less prestigious setting
    - Transfer of responsibility: higher when experimenter took blame
    - Dissenting confederate: obedience dropped sharply

Explanations for Obedience

Agentic state (Milgram):
- Person sees themselves as an agent carrying out another's wishes
- Shifts responsibility to authority figure
- Autonomous state → Agentic state when entering hierarchy

Legitimacy of authority:
- We obey those we perceive as legitimate authority
- Institutional backing increases perceived legitimacy

Gradual commitment:
- Small initial requests escalate
- Hard to back out once started (foot-in-the-door)

Buffers:
- Physical or psychological distance from consequences
- Reduced empathy, increased obedience

A2 Level Content

Situational vs Dispositional Explanations

  • Situational: Behaviour caused by environment/social pressures (Milgram, Zimbardo)
  • Dispositional: Behaviour caused by personality traits (Authoritarian Personality — Adorno)
  • The debate: Are we all capable of cruelty under the right conditions, or only certain personalities?

Resistance to Social Influence

  • Locus of control (Rotter): Internal LOC = believe they control their own fate → more resistant
  • Social support: Having an ally reduces conformity and obedience
  • Prior commitment: Publicly stating a position makes it harder to back down
  • Morality: Strong moral convictions increase resistance

Attitudes and Behaviour

  • Attitude: A learned, stable evaluation of something — affects, cognitions, and behaviours
  • Attitudes don't always predict behaviour (LaPiere, 1934 — racist attitudes but served Chinese couple)
  • Persuasion: Central route (logic, evidence) vs Peripheral route (emotion, attractiveness)

Zimbardo (1973) — Stanford Prison Experiment

  • Participants randomly assigned prisoner or guard roles
  • Study terminated early due to extreme guard brutality and prisoner distress
  • Demonstrated power of situational factors and deindividuation
  • Ethical concerns: harm, lack of fully informed consent, no right to withdraw made clear

Last-Minute Summary

  • Conformity = changing to fit group norms; Obedience = following orders
  • Normative = fit in; Informational = believe group is right
  • Asch: 75% conformed at least once; ally reduces conformity
  • Milgram: 65% to 450V; proximity, location, responsibility affect obedience
  • Explanations: Agentic state, legitimacy of authority, gradual commitment, buffers
  • Situational vs Dispositional — environment vs personality
  • Resistance: locus of control, social support, prior commitment, morality
  • Zimbardo: power of roles and situations; major ethical concerns
  • Attitudes don't always predict behaviour; central vs peripheral persuasion routes
