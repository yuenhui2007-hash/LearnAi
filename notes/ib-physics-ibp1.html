Measurements & Uncertainties

IB DP Physics HL/SL — Topic 1

Overview

Measurements and Uncertainties (Topic 1) is the foundation of IB Physics and appears in every examination paper. It introduces the SI system, error analysis, and the mathematical tools needed throughout the course. This topic is assessed across Paper 1 (MCQ), Paper 2 (structured), and Paper 3 (data analysis), and is essential for the Internal Assessment (IA). Mastery of uncertainty propagation is particularly valued in IA criterion D (analysis).

1.1 Fundamental SI Units

All measurements in physics are expressed in terms of the seven SI base units. You must know these for Paper 1 and Paper 2.

  • Length — metre (m)
  • Mass — kilogram (kg)
  • Time — second (s)
  • Electric current — ampere (A)
  • Temperature — kelvin (K)
  • Amount of substance — mole (mol)
  • Luminous intensity — candela (cd)

Command Terms

State — give a specific name, value or other brief answer without explanation or calculation.
Outline — give a brief account or summary.

Derived Units

Derived units are combinations of base units. Common derived units you must recall:

Force (N) = kg m s⁻²
Energy (J) = kg m² s⁻²
Power (W) = kg m² s⁻³
Pressure (Pa) = kg m⁻¹ s⁻²
Frequency (Hz) = s⁻¹
Electric charge (C) = A s

Metric Prefixes

You must convert between prefixes quickly. Common prefixes tested:

nano (n) = 10⁻⁹    micro (μ) = 10⁻⁶    milli (m) = 10⁻³
centi (c) = 10⁻²    kilo (k) = 10³    mega (M) = 10⁶    giga (G) = 10⁹

Worked Example 1: Prefix Conversion

Question: Convert 250 nm to metres.

Solution:

250 nm = 250 × 10⁻⁹ m = 2.50 × 10⁻⁷ m

The answer must be given to 3 significant figures to match the precision of the original value.

1.2 Uncertainties & Errors

Understanding the difference between random errors and systematic errors is critical for the IA and all papers.

  • Random errors — scatter readings about the true value; reduced by repeating measurements and averaging. These affect precision.
  • Systematic errors — shift all readings by the same amount in one direction; caused by faulty equipment or poor technique. These affect accuracy.

Absolute and Relative Uncertainty

Absolute uncertainty (Δx) — uncertainty in the same units as the measurement
Fractional uncertainty = Δx / x
Percentage uncertainty = (Δx / x) × 100%

Worked Example 2: Uncertainty Calculation

Question: A length is measured as (25.0 ± 0.5) cm. Calculate the percentage uncertainty.

Solution:

Percentage uncertainty = (0.5 / 25.0) × 100% = 2%

Uncertainty in Readings vs. Measurements

A reading uncertainty is typically ± half the smallest division on an analogue scale, or ±1 in the last significant digit on a digital display. A measurement uncertainty includes both the reading uncertainty and any calibration uncertainty of the instrument.

1.3 Significant Figures

Significant figures reflect the precision of a measurement. IB examiners penalise answers given to the wrong number of significant figures.

  • Non-zero digits are always significant.
  • Zeros between non-zero digits are significant (e.g., 2005 has 4 sf).
  • Leading zeros are not significant (e.g., 0.0045 has 2 sf).
  • Trailing zeros after a decimal point are significant (e.g., 4.500 has 4 sf).

Rule for Calculations

For multiplication/division: the result has the same number of significant figures as the least precise value.
For addition/subtraction: the result has the same number of decimal places as the value with the fewest decimal places.

1.4 Error Propagation

When combining measurements, uncertainties propagate according to strict rules. This is tested in Paper 2 and essential for IA analysis.

Addition and Subtraction

If z = x + y or z = x − y, then:
Δz = Δx + Δy (absolute uncertainties add)

Multiplication and Division

If z = xy or z = x/y, then:
Δz/z = Δx/x + Δy/y (fractional/percentage uncertainties add)

Powers

If z = xⁿ, then:
Δz/z = |n| × (Δx/x)

Worked Example 3: Propagation in Density

Question: A block has mass m = (50.0 ± 0.5) g and volume V = (20.0 ± 0.4) cm³. Calculate the density and its absolute uncertainty.

Solution:

Density ρ = m/V = 50.0/20.0 = 2.50 g cm⁻³

Percentage uncertainty in m = (0.5/50.0) × 100% = 1.0%
Percentage uncertainty in V = (0.4/20.0) × 100% = 2.0%

Total percentage uncertainty in ρ = 1.0% + 2.0% = 3.0%
Absolute uncertainty in ρ = 3.0% of 2.50 = 0.075 g cm⁻³

Final answer: ρ = (2.50 ± 0.08) g cm⁻³ (to 2 sf for uncertainty, 3 sf for value)

1.5 Graphical Analysis

IB Physics requires you to plot graphs with error bars and determine uncertainties in gradients and intercepts. This appears in Paper 3 (data analysis) and is central to the IA.

  • Draw a line of best fit that passes through as many error bars as possible.
  • Draw lines of worst fit (steepest and shallowest reasonable lines) to find gradient uncertainty.
  • Gradient uncertainty = ½ × (gradient of steepest line − gradient of shallowest line).

IA Connection

In your IA, you must include uncertainty bars on your graphs and discuss how uncertainties affect your conclusion. Criterion D (Analysis) assesses your ability to process uncertainties correctly.

Common Misconceptions

  • Random vs. systematic: Students often confuse them. Remember: random = scatter (precision), systematic = offset (accuracy).
  • Percentage uncertainty: Do not forget to multiply by 100%. A fractional uncertainty of 0.05 is 5%, not 0.05%.
  • Significant figures in intermediate steps: Never round intermediate values — only round the final answer.
  • Error bars: Error bars represent uncertainties, not ranges of data points.

Exam-Specific Information

  • Paper 1: Typically 2–4 MCQs on unit conversions, prefix recognition, and significant figures.
  • Paper 2: Structured questions on uncertainty propagation (especially with density, speed, or acceleration calculations).
  • Paper 3: Data analysis question requiring graph plotting with error bars and gradient uncertainty.
  • IA: Uncertainty analysis is assessed in Criterion D.

Practice Questions

  • Calculate the percentage uncertainty in g = 9.81 m s⁻² if measured as (9.81 ± 0.05) m s⁻².
  • A rectangle has sides (5.0 ± 0.1) cm and (3.0 ± 0.1) cm. Find the area and its absolute uncertainty.
  • Explain whether zero error on a micrometer screw gauge is random or systematic.
  • Convert 450 MW to W, giving your answer in standard form to 2 significant figures.

Last-Minute Summary

  • Know all 7 SI base units and common derived units by heart.
  • Random error → precision; systematic error → accuracy.
  • For multiplication/division: add percentage uncertainties.
  • For addition/subtraction: add absolute uncertainties.
  • Always give uncertainties to 1 or 2 significant figures, and match the value's precision.
  • Include error bars and gradient uncertainty analysis in IA graphs.

Exam Tips

  • Read each question carefully and identify the command term before answering.
  • Show all working for calculation questions — method marks are generous in IB Physics.
  • When asked to "calculate," always include units and correct significant figures.
  • In "explain" questions, link your answer back to the physics principle (e.g., explain why percentage uncertainty decreases with larger measurements).
  • Use a ruler for graph questions — examiners check for properly drawn best-fit lines.
