A2 Level

Electric Fields

9702 (A-Level) · 0625 (IGCSE)

Detailed Notes

1. Electric Field Strength

An electric field is a region of space where a charged particle experiences a force. The electric field strength E at a point is defined as the force per unit positive charge:

E = F/Q (unit: N/C or V/m)
For a point charge: E = Q/(4πε₀r²)

where ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² F/m is the permittivity of free space. The field due to a point charge is radial — it points outward for a positive charge and inward for a negative charge.

📖 Key Definition

Electric Field Strength (E): The force per unit positive charge acting at a point in the field. Unit: N/C or V/m.

Electric field strength is a vector quantity. The direction is defined as the direction of the force on a positive test charge.

2. Electric Potential

Electric potential at a point is the work done per unit charge in bringing a positive test charge from infinity to that point. Potential is a scalar quantity.

V = W/Q (unit: volt, V)
For a point charge: V = Q/(4πε₀r)
Potential is always zero at infinity. For a positive point charge, V is positive everywhere and decreases as r increases. For a negative point charge, V is negative everywhere and increases (becomes less negative) as r increases.

3. Relationship between E and V

The electric field strength is related to the potential gradient:

E = -dV/dr

The negative sign indicates that E points in the direction of decreasing potential. In a uniform field (between parallel plates), this simplifies to E = V/d.

4. Uniform Electric Field

Between two parallel conducting plates separated by distance d and maintained at a potential difference V, the electric field is uniform (constant in magnitude and direction):

E = V/d

The field lines are parallel, equally spaced, and perpendicular to the plates. A positive charge in this field experiences a constant force F = QE in the direction from positive to negative plate.

📝 Worked Example — Electron in Uniform Field

Question: An electron is projected horizontally into a uniform electric field of 2.5 × 10⁴ N/C directed vertically upwards. The electron enters with speed 3.0 × 10⁶ m/s. Calculate: (a) the acceleration of the electron, (b) the vertical deflection after travelling 5.0 cm horizontally. (e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C, m = 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ kg)

Solution:

(a) F = eE = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 2.5 × 10⁴ = 4.0 × 10⁻¹⁵ N (downwards, since electron is negative)

a = F/m = 4.0 × 10⁻¹⁵ / 9.11 × 10⁻³¹ = 4.39 × 10¹⁵ m/s² downwards

(b) Time in field: t = x/v = 0.050 / 3.0 × 10⁶ = 1.67 × 10⁻⁸ s

Vertical deflection: y = ½at² = ½ × 4.39 × 10¹⁵ × (1.67 × 10⁻⁸)² = 6.1 × 10⁻⁴ m (0.61 mm)

5. Coulomb's Law

The force between two point charges is directly proportional to the product of their charges and inversely proportional to the square of their separation:

F = Q₁Q₂/(4πε₀r²)

This is an inverse-square law, analogous to Newton's law of gravitation. Like charges repel; opposite charges attract.

📝 Worked Example — Coulomb Force

Question: Two point charges, +3.0 μC and -5.0 μC, are placed 20 cm apart in vacuum. Calculate the magnitude of the force between them.

Solution:

F = (3.0 × 10⁻⁶)(5.0 × 10⁻⁶) / (4π × 8.85 × 10⁻¹² × 0.20²)

F = 1.5 × 10⁻¹¹ / (4.44 × 10⁻¹²) = 3.38 N (attractive)

6. Electric Field Patterns

  • Point positive charge: Radial field, lines radiate outward. E decreases as 1/r².
  • Point negative charge: Radial field, lines radiate inward.
  • Parallel plates: Uniform field, straight parallel lines from + to − plate.
  • Dipole: Field lines originate from + charge and terminate on − charge.
Electric field lines never cross. The density of field lines indicates the strength of the field — closer lines mean stronger field.

⚠️ Common Misconception

Students often confuse electric field strength E with electric potential V. Remember: E is force per unit charge (vector, N/C), while V is energy per unit charge (scalar, V). In a uniform field, E = V/d, but for a point charge, E ∝ 1/r² while V ∝ 1/r.

🎯 Exam-Style Question

Question: (a) Define electric field strength. (b) Two parallel plates are separated by 4.0 cm and have a potential difference of 1200 V. Calculate the electric field strength between the plates. (c) An α-particle (charge +2e, mass 6.6 × 10⁻²⁷ kg) is placed midway between the plates. Calculate the force on the α-particle and its initial acceleration. [5 marks]

Mark Scheme

• (a) Force per unit positive charge (1 mark)

• (b) E = V/d = 1200/0.040 = 3.0 × 10⁴ V/m (or N/C) (1 mark)

• (c) F = QE = 2 × 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 3.0 × 10⁴ = 9.6 × 10⁻¹⁵ N (1 mark)

• a = F/m = 9.6 × 10⁻¹⁵ / 6.6 × 10⁻²⁷ = 1.45 × 10¹² m/s² (accept 1.5 × 10¹²) (2 marks — 1 method, 1 answer)

🎯 Exam-Style Question

Question: A proton moves from a point where the potential is +200 V to a point where the potential is +800 V. Calculate the change in potential energy and state whether work is done by or on the field. (e = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ C) [3 marks]

Mark Scheme

• ΔV = 800 − 200 = 600 V (1 mark)

• ΔPE = QΔV = 1.6 × 10⁻¹⁹ × 600 = 9.6 × 10⁻¹⁷ J (1 mark)

• Work is done on the field / by an external agent (proton moves to higher potential, against the field) (1 mark)

Last-Minute Summary

  • E = F/Q; Point charge: E = Q/(4πε₀r²) (radial field)
  • V = W/Q; Point charge: V = Q/(4πε₀r)
  • E = -dV/dr; Uniform field: E = V/d
  • Coulomb: F = Q₁Q₂/(4πε₀r²) (inverse square law)
  • Point charge: radial field; Parallel plates: uniform field
  • ε₀ = 8.85 × 10⁻¹² F/m
  • Field lines: never cross, denser = stronger field

Exam Tips

  • Always state the direction of electric fields and forces — they are vectors.
  • Remember that a negative charge experiences a force opposite to the direction of E.
  • For potential calculations, V = 0 at infinity by convention.
  • In a uniform field, equipotential lines are parallel to the plates.
  • When adding fields from multiple charges, use vector addition (not scalar).
  • Check whether the question asks for magnitude only or magnitude and direction.
