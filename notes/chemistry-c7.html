Dynamic Equilibrium

📘 Key Definition

Dynamic equilibrium: In a reversible reaction, the state where the rate of the forward reaction equals the rate of the reverse reaction, so the concentrations of reactants and products remain constant (but not necessarily equal).

Characteristics of dynamic equilibrium:

  • It occurs in a closed system
  • The forward and reverse reactions continue to occur (dynamic)
  • Concentrations of reactants and products remain constant
  • It can be approached from either direction
The equilibrium position describes the relative amounts of reactants and products at equilibrium. It can lie to the left (reactants favoured) or to the right (products favoured).

The Equilibrium Constant Kc

📘 Key Definition

Equilibrium constant (Kc): The ratio of product concentrations to reactant concentrations at equilibrium, each raised to the power of their stoichiometric coefficients.

For: aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD

Kc = [C]ᶜ[D]ᵈ / [A]ᵃ[B]ᵇ

• Square brackets [ ] denote equilibrium concentration in mol dm⁻³
• Kc has units that depend on the stoichiometry
• Kc is temperature-dependent only — not affected by concentration, pressure, or catalyst
Worked Example: Writing Kc Expressions

For: N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g)

Kc = [NH₃]² / ([N₂][H₂]³)

Units: (mol dm⁻³)² / ((mol dm⁻³)(mol dm⁻³)³) = mol⁻² dm⁶

Worked Example: Calculating Kc

For: H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g)

At equilibrium: [H₂] = 0.10, [I₂] = 0.10, [HI] = 0.80 mol dm⁻³

Kc = [HI]² / ([H₂][I₂]) = (0.80)² / (0.10 × 0.10) = 0.64 / 0.01 = 64

No units (mol dm⁻³ cancels out).

If Kc > 1: products favoured (equilibrium lies to the right)
If Kc < 1: reactants favoured (equilibrium lies to the left)
If Kc ≈ 1: significant amounts of both reactants and products present

Kp for Gaseous Equilibria

📘 Key Definition

Partial pressure: The pressure a gas would exert if it alone occupied the container. p(A) = mole fraction of A × total pressure.

Equilibrium constant (Kp): The ratio of partial pressures of products to reactants at equilibrium, each raised to their stoichiometric coefficients.

For: aA(g) + bB(g) ⇌ cC(g) + dD(g)

Kp = (pC)ᶜ(pD)ᵈ / (pA)ᵃ(pB)ᵇ

Partial pressure: p(A) = n(A)/n(total) × P(total) = mole fraction × total pressure
Units of Kp depend on stoichiometry (usually atm or Pa raised to some power)
Worked Example: Calculating Kp

For: N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g)

At 1.0 atm total pressure, the equilibrium mixture contains 0.20 mol N₂O₄ and 0.80 mol NO₂.

Solution:

n(total) = 0.20 + 0.80 = 1.00 mol

Mole fraction N₂O₄ = 0.20/1.00 = 0.20; p(N₂O₄) = 0.20 × 1.0 = 0.20 atm

Mole fraction NO₂ = 0.80/1.00 = 0.80; p(NO₂) = 0.80 × 1.0 = 0.80 atm

Kp = (pNO₂)² / p(N₂O₄) = (0.80)² / 0.20 = 3.2 atm

Le Chatelier's Principle

📘 Key Definition

Le Chatelier's Principle: If a dynamic equilibrium is disturbed by changing the conditions (concentration, temperature, pressure), the position of equilibrium moves to counteract the disturbance.

Effect of Concentration

Increasing the concentration of a reactant shifts equilibrium to the right (products) to use up the added reactant.

Increasing the concentration of a product shifts equilibrium to the left (reactants).

Effect of Pressure (gaseous equilibria only)

Increasing pressure shifts equilibrium toward the side with FEWER moles of gas.

Decreasing pressure shifts equilibrium toward the side with MORE moles of gas.

Effect of Temperature

Increasing temperature shifts equilibrium in the endothermic direction (absorbs the added heat).

Decreasing temperature shifts equilibrium in the exothermic direction.

Effect of Catalyst

A catalyst speeds up both forward and reverse reactions equally. It does NOT shift the position of equilibrium or change Kc/Kp. It only helps equilibrium be reached faster.

Worked Example: Applying Le Chatelier's Principle

For: N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g)     ΔH = −92 kJ mol⁻¹

Predict the effect of:

(a) Increasing pressure: Shifts right (4 moles gas → 2 moles gas)

(b) Increasing temperature: Shifts left (reverse is endothermic)

(c) Adding a catalyst: No effect on position; rate increases

(d) Removing NH₃: Shifts right to produce more NH₃

⚠️ Common Misconceptions

• Adding a catalyst does NOT change Kc or the equilibrium position — it only speeds up attainment of equilibrium.

• Kc is constant at constant temperature. Changing concentration shifts the position but Kc remains the same.

• Pressure changes only affect gaseous equilibria where Δn(gas) ≠ 0.

• Increasing temperature always increases the rate of both forward and reverse reactions, but increases the endothermic direction more.

The Haber Process and Contact Process

Haber Process — Ammonia Synthesis

N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g)     ΔH = −92 kJ mol⁻¹

ConditionCompromise ChoiceReasoning
Temperature450°C (moderately high)Low T favours yield (exothermic) but is too slow; high T favours rate but reduces yield
Pressure200 atm (high)High pressure favours products (4 → 2 moles gas) but very high pressure is expensive/dangerous
CatalystIron with promotersIncreases rate without affecting equilibrium position
The Haber process is a compromise. Low temperature gives high yield but is too slow. High temperature is fast but gives low yield. 450°C balances reasonable yield with acceptable rate. Unreacted N₂ and H₂ are recycled.

Contact Process — Sulfuric Acid

2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g)     ΔH = −196 kJ mol⁻¹

Conditions: 450°C, 1-2 atm, V₂O₅ catalyst

Only moderate pressure is needed because the equilibrium already favours products significantly at atmospheric pressure. Higher pressure would be expensive with diminishing returns.

Exam-Style Questions

📝 Exam Question 1

For: 2SO₂(g) + O₂(g) ⇌ 2SO₃(g)     ΔH = −196 kJ mol⁻¹

(a) Write an expression for Kc. [1]

(b) State and explain the effect on the equilibrium position of increasing temperature. [2]

(c) Explain why a catalyst is used industrially despite not affecting the equilibrium position. [2]

Mark Scheme:
(a) Kc = [SO₃]² / ([SO₂]²[O₂]) (1).
(b) Equilibrium shifts left / towards reactants (1). Forward reaction is exothermic / increasing T favours endothermic direction (1).
(c) Catalyst increases rate of reaction (1). Equilibrium reached faster / greater throughput / more product per unit time (1).
📝 Exam Question 2

For: H₂(g) + CO₂(g) ⇌ H₂O(g) + CO(g)

At equilibrium in a 1.0 dm³ container: [H₂] = 0.10, [CO₂] = 0.20, [H₂O] = 0.40, [CO] = 0.40 mol dm⁻³.

(a) Calculate Kc and state its units. [2]

(b) If the volume is doubled, state the effect on Kc and the equilibrium position. [2]

Mark Scheme:
(a) Kc = (0.40 × 0.40) / (0.10 × 0.20) = 0.16 / 0.02 = 8.0 (1). No units (1).
(b) Kc unchanged (1) because Kc depends only on temperature (1). Position unchanged (1) because equal moles of gas on both sides (1). Max 2.

📝 Quick Summary

  • Dynamic equilibrium: forward rate = reverse rate; concentrations constant
  • Kc = [products] / [reactants] (raised to stoichiometric powers)
  • Kp uses partial pressures for gases
  • Kc and Kp are temperature-dependent only
  • Le Chatelier: system counteracts disturbances
  • Concentration: shifts to oppose change
  • Pressure: shifts to side with fewer gas moles
  • Temperature: shifts in endothermic direction when heated
  • Catalyst: no effect on position or K; speeds up both directions

💡 Exam Tips

  • Always write Kc expressions with concentrations, not amounts
  • Watch your units — they depend on the stoichiometry
  • When calculating equilibrium concentrations, set up an ICE table (Initial, Change, Equilibrium)
  • For gas equilibria: mole fraction = moles of gas / total moles
  • Remember: Kc doesn't change with concentration or pressure (at constant T)
  • The Haber process is the classic compromise example
