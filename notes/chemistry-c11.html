Alcohols — Classification and Reactions

Alcohols contain the hydroxyl (-OH) functional group. They are classified based on the carbon bearing the OH group.

📘 Key Definitions

Primary (1°) alcohol: OH attached to a carbon bonded to one other carbon (and two H atoms).

Secondary (2°) alcohol: OH attached to a carbon bonded to two other carbons (and one H atom).

Tertiary (3°) alcohol: OH attached to a carbon bonded to three other carbons (and no H atoms).

Worked Example: Identifying Alcohol Class by Oxidation

When oxidised with acidified K₂Cr₂O₇:

• Primary alcohol → aldehyde (distil) → carboxylic acid (reflux)

• Secondary alcohol → ketone (reflux)

• Tertiary alcohol → no reaction (no H on OH carbon to remove)

Oxidation of Alcohols

Oxidising agents: acidified potassium dichromate(VI) (K₂Cr₂O₇/H₂SO₄) — orange → green, or acidified potassium manganate(VII) (KMnO₄/H₂SO₄) — purple → colourless.

Primary alcohol → Aldehyde: Distil off the aldehyde as it forms
CH₃CH₂OH + [O] → CH₃CHO + H₂O

Primary alcohol → Carboxylic acid: Heat under reflux
CH₃CH₂OH + 2[O] → CH₃COOH + H₂O

Secondary alcohol → Ketone: Heat under reflux
CH₃CH(OH)CH₃ + [O] → CH₃COCH₃ + H₂O

Tertiary alcohol: No oxidation under these conditions
Oxidation of Primary Alcohols — Apparatus
  1. To aldehyde: Distillation apparatus. The aldehyde has a lower boiling point and distils off immediately, preventing further oxidation.
  2. To carboxylic acid: Reflux apparatus. The condenser returns vapours to the flask, allowing complete oxidation.
Worked Example: Distinguishing Alcohols

Describe how to distinguish between butan-1-ol, butan-2-ol, and 2-methylpropan-2-ol using acidified K₂Cr₂O₇.

Solution:

• Butan-1-ol (primary): Orange → green on warming. If distilled, aldehyde (butanal) forms. If refluxed, carboxylic acid (butanoic acid) forms.

• Butan-2-ol (secondary): Orange → green on warming. Ketone (butanone) forms.

• 2-methylpropan-2-ol (tertiary): No colour change.

Aldehydes and Ketones

📘 Key Definitions

Aldehyde: Contains the −CHO group, bonded to at least one H atom.

Ketone: Contains the C=O group bonded to two carbon atoms.

Distinguishing Tests

TestAldehydeKetone
Tollens' reagent [Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺Silver mirror formsNo reaction
Fehling's / Benedict's solutionRed precipitate (Cu₂O)No reaction
Acidified K₂Cr₂O₇Orange → greenNo reaction
2,4-DNP (Brady's reagent)Orange/yellow precipitateOrange/yellow precipitate
Tollens' and Fehling's tests are positive for aldehydes because aldehydes are easily oxidised to carboxylic acids. Ketones resist oxidation (without breaking C-C bonds). 2,4-DNP gives a precipitate with BOTH aldehydes and ketones — it tests for the carbonyl group, not specifically for aldehydes.
Worked Example: Tollens' Test Equation

CH₃CHO + 2[Ag(NH₃)₂]⁺ + 3OH⁻ → CH₃COO⁻ + 2Ag + 4NH₃ + 2H₂O

Ethanal is oxidised to ethanoate; silver ions are reduced to silver metal (mirror).

Carboxylic Acids and Esters

Carboxylic acids contain the −COOH group. They are weak acids that partially dissociate in water.

CH₃COOH ⇌ CH₃COO⁻ + H⁺     Kₐ = 1.8 × 10⁻⁵ mol dm⁻³

Acid strength increases with: • Electronegative substituents (electron-withdrawing groups stabilise the anion) • Number of electronegative atoms Example: ClCH₂COOH is stronger than CH₃COOH because Cl withdraws electron density, stabilising the carboxylate anion.

Esterification

Carboxylic acid + Alcohol ⇌ Ester + Water
CH₃COOH + C₂H₅OH ⇌ CH₃COOC₂H₅ + H₂O
Conditions: Concentrated H₂SO₄ catalyst, heat under reflux.
Esterification is reversible. To increase yield: use excess of one reactant, or remove product (e.g., distil off ester or water).

Hydrolysis of Esters

Acid hydrolysis: Ester + H₂O ⇌ Acid + Alcohol (dilute H₂SO₄, heat, reversible)
Alkaline hydrolysis (saponification): Ester + NaOH → Carboxylate salt + Alcohol (irreversible)
CH₃COOC₂H₅ + NaOH → CH₃COONa + C₂H₅OH
Worked Example: Identifying an Unknown Ester

An ester C₄H₈O₂ is hydrolysed to give a carboxylic acid that turns orange dichromate green, and an alcohol that also turns dichromate green.

Solution: The acid must be ethanoic (oxidisable? Actually ethanoic acid resists further oxidation). Wait — let me reconsider. If the acid is methanoic (HCOOH), it can be oxidised. If the alcohol is primary, it oxidises too. The ester is likely ethyl methanoate (HCOOCH₂CH₃) or methyl propanoate. Actually ethyl ethanoate (CH₃COOCH₂CH₃) gives ethanoic acid (no further oxidation with dichromate) and ethanol (oxidises). So the alcohol is ethanol and the acid is ethanoic acid. The ester is ethyl ethanoate.

Nucleophilic Substitution Mechanisms

SN1 and SN2 Mechanisms

FeatureSN1SN2
StepsTwo stepsOne step
IntermediateCarbocationTransition state (no intermediate)
Rate equationRate = k[halogenoalkane]Rate = k[halogenoalkane][nucleophile]
StereochemistryRacemisationInversion of configuration
Favoured byTertiary halogenoalkanesPrimary halogenoalkanes
SN1 Mechanism — Tertiary Halogenoalkane

Step 1 (slow, RDS): (CH₃)₃C-Br → (CH₃)₃C⁺ + Br⁻

Formation of tertiary carbocation.

Step 2 (fast): (CH₃)₃C⁺ + OH⁻ → (CH₃)₃C-OH

Nucleophile attacks from either side → racemic mixture if chiral.

SN2 Mechanism — Primary Halogenoalkane

CH₃CH₂-Br + OH⁻ → CH₃CH₂-OH + Br⁻

The nucleophile attacks from the opposite side to the leaving group.

The transition state has a trigonal bipyramidal geometry.

Inversion of configuration occurs at the chiral centre (Walden inversion).

Primary halogenoalkanes favour SN2 because there is less steric hindrance for the nucleophile to attack. Tertiary halogenoalkanes favour SN1 because the tertiary carbocation is highly stable.

Exam-Style Questions

📝 Exam Question 1

(a) Explain why propan-1-ol has a higher boiling point than propane. [2]

(b) Draw the apparatus used to oxidise propan-1-ol to propanal. [2]

(c) State the colour change observed. [1]

Mark Scheme:
(a) Propan-1-ol has hydrogen bonding between molecules (1). Propane only has van der Waals forces (1). Hydrogen bonding is stronger than van der Waals.
(b) Distillation apparatus showing flask, thermometer, condenser, collection vessel (2).
(c) Orange to green (1).
📝 Exam Question 2

Describe chemical tests to distinguish between propanal and propanone. [4]

Mark Scheme:
Add Tollens' reagent (1). Propanal gives silver mirror (1). Propanone gives no reaction (1).
Or: Add Fehling's solution and warm (1). Propanal gives red precipitate (1). Propanone gives no reaction (1). Max 4.

📝 Quick Summary

  • Alcohols classified as primary, secondary, tertiary based on OH-bearing carbon
  • Primary → aldehyde (distil) → carboxylic acid (reflux)
  • Secondary → ketone (reflux)
  • Tertiary → resistant to oxidation
  • Oxidising agent: acidified K₂Cr₂O₇ (orange → green)
  • Aldehydes reduce Tollens' (silver mirror) and Fehling's (red ppt); ketones do not
  • 2,4-DNP tests for carbonyl group in both aldehydes and ketones
  • Esterification: acid + alcohol ⇌ ester + water (conc. H₂SO₄, reversible)
  • SN1: two steps, carbocation intermediate, racemisation, tertiary favoured
  • SN2: one step, inversion, primary favoured

💡 Exam Tips

  • Draw apparatus carefully — distinguish distillation from reflux
  • Primary alcohol oxidation requires two steps to reach carboxylic acid
  • Tollens' and Fehling's are aldehyde-specific; 2,4-DNP is carbonyl-general
  • For ester hydrolysis: acid = reversible; alkali = irreversible
  • SN1/SN2: steric hindrance and carbocation stability determine mechanism
