📗 AS Level Content

Amines

Amines are derivatives of ammonia where one or more H atoms are replaced by alkyl/aryl groups:

  • Primary amine: RNH₂ (e.g., methylamine CH₃NH₂)
  • Secondary amine: R₂NH (e.g., dimethylamine (CH₃)₂NH)
  • Tertiary amine: R₃N (e.g., trimethylamine (CH₃)₃N)

Amines are weak bases (lone pair on N accepts protons):

CH₃NH₂ + H₂O ⇌ CH₃NH₃⁺ + OH⁻
Basicity order: tertiary < secondary < primary < NH₃ (in gas phase) / tertiary > secondary > primary (in aqueous, due to solvation)

Mechanisms & Pathways

AS Chemistry requires understanding reaction mechanisms in detail:

Preparation of Amines

  • Reduction of nitriles: CH₃CN + 4[H] → CH₃CH₂NH₂ (LiAlH₄ or H₂/Ni)
  • Reduction of nitro compounds: C₆H₅NO₂ + 6[H] → C₆H₅NH₂ (Sn/HCl or H₂/Ni)
  • Hofmann degradation of amides: RCONH₂ + Br₂ + 4NaOH → RNH₂ + Na₂CO₃ + 2NaBr + 2H₂O

Reactions of Amines

  • With acids: form salts (e.g., CH₃NH₂ + HCl → CH₃NH₃⁺Cl⁻)
  • With acyl chlorides: form amides
  • With halogenoalkanes: further substitution to give 2°, 3° amines, then quaternary salts

Nitriles

Contain -C≡N group. Prepared from halogenoalkanes + KCN (in ethanol):

CH₃CH₂Br + KCN → CH₃CH₂CN + KBr
CH₃CH₂CN + 2H₂O + H⁺ → CH₃CH₂COOH + NH₄⁺ (hydrolysis)

📙 A2 Level Content

Arenediazonium Salts

Prepared from primary aromatic amines at low temperature (0-5°C):

C₆H₅NH₂ + NaNO₂ + 2HCl → C₆H₅N₂⁺Cl⁻ + NaCl + 2H₂O (at 0-5°C)

Synthesis & Analysis

A2 level focuses on multi-step synthesis and analytical techniques:

Diazonium salts are unstable above 10°C. They undergo coupling reactions and replacement reactions.

Coupling Reactions (Azo Dye Formation)

C₆H₅N₂⁺ + C₆H₅OH → C₆H₅-N=N-C₆H₄OH (pH 8-9, alkaline)
C₆H₅N₂⁺ + C₆H₅NH₂ → C₆H₅-N=N-C₆H₄NH₂ (pH 5-6, weakly acidic)

Azo dyes are coloured due to extended conjugation. Used in textiles and food colourings.

Amides

RCONH₂. Key reactions:

  • Hydrolysis: acid → carboxylic acid + NH₄⁺; alkali → carboxylate + NH₃
  • Reduction: LiAlH₄ → primary amine
  • Hofmann degradation: RCONH₂ + Br₂ + 4NaOH → RNH₂ + Na₂CO₃ + 2NaBr + 2H₂O
  • Dehydration: P₄O₁₀ or SOCl₂ → nitrile

Amino Acids

Contain both -NH₂ and -COOH. Exist as zwitterions at pH 7:

⁺NH₃-CH(R)-COO⁻ (zwitterion at isoelectric point)

Form peptide bonds (amide links) in proteins:

H₂N-CH(R)-COOH + H₂N-CH(R')-COOH → H₂N-CH(R)-CO-NH-CH(R')-COOH + H₂O

🧪 Worked Example

Question: Calculate the mass of NaOH needed to make 500 cm³ of 0.2 mol/dm³ solution. (Mᵣ NaOH = 40)

Solution:
n = c × V = 0.2 × (500/1000) = 0.1 mol
mass = n × Mᵣ = 0.1 × 40 = 4.0 g
Answer: 4.0 g
Forgetting to convert cm³ to dm³ (÷1000) when using n = c × V. Always check your units!
In a balanced equation, mole ratios are the stoichiometric coefficients. Use these to convert between substances.

⚡ Quick Recall

  • n = mass / Mᵣ
  • n = concentration × volume (dm³)
  • 1 dm³ = 1000 cm³
  • % yield = (actual / theoretical) × 100

Last-Minute Summary

🎯 Key Points to Remember

  • Amines: weak bases; primary RNH₂, secondary R₂NH, tertiary R₃N
  • Prepare amines by reduction of nitriles, nitro compounds, or Hofmann degradation
  • Diazonium salts: prepared at 0-5°C from primary aromatic amine + NaNO₂ + HCl
  • Azo dyes from coupling diazonium salts with phenol (alkaline) or aromatic amine (weak acid)
  • Sandmeyer reactions: CuCl, CuBr, CuCN replace -N₂⁺ with -Cl, -Br, -CN
  • Amides hydrolyse to carboxylic acids; reduce to amines; Hofmann degrades to amines
  • Amino acids exist as zwitterions; form peptide bonds (condensation)

⚠️ Common Mistakes

  • Heating diazonium salts above 10°C (they decompose!)
  • Confusing amide hydrolysis conditions (acid vs alkali products differ)
  • Forgetting the pH requirements for azo coupling
  • Not recognising that amino acids are amphoteric
