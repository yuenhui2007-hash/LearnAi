Research Methods

9990 — Foundations of Psychological Investigation

IGCSE Content

Research methods are not a standalone topic at IGCSE. Basic understanding of experiments and data collection supports science subjects generally.

AS Level Content

Types of Research Methods

  • Experiment (实验室/现场实验): IV manipulated, DV measured, controls in place. Only method showing causation.
  • Natural Experiment: IV occurs naturally; researcher doesn't manipulate it. Less control but higher ecological validity.
  • Naturalistic Observation: Behaviour observed in natural setting without interference. High ecological validity; no causation.
  • Controlled Observation: Structured setting with some controls. Balance between control and naturalness.
  • Self-Report: Questionnaires and interviews. Quick data collection but subject to social desirability bias.
  • Case Study: In-depth study of one individual/group. Rich detail but not generalisable.
  • Correlational Study: Measures relationship between two variables. No causation — third variable problem.

Experimental Design

Independent Measures (Between-groups):
- Different participants in each condition
- Pros: no order effects
- Cons: individual differences between groups

Repeated Measures (Within-subjects):
- Same participants in all conditions
- Pros: no individual differences; fewer participants needed
- Cons: order effects (practice, fatigue, boredom)
→ Counterbalance: half do A then B, half do B then A

Matched Pairs:
- Participants matched on key variables, one in each condition
- Pros: controls individual differences without order effects
- Cons: difficult to match perfectly; time-consuming

Sampling Methods

  • Random: Every member of population has equal chance. Unbiased but may not be representative.
  • Opportunity: Use whoever is available. Quick but biased.
  • Systematic: Select every nth person. Simple but may follow a pattern.
  • Stratified: Population divided into subgroups; sample proportionally from each. Representative but complex.

Key Terms

Independent Variable (IV): Variable manipulated by researcher
Dependent Variable (DV): Variable measured by researcher
Extraneous Variable: Unwanted variable that could affect DV
Confounding Variable: Extraneous variable that actually DOES affect DV

Operationalisation: Defining variables in measurable terms
Reliability: Consistency — would the study produce similar results if repeated?
Validity: Accuracy — does the study measure what it claims to measure?
- Internal validity: causal conclusions trustworthy?
- External validity: generalisable to other settings/people?
- Ecological validity: applicable to real life?

A2 Level Content

Data Analysis

  • Descriptive statistics: Mean, median, mode, range, standard deviation
  • Inferential statistics: Tests of significance — determine if results are likely due to chance
  • Levels of measurement: Nominal (categories), Ordinal (ranked), Interval/Ratio (equal intervals)

Ethical Guidelines

  • Informed consent: Participants must know what they're agreeing to
  • Deception: Only when necessary; must be debriefed
  • Debriefing: Explain true purpose after study; offer support
  • Right to withdraw: Participants can leave at any time
  • Confidentiality: Data anonymised; stored securely
  • Protection from harm: Physical and psychological wellbeing prioritised

Qualitative vs Quantitative

Quantitative:
- Numerical data, statistical analysis
- Objective, replicable
- Methods: experiments, questionnaires with closed questions
- Strengths: precise, generalisable, testable hypotheses
- Weaknesses: lacks depth, may miss individual experience

Qualitative:
- Non-numerical data: words, images, descriptions
- Subjective, rich detail
- Methods: interviews, open-ended questionnaires, case studies, observations
- Strengths: depth, insight, generates hypotheses
- Weaknesses: difficult to replicate, researcher bias, time-consuming

Last-Minute Summary

  • Experiment = only method showing causation; has IV, DV, controls
  • Designs: Independent measures, Repeated measures, Matched pairs
  • Sampling: Random, Opportunity, Systematic, Stratified
  • Operationalisation: define variables in measurable terms
  • Reliability = consistency; Validity = accuracy (internal, external, ecological)
  • Quantitative = numerical, statistical; Qualitative = words, depth, insight
  • Ethics: consent, deception, debriefing, right to withdraw, confidentiality, protection from harm
  • Descriptive stats: mean, median, mode, range, SD
  • Levels of measurement: Nominal, Ordinal, Interval/Ratio
  • Always evaluate: What are the strengths AND weaknesses of this method?
