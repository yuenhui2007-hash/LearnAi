IGCSE AS Level

Work, Energy & Power

9702 (A-Level) · 0625 (IGCSE)

Detailed Notes

1. Work Done

Work done by a force is the product of the force and the displacement in the direction of the force. Work is a scalar quantity measured in joules (J). One joule is defined as the work done when a force of one newton moves its point of application by one metre in the direction of the force.

W = Fs cos θ

where F is the magnitude of the force, s is the displacement, and θ is the angle between the force and displacement vectors. When the force and displacement are in the same direction (θ = 0°), this simplifies to W = Fs. When they are perpendicular (θ = 90°), no work is done.

📖 Key Definition

Work: Work done by a constant force is the product of the force and the displacement in the direction of the force. Unit: joule (J).

If a force acts perpendicular to the direction of motion, it does no work on the object. For example, the tension in a string keeping a ball in circular motion does no work because it is always perpendicular to the velocity.

2. Energy

Energy is the capacity to do work. It is also a scalar quantity measured in joules. In mechanics, we primarily deal with kinetic energy, gravitational potential energy, and elastic potential energy.

  • Kinetic energy: The energy possessed by an object due to its motion.
    Eₖ = ½mv²
  • Gravitational potential energy: The energy stored in an object due to its position in a gravitational field. Near Earth's surface:
    Eₚ = mgh
    where h is the vertical height above a reference level.
  • Elastic potential energy: The energy stored in a stretched or compressed spring:
    Eₑ = ½kx²
    where k is the spring constant and x is the extension from the natural length.

3. Principle of Conservation of Energy

Energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transformed from one form to another or transferred from one body to another. In a closed system with no external work done, the total energy remains constant. This is one of the most fundamental principles in all of physics.

In real systems, some energy is always dissipated (usually as thermal energy and sound) due to resistive forces like friction and air resistance. "Conservation of energy" in mechanics problems often means that the loss in potential energy equals the gain in kinetic energy plus work done against resistive forces.

4. Power

Power is the rate at which work is done or energy is transferred. It measures how quickly energy is converted from one form to another.

Power: P = W/t = E/t
Also: P = Fv (when force and velocity are in the same direction)

Unit: watt (W) = J·s⁻¹. One watt is one joule per second.

5. Efficiency

No machine is 100% efficient because some energy is always lost to the surroundings, primarily as heat due to friction. Efficiency compares the useful energy output to the total energy input.

Efficiency = (useful power output / total power input) × 100%

Since power = energy/time, efficiency can also be expressed as useful energy output divided by total energy input. Efficiency is always less than 100% (or less than 1 if expressed as a decimal).

📝 Worked Example — Conservation of Energy with Friction

Question: A 2.0 kg block slides down a rough slope from a height of 5.0 m. Its speed at the bottom is 8.0 m·s⁻¹. Calculate the energy lost to friction. (g = 9.81 m·s⁻²)

Solution:

Initial GPE = mgh = 2.0 × 9.81 × 5.0 = 98.1 J

Final KE = ½mv² = ½ × 2.0 × 8.0² = 64.0 J

Energy lost to friction = 98.1 − 64.0 = 34.1 J

📝 Worked Example — Power and Efficiency

Question: A motor lifts a 500 kg load vertically at a constant speed of 0.40 m·s⁻¹. The motor is supplied with 2.5 kW of electrical power. Calculate the efficiency of the motor. (g = 9.81 m·s⁻²)

Solution:

Useful power output = force × velocity = weight × velocity = mgv

P_out = 500 × 9.81 × 0.40 = 1962 W

P_in = 2500 W

Efficiency = (1962/2500) × 100 = 78.5%

⚠️ Common Misconception

Many students confuse power and energy. Energy is the total capacity to do work (measured in joules), while power is the rate at which work is done (measured in watts). A powerful machine is not necessarily one that does a lot of work — it is one that does work quickly.

6. Derivation of Kinetic Energy

The kinetic energy formula Eₖ = ½mv² can be derived from the equations of motion and Newton's Second Law. Consider an object of mass m accelerated from rest (u = 0) to velocity v by a constant resultant force F over a displacement s.

From F = ma and v² = u² + 2as (with u = 0): v² = 2as → s = v²/(2a)

Work done W = Fs = ma × v²/(2a) = ½mv²

Since the work done by the resultant force equals the change in kinetic energy, we have Eₖ = ½mv².

📝 Worked Example — Braking Distance

Question: A car of mass 1500 kg travelling at 30 m·s⁻¹ brakes to a stop. Calculate the work done by the brakes and the average braking force if the braking distance is 75 m.

Solution:

Initial KE = ½ × 1500 × 30² = 675,000 J

Final KE = 0 (car stops)

Work done by brakes = change in KE = 0 − 675,000 = −675,000 J (negative = work done against motion)

Magnitude of work = 675,000 J

Average braking force = W/s = 675,000/75 = 9000 N

📝 Worked Example — Spring Energy

Question: A spring with spring constant k = 80 N/m is compressed by 0.15 m. A 0.20 kg ball is placed against it and released. Calculate the maximum speed of the ball. (Assume no energy losses.)

Solution:

Elastic PE stored = ½kx² = ½ × 80 × (0.15)² = 0.90 J

This converts entirely to kinetic energy: ½mv² = 0.90

v² = 2 × 0.90 / 0.20 = 9.0

v = 3.0 m·s⁻¹

📝 Worked Example — Variable Force

Question: A force F = (3x² + 2) N acts on a particle, where x is in metres. Calculate the work done as the particle moves from x = 0 to x = 4 m.

Solution:

For a variable force, work done = ∫F dx

W = ∫₀⁴ (3x² + 2) dx = [x³ + 2x]₀⁴ = (64 + 8) − (0 + 0) = 72 J

🎯 Exam-Style Question

Question: A cyclist and bicycle have a total mass of 85 kg. The cyclist freewheels down a slope, descending 12 m vertically. At the bottom, the speed is 14 m·s⁻¹. Calculate the average resistive force if the slope is 80 m long. (g = 9.81 m·s⁻²) [5 marks]

Mark Scheme

• Loss in GPE = mgh = 85 × 9.81 × 12 = 10,006 J (1 mark)

• Gain in KE = ½mv² = ½ × 85 × 14² = 8,330 J (1 mark)

• Work done against resistive force = 10,006 − 8,330 = 1,676 J (1 mark)

• F = W/s = 1,676/80 = 21.0 N (accept 20.9–21.0 N) (1 mark)

• State assumption: no other energy losses / cyclist starts from rest (1 mark)

🎯 Exam-Style Question

Question: (a) State the principle of conservation of energy. (b) A pump raises 250 kg of water through a vertical height of 8.0 m every minute. Calculate the minimum power output of the pump. (c) The pump is 65% efficient. Calculate the electrical power input. [5 marks]

Mark Scheme

• (a) Total energy of an isolated system is constant / energy cannot be created or destroyed, only transferred from one form to another (1 mark)

• (b) Work per minute = mgh = 250 × 9.81 × 8.0 = 19,620 J (1 mark)

• Power = 19,620/60 = 327 W (accept 330 W) (1 mark)

• (c) Efficiency = P_out/P_in → P_in = 327/0.65 = 503 W (accept 500 W) (2 marks — 1 method, 1 answer)

Additional Applications

  • Real-world context: Hydroelectric dams convert gravitational potential energy of water into electrical energy. The power output depends on the height of the dam and the flow rate of water (P = ρgh × flow rate).
  • Transport: Vehicle fuel consumption is related to the work done against resistive forces. At constant speed on a level road, all engine power goes into overcoming drag and rolling resistance.
  • Sports science: The power output of an athlete can be calculated from the rate of climbing stairs or lifting weights, giving insights into fitness and performance.

🎯 Exam-Style Question

Question: A car of mass 1200 kg accelerates from rest to a speed of 25 m·s⁻¹ on a level road. The total resistive force is constant at 800 N. Calculate: (a) the kinetic energy gained by the car, (b) the total work done by the car's engine, (c) the average power developed by the engine if the acceleration takes 15 s. [5 marks]

Mark Scheme

• (a) KE = ½mv² = ½ × 1200 × 25² = 3.75 × 10⁵ J (1 mark)

• (b) Work done against resistance = F × s. First find s using average velocity: s = (0+25)/2 × 15 = 187.5 m. Work against resistance = 800 × 187.5 = 1.50 × 10⁵ J. Total work = 3.75 × 10⁵ + 1.50 × 10⁵ = 5.25 × 10⁵ J (2 marks — 1 for method, 1 for answer)

• (c) P = W/t = 5.25 × 10⁵ / 15 = 3.50 × 10⁴ W (or 35.0 kW) (2 marks — 1 for method, 1 for answer)

Last-Minute Summary

  • Work W = Fs cos θ (J)
  • Kinetic energy Eₖ = ½mv²; GPE Eₚ = mgh; Elastic Eₑ = ½kx²
  • Conservation of energy: total energy constant in a closed system; account for energy dissipated by resistive forces
  • Power P = W/t = E/t = Fv (W)
  • Efficiency = (useful output / total input) × 100%
  • For objects moving at constant velocity: power input = power output + power lost to resistive forces

Exam Tips

  • Read the question carefully — identify exactly what is being asked (work, energy, power, or efficiency).
  • Show all working — method marks are often awarded even if the final answer is wrong.
  • Check units — ensure your final answer has the correct unit (J for energy, W for power).
  • When using conservation of energy, list all forms of energy at the start and end, including any work done against resistive forces.
  • Manage your time — don't spend too long on any single question.
