IGCSE AS A2

The Cold War in Europe, 1941–1995

history-h3

📘 IGCSE Content

Origins (1941–47)

  • Grand Alliance tensions: Different ideologies, delayed Second Front
  • Yalta (Feb 1945): Agreed on post-war Germany; disagreed on Poland
  • Potsdam (July 1945): Truman (atomic bomb knowledge), Stalin — increasing suspicion
  • Iron Curtain speech (March 1946): Churchill — symbolic division of Europe

Containment & Expansion

  • Truman Doctrine (1947): US commitment to stop communism globally
  • Marshall Plan (1948): $13bn economic aid to rebuild Western Europe
  • Berlin Blockade (1948–49): Soviet test; defeated by Western airlift
  • NATO (1949): Military alliance of Western powers

Key Crises

  • Hungarian Uprising (1956): Crushed by Soviet tanks; West did not intervene
  • Berlin Wall (1961): Symbol of division; stopped refugee flow
  • Prague Spring (1968): Dubcek's reforms crushed by Warsaw Pact invasion

📗 AS Level Content

Ideological Conflict

  • Capitalism vs Communism: Free markets vs state planning; democracy vs one-party rule
  • Security dilemma: Both sides saw defensive measures as threats

Détente (1969–79)

  • SALT I (1972): Limited nuclear arms
  • Helsinki Accords (1975): Recognised post-war borders; human rights provisions
  • Reasons: Nuclear parity, economic costs, Sino-Soviet split

📙 A2 Level Content

End of Cold War (1985–91)

  • Gorbachev's reforms: Glasnost (openness) + Perestroika (restructuring)
  • Fall of Berlin Wall (9 Nov 1989): Symbolic end of division
  • German reunification (1990): NATO membership issue resolved
  • Soviet collapse (Dec 1991): Gorbachev resigned; USSR dissolved

Historiography

  • Traditionalists: Soviet expansion caused Cold War
  • Revisionists: US economic imperialism provoked Soviet response
  • Post-revisionists: Mutual misunderstanding and security dilemma

📜 Worked Example

Question: To what extent was Germany responsible for WWI?

Essay Plan:
FOR: Blank cheque, Schlieffen Plan, naval expansion, Fischer thesis
AGAINST: Austrian ultimatum, Russian mobilisation, alliance system, Clark "sleepwalkers"
Conclusion: Significant responsibility but not sole blame
Writing narrative instead of analysis. History essays need CAUSATION — explain WHY and SIGNIFICANCE.
Always use SPECIFIC EVIDENCE — dates, names, statistics. "On 28 June 1914" not "in 1914".

⚡ Quick Recall

  • Long-term: Militarism, Alliances, Imperialism, Nationalism
  • Short-term: Assassination of Franz Ferdinand
  • Treaty of Versailles: War guilt, reparations, territorial losses
  • Cold War: Containment, Truman Doctrine, Marshall Plan

Source Analysis Framework

  • Content: What does the source say? What information does it provide?
  • Provenance: Who wrote it? When? Why? What is their perspective?
  • Reliability: Is it trustworthy? What biases might exist?
  • Utility: How useful is it for a specific enquiry?

Essay Structure

  • Introduction: Define key terms, set out argument/line of reasoning
  • Paragraphs: Point → Evidence → Explanation → Link back to question
  • Synthesis: Compare factors, weigh importance, show interconnections
  • Conclusion: Judgment based on evidence, avoid introducing new points
Always answer the EXACT question asked. Underline key words ("To what extent", "How far", "Most important") and address them directly.
