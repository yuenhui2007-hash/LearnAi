IBMAA1: Number & Algebra

Overview

Number and Algebra (Topic 1) forms the foundation of mathematical reasoning in IB Mathematics AA. It includes sequences and series, exponents, logarithms, the binomial theorem, proof techniques, and complex numbers for HL. This topic appears across all papers and underpins much of calculus and functions.

1.1 Sequences & Series

Arithmetic Sequences

uₙ = u₁ + (n − 1)d
S�� = n/2 (2u₁ + (n − 1)d) = n/2 (u₁ + uₙ)

Geometric Sequences

uₙ = u₁ r^(n−1)
S�� = u₁(1 − rⁿ)/(1 − r) for r ≠ 1
S_∞ = u₁/(1 − r) for |r| < 1

Worked Example 1: Geometric Series

Question: Find the sum to infinity of 8 + 4 + 2 + 1 + ...

Solution:

u₁ = 8, r = 4/8 = 0.5
S_∞ = 8/(1 − 0.5) = 16

1.2 Exponents & Logarithms

a^m × a^n = a^(m+n)
a^m / a^n = a^(m−n)
(a^m)^n = a^(mn)
a^0 = 1
logₐ(xy) = logₐx + logₐy
logₐ(x/y) = logₐx − logₐy
logₐ(x^n) = n logₐx
logₐa = 1, logₐ1 = 0
Change of base: logₐb = log_c b / log_c a

Worked Example 2: Solving Exponential Equations

Question: Solve 3^(2x) = 7.

Solution:

Take ln of both sides: 2x ln 3 = ln 7
x = ln 7 / (2 ln 3) = 0.885 (3 sf)

1.3 Binomial Theorem

(a + b)ⁿ = Σ (n choose r) a^(n−r) b^r for r = 0 to n
where (n choose r) = n! / (r!(n−r)!)

Worked Example 3: Binomial Expansion

Question: Find the coefficient of x³ in the expansion of (2x + 1)⁵.

Solution:

General term: (5 choose r)(2x)^(5−r)(1)^r
For x³: 5−r = 3 → r = 2
Coefficient = (5 choose 2)(2)³ = 10 × 8 = 80

1.4 Proof Techniques [HL only]

  • Proof by deduction: direct logical argument from known facts.
  • Proof by exhaustion: checking all possible cases.
  • Proof by contradiction: assume the opposite is true and show this leads to a contradiction.
  • Proof by induction: prove for n=1, assume for n=k, prove for n=k+1.

HL Exam Focus

May 2024 HL Paper 1: Proof by induction for a summation formula.

1.5 Complex Numbers [HL only]

z = a + bi, where i² = −1
Modulus: |z| = √(a² + b²)
Argument: arg(z) = arctan(b/a) (adjusted for quadrant)
Polar form: z = r(cis θ) = r(cos θ + i sin θ)
De Moivre's theorem: (r cis θ)ⁿ = rⁿ cis(nθ)

Common Misconceptions

  • Geometric series: S_∞ only converges if |r| < 1.
  • Logarithms: log(a + b) ≠ log a + log b. Only multiplication inside the log becomes addition outside.
  • Binomial: The power of the first term decreases; the power of the second term increases.
  • Proof by induction: Students often forget to prove the n=1 base case.

Exam-Specific Information

  • Paper 1: 3–4 questions on sequences, logarithms, and binomial expansions.
  • Paper 2: Contextual problems involving compound interest, depreciation, and population models.
  • Paper 3 (HL): Proof questions and complex number problems.
  • IA: Number theory explorations and proof-based investigations are possible.

IBMAA2: Functions

Detailed Notes

1. Quadratics

  • Solving: Factorising, formula, completing square
  • Formula: x = [−b ± √(b²−4ac)]/(2a)
  • Discriminant: b²−4ac > 0 (2 roots), = 0 (1 root), < 0 (no real roots)

2. Functions

  • Domain: Allowed input values
  • Range: Output values
  • Inverse: f⁻¹(x); reflection in y = x
  • Composite: fg(x) = f(g(x))

3. Inequalities

  • Solve like equations; reverse sign when ×/÷ by negative

Last-Minute Summary

  • Quadratic formula: x = [−b ± √(b²−4ac)]/(2a)
  • Discriminant determines number of roots
  • Domain = inputs; Range = outputs
  • Reverse inequality when ×/÷ by negative

Exam Tips

  • Always check your solutions by substitution
  • For inequalities involving quadratics, sketch the graph first
  • When finding range of composite function, consider domain of inner function
  • Inverse functions: domain and range swap

IBMAA3: Geometry

Detailed Notes

1. Trigonometry

  • SOH CAH TOA
  • Sine rule: a/sin A = b/sin B = c/sin C
  • Cosine rule: a² = b² + c² − 2bc cos A
  • Area: ½ab sin C

2. Radians

  • π rad = 180°
  • Arc length: s = rθ
  • Sector area: A = ½r²θ

Last-Minute Summary

  • Sine rule: a/sin A = b/sin B
  • Cosine rule: a² = b² + c² − 2bc cos A
  • Arc: s = rθ; Sector: A = ½r²θ

Exam Tips

  • When using sine rule for angle: ambiguous case possible (two solutions)
  • Cosine rule for included angle or when all three sides known
  • Convert degrees to radians for calculus (differentiation/integration)
  • Small angle approximations: sin θ ≈ θ, tan θ ≈ θ, cos θ ≈ 1 − θ²/2

IBMAA4: Statistics

Overview

Statistics and Probability (Topic 4) covers data analysis, probability rules, distributions, and regression. HL students study additional distributions and hypothesis testing. This topic is heavily tested in Paper 2 and is useful for the IA.

4.1 Descriptive Statistics

Mean: x̄ = Σx / n
Median: middle value when data is ordered
Mode: most frequent value

Variance: σ² = Σ(x − x̄)² / n (population) or s² = Σ(x − x̄)² / (n−1) (sample)
Standard deviation: σ = √variance
Interquartile range: IQR = Q₃ − Q₁

4.2 Probability

P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B)
P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B|A) = P(B) × P(A|B)
Conditional probability: P(A|B) = P(A ∩ B) / P(B)
Independent events: P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B)
Mutually exclusive: P(A ∩ B) = 0

4.3 Discrete Distributions

Binomial Distribution

X ~ B(n, p)
P(X = r) = (n choose r) p^r (1−p)^(n−r)
E(X) = np, Var(X) = np(1−p)

Worked Example 1: Binomial

Question: A fair die is rolled 10 times. Find the probability of getting exactly 3 sixes.

Solution:

X ~ B(10, 1/6)
P(X = 3) = (10 choose 3)(1/6)³(5/6)⁷ = 120 × (1/216) × (5/6)⁷ ≈ 0.155

4.4 Continuous Distributions

Normal Distribution

X ~ N(μ, σ²)
Standardise: Z = (X − μ)/σ
Z ~ N(0, 1)
Use GDC or tables to find probabilities.

4.5 Correlation & Regression

Pearson's correlation coefficient: r = cov(X,Y) / (σ_X σ_Y)
−1 ≤ r ≤ 1; r close to ±1 indicates strong linear correlation

Regression line: y = ax + b
a = cov(X,Y) / var(X), b = ȳ − a x̄

Extrapolation beyond the data range is unreliable.

4.6 Hypothesis Testing [HL only]

Null hypothesis (H₀): statement of no effect
Alternative hypothesis (H₁): statement of effect

p-value: probability of obtaining results at least as extreme as observed, assuming H₀ is true
If p-value < significance level (α), reject H₀

HL Exam Focus

May 2024 HL Paper 3: Chi-squared test for independence with contingency table.

Common Misconceptions

  • Correlation vs. causation: Correlation does not imply causation.
  • Independent vs. mutually exclusive: Independent events can occur together; mutually exclusive events cannot.
  • Normal distribution: The total area under the curve is 1, not the height.
  • p-value: A small p-value means the data is unlikely under H₀, not that H₀ is definitely false.

Exam-Specific Information

  • Paper 1: 2–3 questions on probability and basic statistics.
  • Paper 2: Structured questions on distributions, regression, and data analysis.
  • Paper 3 (HL): Hypothesis testing and advanced probability.
  • IA: Statistical investigations (correlation studies, probability simulations) are popular.

Past Paper Trends (2019–2024)

May 2023: Binomial probability with conditional probability.

  • P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B).

IBMAA5: Calculus

Overview

Calculus (Topic 5) is the capstone of IB Mathematics AA, covering limits, differentiation, integration, and differential equations. HL students study additional techniques and Maclaurin series. This topic is the most heavily weighted in all exam papers.

5.1 Limits & Continuity

lim(x→a) f(x) = L means f(x) approaches L as x approaches a
A function is continuous at x = a if lim(x→a) f(x) = f(a)

Important limits:
lim(x→0) sin x / x = 1
lim(x→0) (e^x − 1) / x = 1
lim(x→∞) (1 + 1/x)^x = e

5.2 Differentiation

Power rule: d/dx(x^n) = nx^(n−1)
Product rule: d/dx(uv) = u'v + uv'
Quotient rule: d/dx(u/v) = (u'v − uv')/v²
Chain rule: d/dx(f(g(x))) = f'(g(x)) × g'(x)

d/dx(sin x) = cos x
d/dx(cos x) = −sin x
d/dx(tan x) = sec²x
d/dx(e^x) = e^x
d/dx(ln x) = 1/x

Applications

  • Gradient of a curve: f'(x) at a point.
  • Tangents and normals: tangent gradient = f'(a); normal gradient = −1/f'(a).
  • Increasing/decreasing: f'(x) > 0 → increasing; f'(x) < 0 → decreasing.
  • Stationary points: f'(x) = 0. Second derivative test: f''(x) > 0 → minimum; f''(x) < 0 → maximum.
  • Optimisation: find maximum/minimum in a practical context.
  • Kinematics: s = displacement, v = ds/dt, a = dv/dt = d²s/dt².

Worked Example 1: Optimisation

Question: Find the dimensions of a rectangle with perimeter 20 m that maximises area.

Solution:

2x + 2y = 20 → y = 10 − x
A = x(10 − x) = 10x − x²
dA/dx = 10 − 2x = 0 → x = 5
y = 5 → square with side 5 m gives maximum area = 25 m²

5.3 Integration

∫ x^n dx = x^(n+1)/(n+1) + C (n ≠ −1)
∫ 1/x dx = ln|x| + C
∫ e^x dx = e^x + C
∫ sin x dx = −cos x + C
∫ cos x dx = sin x + C

Definite integral: ∫[a,b] f(x) dx = F(b) − F(a)
Area between curves: ∫[a,b] (f(x) − g(x)) dx where f(x) ≥ g(x)

Worked Example 2: Area

Question: Find the area enclosed by y = x² and y = x.

Solution:

Intersection: x² = x → x = 0, 1
Area = ∫[0,1] (x − x²) dx = [x²/2 − x³/3]₀¹ = ½ − ⅓ = 1/6

5.4 Differential Equations

First-order separable: dy/dx = f(x)g(y) → ∫ dy/g(y) = ∫ f(x) dx
Slope fields: sketch approximate solutions from dy/dx = F(x,y)
Euler's method: y_(n+1) = y_n + h × F(x_n, y_n)

5.5 Maclaurin Series [HL only]

f(x) = f(0) + f'(0)x + f''(0)x²/2! + f'''(0)x³/3! + ...

e^x = 1 + x + x²/2! + x³/3! + ...
sin x = x − x³/3! + x⁵/5! − ...
cos x = 1 − x²/2! + x⁴/4! − ...
ln(1+x) = x − x²/2 + x³/3 − ... (|x| < 1)

HL Exam Focus

May 2024 HL Paper 3: Maclaurin series approximation with error bound using Lagrange remainder.

Common Misconceptions

  • Constant of integration: Always include +C for indefinite integrals.
  • Area below x-axis: Definite integrals can be negative — area is the absolute value.
  • Chain rule: Remember to multiply by the derivative of the inner function.
  • Stationary points: f'(x) = 0 is necessary but not sufficient — check the second derivative or sign change.

Exam-Specific Information

  • Paper 1: 6–8 questions on differentiation, integration, and their applications.
  • Paper 2: Modelling and optimisation problems, area and volume calculations.
  • Paper 3 (HL): Proof-based calculus, Maclaurin series, and advanced differential equations.
  • IA: Calculus-based explorations (optimisation, area, kinematics) are very popular.

  • Separable differential equations: separate variables and integrate both sides.
  • HL: Maclaurin series = Taylor series centred at 0.
