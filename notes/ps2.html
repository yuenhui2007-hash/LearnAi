Biological Psychology

9990 — The Brain, Nervous System, and Behaviour

IGCSE Content

Biological psychology is not part of the IGCSE syllabus. Basic biology of the nervous system supports understanding at AS/A2.

AS Level Content

The Nervous System

  • Central Nervous System (CNS): Brain and spinal cord — processes information
  • Peripheral Nervous System (PNS): All other nerves — connects CNS to body
  • Somatic Nervous System: Voluntary control of skeletal muscles
  • Autonomic Nervous System (ANS): Involuntary control of internal organs
    • Sympathetic: "Fight or flight" — arousal, energy mobilisation
    • Parasympathetic: "Rest and digest" — calming, energy conservation

Neuron Structure and Function

Key parts:
- Dendrites: Receive messages from other neurons
- Cell body (soma): Contains nucleus; maintains cell
- Axon: Transmits signal away
- Myelin sheath: Insulates axon; speeds transmission
- Terminal buttons: Release neurotransmitters

Action potential: Electrical impulse travels down axon
- All-or-nothing principle: neuron fires fully or not at all
- Refractory period: brief recovery time after firing

Synaptic transmission:
1. Electrical impulse reaches terminal button
2. Neurotransmitters released into synaptic gap
3. Neurotransmitters bind to receptor sites on post-synaptic neuron
4. Excitatory or inhibitory message passed on
5. Reuptake or enzyme breakdown clears neurotransmitters

Key Neurotransmitters

  • Dopamine: Reward, pleasure, motor control. Linked to Parkinson's (too little) and schizophrenia (too much).
  • Serotonin: Mood regulation, sleep, appetite. Low levels linked to depression.
  • Acetylcholine (ACh): Learning, memory, muscle contraction. Alzheimer's linked to ACh deficiency.
  • Noradrenaline (Norepinephrine): Alertness, arousal, stress response.
  • GABA: Inhibitory — reduces neuronal excitability. Anxiety linked to low GABA.

Brain Structures

  • Cerebral cortex: Higher cognitive functions — divided into four lobes
    • Frontal lobe: decision-making, planning, personality, motor cortex
    • Parietal lobe: somatosensory processing, spatial awareness
    • Temporal lobe: auditory processing, language (Wernicke's area), memory
    • Occipital lobe: visual processing
  • limbic system: Emotion and memory
    • Hippocampus: memory formation
    • Amygdala: fear and emotion processing
    • Hypothalamus: regulates hormones, hunger, thirst, temperature
  • Brain stem: Basic life functions — breathing, heartbeat
  • Cerebellum: Balance, coordination, motor learning

A2 Level Content

Brain Imaging Techniques

  • fMRI (functional MRI): Measures blood flow to active brain areas. Good spatial resolution; poor temporal resolution.
  • PET scan: Measures glucose metabolism. Invasive (radioactive tracer); shows activity over time.
  • EEG (Electroencephalography): Measures electrical activity via scalp electrodes. Excellent temporal resolution; poor spatial resolution.
  • CT scan: X-ray images of brain structure. Good for detecting tumours/lesions; doesn't show function.

Localisation of Function

  • Phineas Gage (1848): Iron rod through frontal lobe — personality changed dramatically. Evidence for frontal lobe role in personality.
  • Broca's area: Frontal lobe — speech production. Damage = Broca's aphasia (can understand but can't speak fluently).
  • Wernicke's area: Temporal lobe — language comprehension. Damage = Wernicke's aphasia (fluent but meaningless speech).
  • Split-brain research (Sperry): Corpus callosum severed — each hemisphere processes information independently.

Hemispheric Lateralisation

  • Left hemisphere: Language, logic, sequential processing, analytical thinking
  • Right hemisphere: Spatial awareness, creativity, holistic processing, emotion
  • Most people are left-hemisphere dominant for language
  • Plasticity: the brain can reorganise after damage, especially in younger people

Last-Minute Summary

  • CNS = brain + spinal cord; PNS = somatic + autonomic (sympathetic/parasympathetic)
  • Neuron: dendrites → cell body → axon → terminal buttons
  • Action potential = all-or-nothing; synaptic transmission via neurotransmitters
  • Key neurotransmitters: dopamine, serotonin, acetylcholine, noradrenaline, GABA
  • Brain lobes: Frontal (decision), Parietal (sensory), Temporal (auditory/language), Occipital (vision)
  • Limbic system: hippocampus (memory), amygdala (fear), hypothalamus (hormones)
  • Brain imaging: fMRI (blood flow), PET (metabolism), EEG (electrical), CT (structure)
  • Broca's area = speech production; Wernicke's area = comprehension
  • Left hemisphere: language/logic; Right hemisphere: spatial/creativity
  • Plasticity: brain can reorganise after damage
