📋 Cambridge Exam Guidance (9706)

Paper Structure

  • Paper 1 (Multiple Choice): 1 hour, 30 marks — 30% of AS, 15% of A-Level
  • Paper 2 (Structured Questions): 1 hour 45 min, 90 marks — 70% of AS, 35% of A-Level
  • Paper 3 (Structured Questions - A2): 3 hours, 150 marks — 50% of A-Level

Key Assessment Objectives

  • AO1: Knowledge & Understanding (30%) — Define, identify, state accounting concepts
  • AO2: Application (40%) — Prepare financial statements, process accounting data
  • AO3: Analysis & Evaluation (30%) — Interpret, compare, advise, justify decisions
Critical Exam Rules:
  • Always show all workings — method marks are awarded even if final answer is wrong
  • Use correct formats for financial statements — incorrect format loses presentation marks
  • Read the question requirement carefully — "prepare" vs "show" vs "calculate"
  • Time management: 1 mark ≈ 1.2 minutes in Paper 3

IAS 1 — Presentation of Financial Statements

IAS 1 Presentation of Financial Statements

Complete Set of Financial Statements:

  1. Statement of Financial Position (SOFP) at end of period
  2. Statement of Profit or Loss (SOPL) and Other Comprehensive Income
  3. Statement of Changes in Equity (SOCIE)
  4. Statement of Cash Flows (IAS 7)
  5. Notes to the accounts

Fundamental Concepts (MUST MEMORIZE):

ConceptDefinitionApplication
Going ConcernEntity will continue operating for foreseeable futureAssets valued at going concern values, not liquidation
Accruals (Matching)Transactions recorded when incurred, not when cash movedMatch expenses to period they relate to
ConsistencySame accounting methods period to periodEnables meaningful comparison
PrudenceDo not overstate assets/income, understate liabilities/expensesProvide for losses, don't anticipate profits
MaterialityInformation that could influence decisionsOnly material items need separate disclosure
Business EntityBusiness separate from ownerOwner drawings = reduction in equity, not expense
Money MeasurementOnly transactions measurable in money recordedStaff skill not recorded as asset
Historical CostRecord at original transaction priceProvides objective, verifiable evidence
RealisationProfit only recognised when realisedDon't record profit until sale complete
DualityEvery transaction has two effectsDebit = Credit in every entry

MEMORIZE: Current vs Non-Current

  • Current Asset: Expected to be sold/used/consumed within 12 months
  • Non-Current Asset: Held for more than 12 months for use in business
  • Current Liability: Due to be settled within 12 months
  • Non-Current Liability: Due after more than 12 months

IAS 2 — Inventories

IAS 2 Inventories

Valuation Rule:

Inventory value = LOWER of Cost AND Net Realisable Value (NRV)

Cost Includes:

  • Purchase cost (after trade discounts, before settlement discounts)
  • Carriage/freight INWARDS
  • Import duties
  • Conversion costs (labour + production overheads)

Cost EXCLUDES:

  • Carriage OUTWARDS (selling expense)
  • Administrative overheads
  • Selling costs
  • Abnormal waste
  • Storage costs (unless necessary before next stage)

Net Realisable Value (NRV):

NRV = Expected selling price − Estimated costs of completion − Estimated costs to sell

MEMORIZE: Inventory Costing Methods

MethodWhen to UseFormula/Rule
FIFOPerishable goods, inflationFirst In, First Out — oldest inventory sold first
LIFONot permitted under IAS 2!Last In, First Out — NOT ALLOWED
AVCONon-perishable, price fluctuationsWeighted Average Cost = Total Cost / Total Units

Important: IAS 2 does NOT permit LIFO. Only FIFO and weighted average are acceptable.

IAS 16 — Property, Plant & Equipment

IAS 16 Property, Plant & Equipment

Initial Measurement:

Cost = Purchase price + Directly attributable costs − Trade discounts − Settlement discounts

Directly attributable costs: Site preparation, delivery, installation, professional fees, dismantling cost (present value)

Subsequent Measurement (Choose ONE policy):

Cost ModelRevaluation Model
Carry at cost − Accumulated depreciation − Impairment lossesCarry at fair value at revaluation date − Subsequent depreciation − Impairment losses
Simple, no revaluation neededAssets revalued regularly to fair value

Depreciation Methods (MUST KNOW):

MethodFormulaWhen Used
Straight Line(Cost − Residual Value) / Useful LifeConsistent benefit each period
Reducing BalanceCarrying Amount × Rate%More benefit in early years (tech assets)
Units of Production(Cost − RV) × (Actual Output / Total Expected Output)Usage varies significantly

MEMORIZE: Depreciation Rules

  • Depreciation starts when asset is available for use
  • Depreciation stops when asset is derecognised or classified as held for sale
  • Residual value and useful life reviewed annually
  • Change in estimate = prospective adjustment (not retrospective)
  • Land is not depreciated (unlimited life)
  • Buildings ARE depreciated

Revaluation Accounting (CRITICAL):

Upward revaluation:
Dr PPE (Statement of Financial Position)
    Cr Revaluation surplus (Other Comprehensive Income → Equity)

Downward revaluation (first time):
Dr Revaluation surplus (to extent of previous gains)
Dr Statement of Profit or Loss (remaining loss)
    Cr PPE

Depreciation after revaluation:
New depreciation = Revalued amount / Remaining useful life

Disposal of PPE:

Step 1: Update depreciation to date of disposal
Step 2: Remove cost and accumulated depreciation
Step 3: Record proceeds
Step 4: Calculate profit/loss on disposal

Profit/Loss = Sale proceeds − Carrying amount at disposal date

Journal:
Dr Bank (proceeds)
Dr Accumulated depreciation (clear)
Dr Loss on disposal (if CA > proceeds)
    Cr PPE at cost (clear)
    Cr Profit on disposal (if proceeds > CA)

IAS 37 — Provisions, Contingent Liabilities & Contingent Assets

IAS 37 Provisions, Contingent Liabilities and Contingent Assets

Definition of Provision:

A provision is a liability of uncertain timing or amount.

Recognition Criteria (ALL must be met):

  1. Present obligation from past event
  2. Probable outflow of economic benefits (>50% likely)
  3. Amount can be reliably estimated

Measurement:

Best estimate of expenditure required to settle obligation
If time value is material: Discount to present value using pre-tax discount rate
ItemDefinitionAccounting Treatment
ProvisionProbable outflow, reliable estimateRecognise as liability in SOFP, expense in SOPL
Contingent LiabilityPossible obligation OR probable but cannot estimate reliablyDO NOT recognise. Disclose in notes only
Contingent AssetPossible inflow from past eventDO NOT recognise. Disclose if probable

MEMORIZE: Probability Thresholds

  • Remote: No disclosure needed
  • Possible (not probable): Contingent liability — disclose
  • Probable (>50%): Recognise provision
  • Virtually certain: Recognise asset (no longer contingent)

Common Examples:

SituationTreatment
Warranty/guaranteeRecognise provision at point of sale
Legal claim against company (probable loss)Recognise provision
Environmental cleanup (legal/constructive obligation)Recognise provision
Restructuring (detailed formal plan announced)Recognise provision
Future operating lossesNO provision (future, not present obligation)

IFRS 15 — Revenue from Contracts with Customers

IFRS 15 Revenue from Contracts with Customers

5-Step Model (MUST MEMORIZE):

  1. Identify the contract with a customer
  2. Identify the performance obligations in the contract
  3. Determine the transaction price
  4. Allocate the transaction price to the performance obligations
  5. Recognise revenue when (or as) the entity satisfies a performance obligation

When to Recognise:

TypeWhen to RecogniseExample
Sale of goodsControl transferred to customer (usually at delivery)Retail sale
Services over timeAs service performed (percentage completion)Construction, consultancy
Services at point in timeWhen service completeHaircut, repair
SubscriptionsOver subscription periodSoftware licence

Transaction Price Adjustments:

  • Variable consideration: Estimate and constrain (don't overstate)
  • Significant financing component: Adjust for time value of money
  • Non-cash consideration: Measure at fair value
  • Consideration payable to customer: Deduct from transaction price

MEMORIZE: Trade vs Settlement Discounts

TypeWhen GivenAccounting Treatment
Trade DiscountAt point of sale (bulk, regular customer)Deduct from revenue at start
Settlement (Cash) DiscountFor early paymentDeduct only if payment made within discount period

IAS 8 — Accounting Policies, Changes in Estimates & Errors

IAS 8 Accounting Policies, Changes in Estimates and Errors
Change TypeTreatmentExample
Accounting Policy (voluntary or new standard)Retrospective — restate comparatives, adjust opening equityChange inventory valuation method
Accounting EstimateProspective — from date of change, no restatementChange depreciation method or useful life
Prior Period ErrorRetrospective restatement — correct all periodsOmission of transaction, fraud
Key Distinction:
Policy change = HOW you account for something (retrospective)
Estimate change = new information about existing situation (prospective)
Error = mistake in previous periods (retrospective restatement)

IAS 10 — Events After the Reporting Period

IAS 10 Events After the Reporting Period
TypeDefinitionAccounting Treatment
Adjusting EventsProvide evidence of conditions that existed at reporting dateAdjust financial statements
Non-Adjusting EventsIndicate conditions arising after reporting dateDo NOT adjust. Disclose if material

Examples of Adjusting Events:

  • Bankruptcy of customer who owed money at year end
  • Sale of inventory after year end at below cost (shows NRV lower than cost)
  • Evidence of fraud or errors discovered
  • Settlement of court case confirming obligation at year end

Examples of Non-Adjusting Events:

  • Natural disaster destroying assets after year end
  • Major purchase or disposal of subsidiary after year end
  • Announcement of plan to discontinue operation
  • Decline in market value of investments after year end

IAS 38 — Intangible Assets

IAS 38 Intangible Assets

Recognition Criteria:

  1. Identifiable (separable or arising from contractual rights)
  2. Control over resource
  3. Future economic benefits probable
  4. Cost can be measured reliably

Research vs Development:

PhaseDefinitionAccounting
ResearchOriginal investigation to gain new knowledgeExpense as incurred (cannot capitalise)
DevelopmentApplication of research to create new/improved productsCapitalise if ALL criteria met:

Development Capitalisation Criteria (ALL must be met):

  1. Technical feasibility of completing asset
  2. Intention to complete and use/sell
  3. Ability to use/sell
  4. How asset will generate future benefits
  5. Adequate resources available
  6. Expenditure can be reliably measured

MEMORIZE: Internally Generated Intangibles

Goodwill: Never capitalised internally. Only recognised in business combination.

Brands/Mastheads: Cannot capitalise internally generated.

Software: Can capitalise development costs if criteria met.

Amortisation:

  • Finite life intangibles: Amortise over useful life
  • Infinite life intangibles: No amortisation, annual impairment test

📈 Key Accounting Ratios

Profitability Ratios:

RatioFormulaMeaning
Gross Profit MarginGross Profit / Revenue × 100% of revenue kept after COGS
Operating Profit MarginOperating Profit / Revenue × 100% of revenue kept after all operating expenses
Net Profit MarginProfit for year / Revenue × 100Final % of revenue as profit
Return on Capital Employed (ROCE)Operating Profit / Capital Employed × 100Efficiency of using long-term funds

Capital Employed = Equity + Non-current liabilities (or Total Assets − Current Liabilities)

Liquidity Ratios:

RatioFormulaIdeal
Current RatioCurrent Assets / Current Liabilities1.5 – 2.0
Quick (Acid Test) Ratio(Current Assets − Inventory) / Current Liabilities1.0

Gearing Ratios:

RatioFormulaInterpretation
Gearing RatioNon-current Liabilities / Capital Employed × 100% funded by debt vs equity
Interest CoverOperating Profit / Finance CostsAbility to pay interest

Efficiency Ratios:

RatioFormulaMeaning
Inventory TurnoverCost of Sales / Average InventoryHow many times inventory sold per year
Inventory DaysAverage Inventory / Cost of Sales × 365Average days inventory held
Receivables DaysAverage Receivables / Revenue × 365Average days to collect payment
Payables DaysAverage Payables / Cost of Sales × 365Average days to pay suppliers
