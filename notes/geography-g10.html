3D Geometry & Mensuration

Geography G10

📘 IGCSE Content

1.1 Prisms and Cylinders

Volume of prism = cross-sectional area × length Volume of cylinder = πr²h Curved surface area of cylinder = 2πrh Total surface area of cylinder = 2πrh + 2πr²

1.2 Pyramids and Cones

Volume of pyramid = (1/3) × base area × height Volume of cone = (1/3)πr²h Curved surface area of cone = πrl Total surface area of cone = πrl + πr² where l = slant height = √(r² + h²)

1.3 Spheres

Volume = (4/3)πr³ Surface area = 4πr²

1.4 Worked Example — Cone

Problem: A cone has radius 5 cm and height 12 cm. Find its volume, slant height, and total surface area.

Solution:

Slant height l = √(5² + 12²) = √(25 + 144) = √169 = 13 cm

Volume = (1/3)π × 25 × 12 = 100π ≈ 314 cm³

Curved SA = π × 5 × 13 = 65π cm²

Total SA = 65π + 25π = 90π ≈ 283 cm²

1.5 Frustums

A frustum is the portion of a cone or pyramid remaining after the top is cut off by a plane parallel to the base.

Volume of frustum = (1/3)πh(R² + Rr + r²) Curved SA = πl(R + r) where R = lower radius, r = upper radius, l = slant height of frustum

1.6 Similar Solids

If scale factor of lengths is k:

Area ratio = k² Volume ratio = k³

1.7 3D Pythagoras and Trigonometry

For a cuboid with sides a, b, c:

Space diagonal = √(a² + b² + c²)

Angle between space diagonal and base: tan θ = c/√(a²+b²)

📗 AS Level Content

2.1 Angle Between Line and Plane

The angle between a line and a plane is the angle between the line and its projection onto the plane.

sin θ = (perpendicular from point on line to plane) / (length of line segment)

Worked Example: A line from point A to point B goes from ground level to the top of a 6 m wall. The horizontal distance is 8 m. Find the angle of elevation.

tan θ = 6/8 = 0.75, so θ = tan⁻¹(0.75) ≈ 36.9°

2.2 Angle Between Two Planes

To find the angle between two planes, find the angle between their normal vectors, or construct a line in each plane perpendicular to the line of intersection.

2.3 Coordinate Geometry in 3D

Distance between (x₁,y₁,z₁) and (x₂,y₂,z₂): d = √[(x₂−x₁)² + (y₂−y₁)² + (z₂−z₁)²] Midpoint: ((x₁+x₂)/2, (y₁+y₂)/2, (z₁+z₂)/2)

2.4 Section Formula in 3D

Point dividing AB in ratio m:n: ((nx₁+mx₂)/(m+n), (ny₁+my₂)/(m+n), (nz₁+mz₂)/(m+n))

2.5 Equation of a Plane

ax + by + cz = d Normal vector: (a, b, c)

📙 A2 Level Content

3.1 Volume by Integration

For a solid of revolution about the x-axis:

V = π ∫[a to b] y² dx

For rotation about the y-axis:

V = π ∫[c to d] x² dy

Worked Example: Find volume when y = √x from x = 0 to x = 4 is rotated about x-axis.

V = π ∫[0 to 4] x dx = π [x²/2][0 to 4] = π × 8 = 8π

3.2 Surface Area by Integration

S = 2π ∫[a to b] y √(1 + (dy/dx)²) dx

This gives the surface area of a solid of revolution about the x-axis.

3.3 Spherical Coordinates

x = r sin θ cos φ y = r sin θ sin φ z = r cos θ

where r = radius, θ = polar angle from z-axis, φ = azimuthal angle in xy-plane.

Volume element: dV = r² sin θ dr dθ dφ

3.4 Shortest Path on a Sphere

The shortest path between two points on a sphere is along a great circle (a circle with centre at the sphere's centre).

For a sphere of radius R, the great circle distance between points with central angle θ (in radians) is:

Arc length = Rθ

Using latitudes φ₁, φ₂ and longitude difference Δλ:

cos θ = sin φ₁ sin φ₂ + cos φ₁ cos φ₂ cos Δλ

3.5 Higher-Dimensional Geometry

An n-dimensional hypercube has:

2ⁿ vertices n × 2ⁿ⁻¹ edges ...

These concepts appear in linear algebra, computer science (hypercubes as network topologies), and theoretical physics.

📐 Worked Example

Question: Find the interior angle of a regular decagon.

Solution:
n = 10
Interior angle = (n − 2) × 180° / n
= (10 − 2) × 180° / 10 = 8 × 18° = 144°
Answer: 144°
Confusing interior and exterior angles. Exterior angles of ANY polygon always sum to 360°.
For regular polygons: interior + exterior = 180°. Use this to check your answers.

⚡ Quick Recall

  • Interior angle sum = (n − 2) × 180°
  • Exterior angle sum = 360°
  • Regular polygon interior = (n−2)×180°/n
