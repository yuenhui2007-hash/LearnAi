Thermal Physics

9702 (A-Level) · 0625 (IGCSE)

Last-Minute Revision Summary

  • T(K) = T(degC) + 273; Absolute zero = 0 K = -273 degC -- minimum possible temperature
  • Ideal gas: pV = nRT = NkT; R = 8.31 J/(mol K), k = 1.38 x 10^-23 J/K
  • Kinetic theory: pV = (1/3) N m c^2_bar; average Ek = (3/2) kT -- depends ONLY on temperature
  • c_rms = sqrt(3kT/m) = sqrt(3RT/M) -- lighter molecules move faster at same T
  • Specific heat: Q = mc delta T; Specific latent heat: Q = mL
  • During phase change, temperature constant -- energy breaks bonds, does not increase KE
  • 1st Law: delta U = Q - W (increase in internal energy = heat in - work out by system)
  • Internal energy of ideal gas depends only on temperature

Key Formulas

pV = nRT
Ek = (3/2) kT
Q = mc delta T
Q = mL
delta U = Q - W

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Forgetting to convert degC to K for gas law calculations
  • Including delta T during phase change when temperature is constant
  • Confusing specific heat capacity with specific latent heat
  • Using wrong sign convention in first law (check whether W is work done BY or ON system)

Quick Exam Tips

  • Always convert Celsius to Kelvin before using ideal gas equation
  • For gas law: pV/T = constant (fixed amount of gas)
  • In calorimetry: heat lost by hot = heat gained by cold (unless stated otherwise)
  • During phase change, temperature is constant -- do not include delta T for phase-changing substance
  • Check whether question asks for specific heat capacity or specific latent heat
  • In first law problems, be clear about sign convention for work done
