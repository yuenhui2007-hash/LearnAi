The Accounting Equation

0452 / 9706 — Foundation of Financial Accounting

IGCSE Content

Core Principle

The accounting equation states that a business's assets are financed by either liabilities (owed to outsiders) or capital (owner's equity). Every transaction affects at least two items in the equation — this is the basis of double-entry bookkeeping.

Assets = Capital + Liabilities
Or: Assets − Liabilities = Capital (Owner's Equity)

Definitions

  • Assets: Resources owned/controlled by the business expected to provide future economic benefit. Includes cash, inventory, receivables, equipment, buildings, vehicles.
  • Liabilities: Amounts owed by the business to outsiders (creditors, loans, payables). Includes trade payables, bank loans, accruals.
  • Capital (Owner's Equity): The owner's claim on the business assets. It increases with profits and additional investments; decreases with drawings and losses.

Effect of Transactions on the Equation

Every transaction has a dual effect. Common scenarios:

  • Owner invests cash: Assets ↑ (Cash), Capital ↑
  • Purchase inventory on credit: Assets ↑ (Inventory), Liabilities ↑ (Payables)
  • Pay a creditor by cheque: Assets ↓ (Bank), Liabilities ↓ (Payables)
  • Owner withdraws cash for personal use: Assets ↓ (Cash), Capital ↓ (Drawings)
  • Buy equipment for cash: Assets ↑ (Equipment), Assets ↓ (Cash) — no net change in total assets

Worked Example

Sam starts business with $10,000 cash, buys inventory $4,000 on credit, and purchases a computer $2,000 cash:

After all transactions:
Assets: Cash $8,000 + Inventory $4,000 + Computer $2,000 = $14,000
Liabilities: Trade Payables $4,000
Capital: $10,000
Check: $14,000 = $10,000 + $4,000 ✓

AS Level Content

Expanded Accounting Equation

At AS Level, the equation is expanded to incorporate profit/loss:

Assets = Capital + (Profit − Loss) + Liabilities
Or more fully:
Closing Capital = Opening Capital + Capital Introduced + Profit − Drawings
Where Profit = Revenue − Expenses

Relationship to Financial Statements

The accounting equation is the structural basis of the Statement of Financial Position (Balance Sheet):

  • Assets are listed in order of liquidity (non-current first, then current)
  • Liabilities split into current (due within 12 months) and non-current
  • The balancing figure is always capital/equity

Capital vs Revenue Transactions

  • Capital expenditure: Purchase of non-current assets, improvements that extend useful life → increases assets, affects future periods
  • Revenue expenditure: Day-to-day running costs, repairs and maintenance → treated as expense in current period
  • Capital income: Sale of non-current assets, owner investments
  • Revenue income: Sales revenue, commission received, rent received

A2 Level Content

Limitations of the Historical Cost Concept

A2 explores weaknesses in the accounting equation when assets are held at historical cost:

  • Assets may be undervalued if market prices have risen (e.g., property)
  • During inflation, monetary assets (cash, receivables) lose purchasing power
  • The equation doesn't reflect intangible value (goodwill, brand value)

Valuation Adjustments

  • Revaluation of non-current assets: Carrying amount increased to fair value; surplus credited to revaluation reserve (part of equity)
  • Impairment losses: When recoverable amount falls below carrying amount, write-down reduces profit and assets
  • Depreciation: Systematic allocation of cost over useful life — reduces asset value and accumulated profits

Last-Minute Summary

  • Accounting Equation: Assets = Capital + Liabilities
  • Dual effect: Every transaction changes at least two items
  • Assets = what the business OWNS; Liabilities = what the business OWES; Capital = owner's claim
  • Expanded equation: Closing Capital = Opening Capital + Capital Introduced + Profit − Drawings
  • Profit = Revenue − Expenses
  • Capital expenditure → non-current assets; Revenue expenditure → expenses in P&L
  • Revaluation surplus → equity (revaluation reserve); Impairment → reduces profit
  • Equation must ALWAYS balance — if it doesn't, there's an error
