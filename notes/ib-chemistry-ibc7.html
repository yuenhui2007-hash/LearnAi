Equilibrium

IB DP Chemistry HL/SL — Topic 7

Overview

Equilibrium (Topic 7) describes reversible reactions where forward and reverse reaction rates are equal. Understanding how equilibrium responds to changes in conditions is fundamental to industrial chemistry and is tested across all papers. Le Chatelier's principle is one of the most frequently examined concepts in IB Chemistry.

7.1 Dynamic Equilibrium

  • At equilibrium, forward and reverse reaction rates are equal.
  • Concentrations of reactants and products remain constant (but not necessarily equal).
  • Equilibrium can only be reached in a closed system.

7.2 The Equilibrium Constant

For a reaction: aA + bB ⇌ cC + dD
K_c = [C]ᶜ[D]ᵈ / [A]ᵃ[B]ᵇ
K_p = (P_C)ᶜ(P_D)ᵈ / (P_A)ᵃ(P_B)ᵇ (for gaseous equilibria)
  • K has no units only if the sum of product stoichiometric coefficients equals the sum of reactant coefficients.
  • K > 1: products favoured; K < 1: reactants favoured.
  • K depends only on temperature.

Worked Example 1: K_c Calculation

Question: For N₂(g) + 3H₂(g) ⇌ 2NH₃(g), equilibrium concentrations are [N₂] = 0.10, [H₂] = 0.30, [NH₃] = 0.20 mol dm⁻³. Calculate K_c.

Solution:

K_c = [NH₃]² / ([N₂][H₂]³) = (0.20)² / ((0.10)(0.30)³) = 0.040 / 0.0027 = 14.8 mol⁻² dm⁶

7.3 ICE Tables

Use ICE (Initial, Change, Equilibrium) to calculate equilibrium concentrations from initial data.

Worked Example 2: ICE Table

Question: For H₂(g) + I₂(g) ⇌ 2HI(g), K_c = 54.3. Initial [H₂] = [I₂] = 1.00 mol dm⁻³. Find equilibrium [HI].

Solution:

Initial: H₂ = 1.00, I₂ = 1.00, HI = 0
Change: H₂ = −x, I₂ = −x, HI = +2x
Equilibrium: H₂ = 1.00 − x, I₂ = 1.00 − x, HI = 2x

K_c = (2x)² / ((1.00 − x)(1.00 − x)) = 54.3
2x / (1.00 − x) = √54.3 = 7.37
2x = 7.37 − 7.37x → 9.37x = 7.37 → x = 0.786
[HI] = 2x = 1.57 mol dm⁻³

7.4 Le Chatelier's Principle

If a dynamic equilibrium is disturbed, the system adjusts to minimise the disturbance.

  • Concentration: Increase reactant → shifts right; increase product → shifts left.
  • Pressure (gases): Increase pressure → shifts towards side with fewer moles of gas.
  • Temperature: Increase T → shifts in endothermic direction; decrease T → shifts in exothermic direction.
  • Catalyst: No effect on position of equilibrium — speeds up both forward and reverse reactions equally.

Common Trap

Adding a catalyst does NOT change K or the equilibrium position — it only reduces the time taken to reach equilibrium.

7.5 Equilibrium & Industry [HL only]

  • Haber process: N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃, ΔH < 0. High pressure favours products; moderate temperature is a compromise between rate and yield.
  • Contact process: 2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃, ΔH < 0. High pressure and moderate temperature with V₂O₅ catalyst.

Common Misconceptions

  • K changes with concentration: False — K only changes with temperature.
  • Adding inert gas at constant volume: No effect on equilibrium — partial pressures/concentrations of reactants and products are unchanged.
  • Equilibrium means equal concentrations: False — equilibrium means constant concentrations, which may or may not be equal.
  • Catalyst: Speeds up attainment of equilibrium but does not change K or the equilibrium position.

Practice Questions

  • Write K_c expressions for: (a) 2SO₂ + O₂ ⇌ 2SO₃, (b) CaCO₃(s) ⇌ CaO(s) + CO₂(g).
  • For N₂O₄(g) ⇌ 2NO₂(g), ΔH > 0. Predict the effect on [NO₂] of: (a) increasing pressure, (b) increasing temperature, (c) adding a catalyst.
  • At equilibrium, [PCl₅] = 0.20, [PCl₃] = 0.050, [Cl₂] = 0.050 mol dm⁻³. Calculate K_c for PCl₅ ⇌ PCl₃ + Cl₂.
  • HL: The Haber process operates at 450°C and 200 atm. Explain why these conditions are a compromise.

Last-Minute Summary

  • K_c = [products]/[reactants] with stoichiometric powers.
  • At equilibrium: forward rate = reverse rate; concentrations constant.
  • Le Chatelier: system opposes the change (concentration, pressure, temperature).
  • Catalyst: no effect on K or equilibrium position.
  • K only changes with temperature.

Exam Tips

  • Read each question carefully and identify the command term before answering.
  • Show all working with units — method marks are generous in IB Chemistry.
  • Give final answers to 2–3 significant figures unless specified otherwise.
  • In "explain" questions, use Le Chatelier's principle explicitly.
  • Pure solids and liquids are omitted from K expressions.
