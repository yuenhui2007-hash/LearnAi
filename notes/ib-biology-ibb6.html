Human Physiology

IB DP Biology HL/SL — Topic 6

Overview

Human Physiology (Topic 6) covers the structure and function of human body systems. This is a content-heavy topic with many diagrams to learn. It is tested across all papers and connects to the IA through physiology experiments (e.g., heart rate, lung capacity).

6.1 Digestion

  • Mouth: mechanical digestion (chewing) + salivary amylase (starch → maltose).
  • Stomach: pepsin (protein → peptides) + HCl (pH 1–2).
  • Small intestine: pancreatic enzymes (amylase, lipase, proteases) + intestinal enzymes; villi increase surface area for absorption.
  • Large intestine: water absorption; bacteria produce vitamins (K, B).

6.2 Gas Exchange

  • Alveoli: thin walls (one cell thick), large surface area, moist, rich blood supply → efficient gas exchange.
  • Oxygen diffuses into blood; CO₂ diffuses out.
  • Haemoglobin: binds O₂ in lungs, releases O₂ in tissues.
Oxygen dissociation curve: sigmoidal shape; shifted right by increased CO₂, decreased pH, increased temperature (Bohr effect) → more O₂ released to active tissues.

6.3 Transport System

  • Heart: double pump — right side to lungs, left side to body.
  • Blood vessels: arteries (thick muscular walls, carry blood away), veins (valves, thin walls, carry blood to heart), capillaries (one cell thick, exchange).
  • Blood composition: plasma (55%), red blood cells (transport O₂), white blood cells (immunity), platelets (clotting).

6.4 Immune System

  • Pathogen: organism that causes disease.
  • Antigen: foreign molecule that triggers an immune response.
  • Antibody: protein produced by plasma cells that binds to a specific antigen.

Specific Immunity

  • Humoral: B cells differentiate into plasma cells → produce antibodies.
  • Cell-mediated: T cells kill infected host cells (helper T cells coordinate, cytotoxic T cells destroy).
  • Memory cells: provide faster, stronger secondary response.

6.5 Nervous System

  • Neuron structure: dendrites (receive signals), cell body, axon (transmits signals), myelin sheath (speeds conduction), synapse (gap between neurons).
  • Resting potential: −70 mV (Na⁺ outside, K⁺ inside).
  • Action potential: depolarisation (Na⁺ influx) → repolarisation (K⁺ efflux) → hyperpolarisation → restoration.
  • Synaptic transmission: neurotransmitter released from presynaptic membrane → diffuses across synaptic cleft → binds to receptors on postsynaptic membrane.

6.6 Hormones & Reproduction

  • Endocrine system: hormones travel in blood to target cells with specific receptors.
  • Insulin: lowers blood glucose (promotes uptake and storage).
  • Glucagon: raises blood glucose (promotes glycogenolysis and gluconeogenesis).
  • Menstrual cycle: FSH (follicle development), LH (ovulation), oestrogen (endometrium thickening), progesterone (maintains endometrium).

Common Misconceptions

  • Arteries always carry oxygenated blood: False — pulmonary arteries carry deoxygenated blood.
  • Antibiotics kill viruses: False — antibiotics target bacteria; antivirals target viruses.
  • Action potential: It is an all-or-nothing event — it does not vary in strength, only in frequency.
  • Hormones vs. nerves: Hormones are slower, longer-lasting, and act on multiple targets; nerves are fast and specific.

Exam-Specific Information

  • Paper 1: 5–6 MCQs on body systems, immunity, and hormone functions.
  • Paper 2: Structured questions on digestion, gas exchange, heart structure, immunity, and the nervous system.
  • Paper 3: Data analysis of heart rate, breathing rate, or blood glucose experiments.
  • IA: Heart rate and exercise, lung capacity, blood glucose, and reaction time experiments are popular.

Practice Questions

  • Explain how the structure of the small intestine is adapted for absorption.
  • Describe the role of haemoglobin in oxygen transport and explain the Bohr effect.
  • Outline the events of the cell-mediated immune response.
  • Explain how insulin and glucagon work together to maintain blood glucose homeostasis.

Last-Minute Summary

  • Digestion: mouth (amylase), stomach (pepsin), small intestine (pancreatic + intestinal enzymes).
  • Gas exchange: alveoli structure → efficient diffusion; Bohr effect shifts O₂ dissociation curve right.
  • Heart: double pump; arteries away, veins to heart, capillaries exchange.
  • Immunity: humoral (B cells/antibodies), cell-mediated (T cells), memory cells for secondary response.
  • Nervous system: resting potential, action potential (Na⁺/K⁺), synaptic transmission (neurotransmitters).
  • Hormones: insulin lowers glucose; glucagon raises glucose; feedback loops maintain homeostasis.

Exam Tips

  • Read each question carefully and identify the command term before answering.
  • Use correct physiological terminology (e.g., "alveoli," "action potential," "synaptic cleft").
  • Draw and label diagrams clearly — heart structure, neuron, and action potential graphs are common.
  • In "explain" questions, link structure to function.
  • For hormone questions, always mention the target organ and feedback mechanism.
