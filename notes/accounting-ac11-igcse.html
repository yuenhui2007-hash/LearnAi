📘 Subject Context

Company Accounts forms the foundation of IGCSE Accounting. Accuracy, neatness, and understanding of double-entry principles are essential for success in both theoretical and practical examination questions.

IGCSE Accounting tests both knowledge and practical application. You must be able to prepare ledger accounts, trial balances, financial statements, and interpret accounting information. Neat, well-presented work earns presentation marks.

Company Accounts

0452 (IGCSE)

Detailed Notes

1. Manufacturing Accounts

  • Direct materials, direct labour, factory overheads

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Debiting and crediting the wrong accounts — always ask: is this account receiving (debit) or giving (credit) value?
  • Incorrectly calculating cost of sales — remember: opening inventory + purchases – closing inventory.
  • Making errors in the adjustment for depreciation — remember to adjust both the asset and the provision for depreciation.
  • Forgetting to balance off ledger accounts at the end of the period.
  • Confusing capital expenditure (asset) with revenue expenditure (expense) — this affects profit and asset values.

✅ Key Points to Remember

  • Understand the purpose and preparation of control accounts for receivables and payables.
  • Know the difference between capital and revenue expenditure and receipts.
  • Be able to reconcile the bank statement with the cash book — understand standing orders, direct debits, dishonoured cheques.
  • Know the difference between trade discount (deducted before recording) and cash discount (recorded as an expense/income).
  • Know how to calculate and account for different depreciation methods: straight-line and reducing balance.

Double-Entry Principles

Every transaction affects at least two accounts. The fundamental rule is:

  • Debit: The account that receives value (assets increase, expenses increase, drawings)
  • Credit: The account that gives value (liabilities increase, income increases, capital)

Worked Example 2: Depreciation Calculation

Problem

A machine costs $50,000 and has an estimated useful life of 5 years with a residual value of $5,000. Calculate the annual depreciation using (a) straight-line method and (b) reducing balance method at 30%.

Solution

(a) Straight-line: (50,000 − 5,000) / 5 = $9,000 per year

(b) Reducing balance:

Year 1: 50,000 × 30% = $15,000; NBV = $35,000

Year 2: 35,000 × 30% = $10,500; NBV = $24,500

Year 3: 24,500 × 30% = $7,350; NBV = $17,150

Worked Example 3: Bank Reconciliation

Problem

The cash book shows a debit balance of $8,500. Bank statement shows $9,200. Outstanding lodgements $1,500; unpresented cheques $2,200; bank charges $200 not recorded in cash book.

Solution

Adjusted cash book balance = 8,500 − 200 = $8,300

Bank reconciliation:

Balance per bank statement: $9,200

Add: Outstanding lodgements: $1,500

Less: Unpresented cheques: ($2,200)

Adjusted bank balance: $8,500

Note: There appears to be a discrepancy of $200 — this requires further investigation (could be an error in recording).

Practice Questions

Question 1

A business has the following information for the year: Opening inventory $8,000, Purchases $45,000, Closing inventory $10,000, Sales $72,000. Calculate cost of sales, gross profit, and gross profit margin.

Answer: Cost of sales = $43,000; Gross profit = $29,000; Gross profit margin = 40.3%

Question 2

Explain the difference between capital expenditure and revenue expenditure, giving two examples of each.

Answer: Capital expenditure: purchase of non-current assets, improvements that extend useful life (e.g., buying machinery, building extension). Revenue expenditure: day-to-day running costs, repairs and maintenance (e.g., wages, rent, repairs).

Question 3

A trader's trial balance does not balance. The debit side totals $52,300 and the credit side totals $51,800. Suggest three types of error that could cause this and explain how each would be corrected.

Answer: (1) Transposition error — digits reversed in one entry. (2) Omission of one side of a double entry. (3) Entry on wrong side — debit recorded as credit or vice versa. Each would be corrected through a journal entry to the suspense account.

🎯 Examination Tips

  • Present your work neatly — use rulers, label all statements clearly, and show all workings.
  • Use the correct terminology — "statement of financial position", "income statement", "equity".
  • Always show your calculations — method marks are awarded even if the final figure is incorrect.
  • Manage your time carefully — longer questions are worth more marks and need adequate time.
  • For ratio analysis, state the formula, show the calculation, and interpret the result in context.
