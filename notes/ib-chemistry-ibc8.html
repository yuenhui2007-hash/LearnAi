Acids & Bases

IB DP Chemistry HL/SL — Topic 8

Overview

Acids and Bases (Topic 8) covers the Brønsted–Lowry theory, pH calculations, buffers, and titrations. This topic is heavily calculation-based and appears across all papers. Titration experiments are among the most popular IA topics.

8.1 Brønsted–Lowry Theory

  • Acid: proton (H⁺) donor.
  • Base: proton (H⁺) acceptor.
  • Conjugate acid–base pair: differ by one H⁺ (e.g., NH₄⁺/NH₃, H₂O/OH⁻).
HA + B ⇌ A⁻ + HB⁺
Acid + Base ⇌ Conjugate base + Conjugate acid

8.2 pH and pOH

pH = −log[H⁺]
pOH = −log[OH⁻]
pH + pOH = 14.00 (at 25°C)
[H⁺] = 10^(−pH)
K_w = [H⁺][OH⁻] = 1.0 × 10⁻¹⁴ mol² dm⁻⁶ (at 25°C)

Worked Example 1: pH Calculation

Question: Calculate the pH of 0.050 mol dm⁻³ HCl.

Solution:

HCl is strong → [H⁺] = 0.050 mol dm⁻³
pH = −log(0.050) = 1.30

8.3 Strong and Weak Acids/Bases

  • Strong acid: completely dissociates (e.g., HCl, HNO₃, H₂SO₄).
  • Weak acid: partially dissociates (e.g., CH₃COOH, H₂CO₃).
  • Strong base: completely dissociates (e.g., NaOH, KOH).
  • Weak base: partially dissociates (e.g., NH₃).

Acid Dissociation Constant (Kₐ)

Kₐ = [H⁺][A⁻] / [HA]
pKₐ = −log Kₐ
Lower pKₐ → stronger acid

Worked Example 2: Weak Acid pH

Question: Calculate the pH of 0.10 mol dm⁻³ CH₃COOH (Kₐ = 1.8 × 10⁻⁵).

Solution:

Kₐ = [H⁺]² / [HA] (assuming [H⁺] << [HA])
[H⁺] = √(Kₐ × [HA]) = √(1.8 × 10⁻⁵ × 0.10) = 1.34 × 10⁻³ mol dm⁻³
pH = −log(1.34 × 10⁻³) = 2.87

8.4 Titrations

  • Equivalence point: moles of acid = moles of base.
  • End point: indicator changes colour (should match equivalence point).

Indicators

IndicatorpH rangeAcid colourBase colour
Methyl orange3.1–4.4RedYellow
Bromothymol blue6.0–7.6YellowBlue
Phenolphthalein8.2–10.0ColourlessPink

Titration Curves

  • Strong acid + strong base: equivalence at pH 7; any indicator works.
  • Weak acid + strong base: equivalence at pH > 7; phenolphthalein suitable.
  • Strong acid + weak base: equivalence at pH < 7; methyl orange suitable.

8.5 Buffers [HL only]

A buffer resists pH change when small amounts of acid or base are added.

Acidic buffer: weak acid + its conjugate base salt (e.g., CH₃COOH + CH₃COONa)
Basic buffer: weak base + its conjugate acid salt (e.g., NH₃ + NH₄Cl)

Henderson–Hasselbalch equation:
pH = pKₐ + log([A⁻]/[HA])

Common Misconceptions

  • pH scale: A change of 1 pH unit means a 10-fold change in [H⁺].
  • Strong vs. concentrated: "Strong" refers to degree of dissociation; "concentrated" refers to amount per volume. A dilute strong acid can have higher pH than a concentrated weak acid.
  • Equivalence point pH: Not always 7 — depends on the acid and base strengths.
  • Water autoionisation: In pure water, [H⁺] = [OH⁻] = 1.0 × 10⁻⁷ mol dm⁻³ at 25°C.

Practice Questions

  • Calculate the pH of 0.010 mol dm⁻³ NaOH.
  • Sketch the titration curve for 25.0 cm³ of 0.10 mol dm⁻³ CH₃COOH titrated with 0.10 mol dm⁻³ NaOH. Label the equivalence point and suggest a suitable indicator.
  • Calculate the pH of a buffer containing 0.10 mol dm⁻³ CH₃COOH and 0.20 mol dm⁻³ CH₃COONa (pKₐ = 4.76).
  • Explain why the pH at the equivalence point of a weak acid + strong base titration is greater than 7.

Last-Minute Summary

  • Acid = proton donor; Base = proton acceptor.
  • pH = −log[H⁺]; pOH = −log[OH⁻]; pH + pOH = 14 (at 25°C).
  • Strong acids/bases dissociate completely; weak acids/bases partially dissociate.
  • Kₐ measures acid strength; pKₐ = −log Kₐ.
  • Indicator choice depends on equivalence point pH.
  • HL: Buffers contain weak acid + conjugate base; pH = pKₐ + log([A⁻]/[HA]).

Exam Tips

  • Read each question carefully and identify the command term before answering.
  • Show all working with units — method marks are generous in IB Chemistry.
  • Give final answers to 2–3 significant figures unless specified otherwise.
  • In "explain" questions, refer to dissociation equilibrium and Le Chatelier's principle.
  • For titration curves, label axes, equivalence point, and buffer region clearly.
