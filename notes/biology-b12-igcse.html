📘 Subject Context

Understanding Inheritance is fundamental to IGCSE Biology. This topic frequently appears in both Paper 2 (Multiple Choice) and Paper 4 (Theory), so mastering the details is essential for achieving a high grade.

In IGCSE Biology examinations, you may be asked to recall facts, explain processes, interpret diagrams, or apply knowledge to unfamiliar contexts. Always use precise biological terminology — marks are awarded for specific key words.

Inheritance

0610 (IGCSE)

IGCSE Content

Key Terms

  • Gene: A length of DNA that codes for a protein.
  • Allele: Alternative form of a gene.
  • Homozygous: Two identical alleles (TT or tt).
  • Heterozygous: Two different alleles (Tt).
  • Dominant: Allele expressed in heterozygote (T).
  • Recessive: Allele only expressed in homozygote (t).
  • Genotype: Genetic makeup (alleles present).
  • Phenotype: Observable characteristic.

Monohybrid Crosses

Cross involving one gene. Use Punnett squares to predict offspring ratios.

  • Homozygous dominant × homozygous recessive → all heterozygous (F1).
  • F1 self-cross → 3:1 phenotypic ratio (dominant:recessive).
  • F1 cross back to recessive parent (test cross) → 1:1 ratio.

Co-dominance & Incomplete Dominance

  • Co-dominance: Both alleles expressed in heterozygote. Example: ABO blood group (IA and IB are co-dominant; both expressed as AB).
  • Incomplete dominance: Heterozygote shows intermediate phenotype. Example: snapdragon flower colour (red × white → pink).

Sex Determination

  • Humans: XX = female, XY = male.
  • Male gametes determine sex (X or Y sperm).
  • Sex-linked genes are carried on sex chromosomes (especially X).

Genetic Diagrams

Always show: parental phenotypes, parental genotypes, gametes, offspring genotypes, offspring phenotypes, and ratios. Use clear genetic symbols.

AS Level Content

Dihybrid Crosses

Cross involving two genes. Assuming independent assortment, phenotypic ratio in F2 = 9:3:3:1.

  • Only applies if genes are on different chromosomes (or far apart on same chromosome).
  • Test cross of dihybrid (heterozygous for both) × double recessive gives 1:1:1:1 ratio.

Chi-Squared Test

χ² = Σ [(O - E)² / E]
Degrees of freedom = number of classes - 1
  • Used to compare observed results with expected ratios.
  • Calculate χ² value, compare to critical value at p=0.05.
  • If χ² < critical value → no significant difference; accept null hypothesis (results fit expected ratio).
  • If χ² > critical value → significant difference; reject null hypothesis.

Linkage

  • Genes on same chromosome tend to be inherited together.
  • Recombination frequency indicates map distance (1% recombination = 1 map unit/centimorgan).
  • Crossing over during prophase I separates linked genes; closer genes have lower recombination frequency.

Epistasis

  • Interaction between different genes where one masks the expression of another.
  • Dominant epistasis: A masks B/b. Example: summer squash colour (A_ = white regardless of B).
  • Recessive epistasis: aa masks B/b. Example: coat colour in Labradors (bb = brown; ee = yellow regardless of B).
  • Ratios modified from 9:3:3:1 (e.g., 12:3:1 or 9:3:4).

Multiple Alleles & ABO Blood Group

  • Three alleles: IA, IB, IO. IA and IB co-dominant; both dominant to IO.
  • Phenotypes: A (IAIA or IAIO), B (IBIB or IBIO), AB (IAIB), O (IOIO).

A2 Level Content

Continuous & Discontinuous Variation

  • Discontinuous: Distinct categories; controlled by one/few genes; little environmental influence. Example: blood group, attached/unattached earlobes.
  • Continuous: Range of values; controlled by many genes (polygenic) + environment. Example: height, skin colour, yield in crops.
  • Polygenic inheritance shows normal distribution. Environmental factors shift the distribution.

Hardy-Weinberg Equilibrium

p + q = 1
p² + 2pq + q² = 1
p = frequency of dominant allele
q = frequency of recessive allele
p² = homozygous dominant frequency
2pq = heterozygous frequency
q² = homozygous recessive frequency
  • Applies to large, randomly mating populations with no mutation, selection, migration, or genetic drift.
  • Useful for calculating carrier frequency (2pq) for recessive genetic diseases.
  • If assumptions are violated, allele frequencies change → evolution.

Genetic Diseases

  • Autosomal dominant: Huntington's disease. Affected individual has affected parent. Homozygous dominant often lethal.
  • Autosomal recessive: Cystic fibrosis, sickle cell anaemia, albinism. Carriers (heterozygotes) unaffected. Both parents must be carriers for affected offspring (25% risk).
  • X-linked recessive: Haemophilia, Duchenne muscular dystrophy, red-green colour blindness. Males more affected (XY). Females carriers (XHXh).
  • X-linked dominant: Rare. Affected father passes to all daughters, none to sons.

Gene Technology & Screening

  • Genetic counselling: Predicting risks using family pedigrees.
  • Pre-natal screening: Amniocentesis (amniotic fluid, 15-20 weeks), chorionic villus sampling (placenta, 10-12 weeks), non-invasive prenatal testing (NIPT, fetal DNA in maternal blood).
  • Embryo screening (PGD): IVF embryos tested for genetic disorders before implantation.
  • Ethical considerations: privacy, discrimination, abortion, designer babies.

🔬 Worked Example

Question: Explain how the structure of the small intestine is adapted for absorption.

Answer:
1. Villi: Finger-like projections increase surface area
2. Microvilli: Further increase surface area on epithelial cells
3. Thin walls: Single cell layer reduces diffusion distance
4. Rich blood supply: Maintains concentration gradient
5. Lacteals: Absorb fatty acids and glycerol
Confusing absorption (taking nutrients into blood) with assimilation (incorporating nutrients into cells/tissues).
Surface area to volume ratio is crucial — as organisms get larger, SA:V decreases, requiring specialised exchange surfaces.

⚡ Quick Recall

  • Photosynthesis: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂
  • Enzymes: Biological catalysts; specific active site
  • DNA replication: Semi-conservative
  • Natural selection: Variation → selection → inheritance

Last-Minute Summary

Key Points

  • Monohybrid: 3:1 (F2), 1:1 (test cross). Dihybrid: 9:3:3:1 (F2), 1:1:1:1 (test cross).
  • Co-dominance: both alleles expressed. Incomplete dominance: intermediate phenotype.
  • Linkage: genes on same chromosome inherited together; recombination frequency ∝ map distance.
  • Epistasis: one gene masks another; modifies dihybrid ratios (12:3:1, 9:3:4, etc.).
  • χ² test: compare observed to expected; df = classes - 1; p = 0.05.
  • Hardy-Weinberg: p² + 2pq + q² = 1. Use to find carrier frequencies.
  • Autosomal dominant, autosomal recessive, X-linked recessive inheritance patterns — recognise from pedigrees.
  • Continuous variation = polygenic + environment; discontinuous = few genes.

Exam tip: In genetic diagrams, always clearly show all steps: parental phenotypes, genotypes, gametes, Punnett square, offspring genotypes and phenotypes, and ratios. For χ², show working: (O-E)²/E for each class. In pedigree analysis, trace carriers carefully — unaffected parents with affected child = recessive; affected individuals in every generation = dominant; more males affected = X-linked recessive.

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Using "growth" instead of "cell division" when describing mitosis — mitosis produces two genetically identical daughter cells.
  • Confusing the terms "absorption" and "assimilation" — absorption is taking nutrients into the blood; assimilation is incorporating them into cells.
  • Mixing up the sites of photosynthesis and respiration — photosynthesis occurs in chloroplasts; aerobic respiration in mitochondria.
  • Forgetting to mention the role of ATP in active processes — always check if energy is required.
  • Forgetting that bacteria are prokaryotes and lack a true nucleus and membrane-bound organelles.

✅ Key Points to Remember

  • Know the stages of the nitrogen cycle and carbon cycle with specific organisms involved.
  • Memorise the balanced equation for photosynthesis and aerobic respiration.
  • Understand the difference between aerobic and anaerobic respiration in terms of products and energy yield.
  • Use precise terminology — examiners award marks for specific words like "denatured", "partially permeable", and "semi-conservative".
  • Understand the concept of limiting factors and be able to apply it to photosynthesis.

🎯 Examination Tips

  • Plan extended response questions before writing — a brief bullet plan helps structure your answer.
  • In data analysis questions, quote actual figures from the graph or table.
  • When comparing, always mention both items — e.g., "X is... whereas Y is...".
  • For "explain" questions, always give a reason (use "because" or "this means that").
  • Read the question carefully — command words like "state", "describe", "explain", and "compare" require different answer structures.
