📘 Subject Context

Business Objectives is a key area in IGCSE Business Studies. Understanding this topic enables you to analyse business situations, evaluate decisions, and apply business theory to real-world case studies.

IGCSE Business Studies tests knowledge, application, analysis, and evaluation. Paper 1 (Short Answer) tests recall and simple application; Paper 2 (Case Study) tests deeper analysis and evaluation of a given business scenario.

⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Giving generic answers without applying them to the business in the case study — always contextualise.
  • Confusing internal recruitment (promoting existing staff) with external recruitment (hiring from outside).
  • Using "market research" as a solution to every problem — be specific about primary vs secondary methods.
  • Confusing a mission statement (purpose) with an aim (long-term goal) with an objective (measurable target).
  • Forgetting to mention both advantages and disadvantages in evaluation questions.

✅ Key Points to Remember

  • Know the four functions of management: planning, organising, leading, controlling.
  • Learn the stages of the product life cycle and appropriate strategies at each stage.
  • Always apply theory to the case study business — use the business name and specific details.
  • Know the factors that influence the choice of business location and method of production.
  • Be able to calculate and interpret financial ratios: gross profit margin, net profit margin, ROCE, current ratio, acid test ratio.

Business Application: bu4

Business Studies requires you to apply theoretical knowledge to real business scenarios. The case study in Paper 2 is the most challenging aspect, requiring analysis, application, and evaluation.

Case Study Strategy
  1. Read the case carefully: Underline key facts about the business, its products, market, and problems.
  2. Identify stakeholders: Consider how decisions affect employees, customers, shareholders, and the community.
  3. Apply theory: Use business concepts (marketing mix, leadership styles, sources of finance) in context.
  4. Evaluate: Give advantages AND disadvantages before making a recommendation.
Application Formula

For maximum marks in application questions, use this structure:

Point + Business Context + Because + This means that + Therefore

Example: "The business should use social media marketing [point] because its target market is teenagers [context]. This means that Instagram and TikTok would reach the target audience effectively. Therefore, sales are likely to increase among this demographic."

Always use the business name from the case study and refer to specific details. Generic answers that could apply to any business score poorly on application marks.

Worked Example 1: Calculating Profit Margins

Problem

A business has revenue of $250,000, cost of sales of $150,000, and operating expenses of $60,000. Calculate the gross profit margin and net profit margin.

Solution

Gross profit = 250,000 − 150,000 = $100,000

Gross profit margin = 100,000 / 250,000 × 100 = 40%

Net profit = 100,000 − 60,000 = $40,000

Net profit margin = 40,000 / 250,000 × 100 = 16%

Worked Example 2: Break-Even Analysis

Problem

A business sells products at $20 each. Fixed costs are $10,000 per month and variable costs are $12 per unit. Calculate the break-even point in units and revenue.

Solution

Contribution per unit = 20 − 12 = $8

Break-even units = 10,000 / 8 = 1,250 units

Break-even revenue = 1,250 × 20 = $25,000

Worked Example 3: Cash Flow Forecast

Problem

A business forecasts the following monthly figures: opening balance $5,000, cash inflows $8,000, cash outflows $9,500. Calculate the closing balance and explain whether the business has a cash flow problem.

Solution

Closing balance = 5,000 + 8,000 − 9,500 = $3,500

The closing balance is positive but decreasing. If this trend continues, the business may face a cash flow problem. Solutions: reduce credit terms, delay payments to suppliers, arrange an overdraft, or reduce expenses.

Practice Questions

Question 1

A retailer buys goods for $40 and applies a markup of 50%. Calculate the selling price and the profit margin.

Answer: Selling price = $60; Profit margin = 33.3%

Question 2

Explain two advantages and two disadvantages of operating as a private limited company rather than a sole trader.

Answer: Advantages: limited liability, easier to raise capital, continuity, more credibility. Disadvantages: more legal formalities, less control for original owner, financial information is public, may be slower decision-making.

Question 3

A business is considering expanding into a new overseas market. Evaluate this decision using the Ansoff matrix and stakeholder analysis.

Answer: This is market development (existing product, new market). Benefits: increased revenue, diversification. Risks: cultural differences, exchange rate fluctuations, competition. Stakeholders: shareholders (potential profit), employees (job security), local community (employment vs environmental impact).

🎯 Examination Tips

  • Use business terminology accurately — e.g., "diseconomies of scale", "cash flow forecast", "market segmentation".
  • For "analyse" questions, use the formula: point → because → this means that → therefore.
  • Always apply your answer to the case study — mention the business name and specific circumstances.
  • For "evaluate" questions, give at least two advantages and two disadvantages before making a judgement.
  • Write in paragraphs, not bullet points, for 4+ mark questions unless instructed otherwise.
