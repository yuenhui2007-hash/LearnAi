Cell Structure & Organisation

Syllabus reference: Cambridge 9700 / 0610 — Topic 1

Light Microscope

The light microscope uses visible light and glass lenses to magnify specimens. Maximum resolution ≈ 200 nm.

  • Resolution — minimum distance between two points that can be distinguished as separate
  • Magnification — size of image / size of object

Eukaryotic Cell Structure

  • Nucleus — contains chromosomes (DNA), controls cell activities
  • Mitochondria — site of aerobic respiration, produces ATP
  • Rough endoplasmic reticulum (RER) — has ribosomes, synthesises proteins
  • Smooth endoplasmic reticulum (SER) — synthesises lipids and steroids
  • Golgi apparatus — modifies, packages, and transports proteins
  • Ribosomes — site of protein synthesis (translation)
  • Lysosomes — contain hydrolytic enzymes for intracellular digestion
  • Cell surface membrane — phospholipid bilayer controlling entry/exit
  • Cell wall — cellulose in plants, chitin in fungi, peptidoglycan in bacteria
  • Chloroplasts — site of photosynthesis (plants only)
  • Permanent vacuole — maintains turgor pressure (plants only)
  • Centrioles — organise spindle fibres during cell division (animal cells only)

Exam Tips

  • Always state the function alongside the structure in answers.
  • Know how to calculate magnification from scale bars.
  • Distinguish between plant and animal cells — cell wall and chloroplasts are key.
