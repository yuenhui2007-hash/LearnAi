Bank Reconciliation

0452 / 9706 — Reconciling the Cash Book with the Bank Statement

IGCSE Content

Why Reconcile?

The cash book (bank column) and bank statement rarely agree because:

  • Some transactions appear in one but not the other (timing differences)
  • Errors may exist in either record
  • The bank may record items the business doesn't know about yet

Common Differences

  • Unpresented cheques: Cheques written and recorded in cash book but not yet cashed by payee → Deduct from bank statement balance
  • Uncredited deposits (lodgements): Cash/cheques paid into bank, recorded in cash book, but not yet cleared → Add to bank statement balance
  • Bank charges/interest: Recorded by bank but not yet in cash book → Record in cash book (Dr Bank Charges, Cr Bank)
  • Standing orders/direct debits: Automatic payments by bank → Record in cash book
  • Dishonoured cheques: Customer cheque bounced → Reverse cash book entry (Dr Receivable, Cr Bank)
  • Direct credits: Money received directly into bank → Record in cash book (Dr Bank, Cr Income)
  • Errors: In either cash book or bank statement

Bank Reconciliation Statement Format

Balance per bank statement $X,XXX
Add: Uncredited deposits (lodgements) XXX
Less: Unpresented cheques (XXX)
-----------------------------------------------
Balance per cash book (updated) $X,XXX

OR (starting from cash book):
Balance per cash book $X,XXX
Add: Bank charges/interest not recorded XXX
Add: Direct debits not recorded XXX
Less: Direct credits not recorded (XXX)
-----------------------------------------------
Adjusted cash book balance $X,XXX

AS Level Content

Updated Cash Book First

Best practice: Update the cash book for all items the bank knows about before preparing the reconciliation:

  1. Enter bank charges, interest, direct debits, direct credits, dishonoured cheques into cash book
  2. Correct any errors found in the cash book
  3. Balance the updated cash book
  4. Reconcile the updated balance to the bank statement using only timing differences

Overdraft Reconciliation

When the bank account is overdrawn (credit balance in cash book, debit on bank statement):

  • Overdraft = bank loan (current liability) or negative bank asset
  • Bank statement shows debit balance = business owes the bank
  • Cash book shows credit balance = overdraft
  • Same reconciliation principles apply but watch signs carefully

A2 Level Content

Bank Loans in Financial Statements

Bank overdrafts and loans must be correctly classified:

  • Bank overdraft: Current liability (repayable on demand)
  • Short-term bank loan (≤1 year): Current liability
  • Long-term bank loan (>1 year): Non-current liability
  • Current portion of long-term loan: Split between current and non-current

Errors by the Bank

If the bank has made an error (e.g., wrong amount debited, another customer's transaction on your statement):

  • Do NOT adjust the cash book
  • Report to bank and show as adjustment in reconciliation only
  • Once corrected by bank, no further action needed
