IGCSE AS Level

Current Electricity

9702 (A-Level) · 0625 (IGCSE)

Detailed Notes

1. Electric Current

I = Q/t (rate of flow of charge)

Unit: ampere (A). Conventional current flows from positive to negative.

2. Charge Carriers

  • In metals: free electrons.
  • In electrolytes: positive and negative ions.
  • In semiconductors: electrons and holes.

3. Potential Difference (Voltage)

V = W/Q (energy transferred per unit charge)

Unit: volt (V).

4. Resistance

R = V/I

Unit: ohm (Ω).

5. Ohm's Law

For a metallic conductor at constant temperature: V ∝ I. The V-I graph is a straight line through the origin.

6. Resistivity

R = ρL/A

Where ρ = resistivity, L = length, A = cross-sectional area.

7. Power in Electrical Circuits

P = VI = I²R = V²/R

  • SI units in practice: Prefix usage in scientific and everyday contexts

  • I = Q/t (A)
  • V = W/Q (V)
  • R = V/I (Ω)

  • Resistivity: R = ρL/A

📐 Worked Example

Question: A ball is thrown vertically upwards at 20 m/s. Calculate the maximum height reached. (g = 9.81 m/s²)

Solution:
At max height, v = 0. Using v² = u² + 2as:
0 = 20² + 2(−9.81)s
0 = 400 − 19.62s
s = 400 / 19.62 = 20.4 m
Answer: 20.4 m
Using g = +9.81 m/s² when the object is moving upwards — g always acts downwards, so use −9.81 m/s² for upward motion.
Always draw a diagram and define your positive direction before solving kinematics problems.

⚡ Quick Recall

  • v = u + at (no displacement)
  • s = ut + ½at² (no final velocity)
  • v² = u² + 2as (no time)
  • s = ½(u+v)t (no acceleration)
  • All apply only for constant acceleration
