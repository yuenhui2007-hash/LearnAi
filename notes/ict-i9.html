Impact of ICT on Society

Topic i9 — Social, Economic, Legal & Ethical Implications

📘 IGCSE Content

1.1 ICT in Everyday Life

ICT (Information and Communication Technology) has transformed how individuals, businesses, and governments operate.

  • Communication: Email, instant messaging, video calls (Zoom, Teams, FaceTime), social media. Faster, cheaper, global reach. Loss of face-to-face interaction potential.
  • Education: Online learning platforms (Moodle, Google Classroom, Khan Academy), digital textbooks, interactive simulations, virtual labs. Enables distance learning and self-paced study. Concerns: screen time, distraction, unequal access.
  • Healthcare: Electronic health records (EHR), telemedicine, wearable fitness trackers, robotic surgery, AI diagnostics. Faster diagnosis, remote care, personalized medicine. Concerns: data privacy, depersonalization.
  • Employment: Remote work, gig economy platforms (Upwork, Uber, Deliveroo), automation of routine tasks. New job categories in tech; displacement in manufacturing and clerical roles.
  • Entertainment: Streaming (Netflix, Spotify), gaming (online multiplayer, esports), virtual reality. On-demand consumption, personalized recommendations.
  • Government: E-government services (tax filing, license renewal online), digital IDs, open data portals. Increased transparency and efficiency; cybersecurity risks.

1.2 The Digital Divide

The gap between those who have access to ICT and those who do not. Dimensions:

  • Access divide: Physical access to devices and internet infrastructure. Rural vs urban; developed vs developing countries.
  • Use divide: Skills and literacy to use ICT effectively. Older generations, low-education populations.
  • Quality divide: Speed and reliability of connection. Fibre vs dial-up; 5G vs 3G.

Consequences: Limited educational opportunities, reduced economic participation, social exclusion, poorer health outcomes. Solutions: public Wi-Fi, subsidized devices, digital literacy programs, infrastructure investment.

1.3 Health Issues

  • Physical: Repetitive Strain Injury (RSI) from keyboard/mouse use; eye strain from screens; back and neck pain from poor posture; obesity from sedentary lifestyle.
  • Mental: Internet addiction; social media anxiety and FOMO (Fear Of Missing Out); cyberbullying; sleep disruption from blue light exposure; reduced attention span from constant notifications.
  • Mitigation: Ergonomic furniture (adjustable chairs, wrist rests), regular breaks (20-20-20 rule: every 20 min, look 20 feet away for 20 seconds), screen filters, limiting screen time, physical exercise, digital wellness features (Screen Time, Focus modes).

1.4 Environmental Impact

  • Positive: Reduced paper use (dematerialization), remote work reducing transport emissions, smart grids optimizing energy use, precision agriculture reducing waste.
  • Negative: Energy consumption of data centres (2% of global electricity, rising), e-waste (53.6 million tonnes in 2019, only 17.4% recycled), rare earth mineral mining for electronics, carbon footprint of device manufacturing and shipping.
  • Solutions: Renewable-powered data centres, circular economy (repair, reuse, recycle), extended producer responsibility, cloud efficiency, longer device lifecycles.

📗 AS Level Content

2.1 Privacy & Data Protection

  • Personal data: Any information relating to an identified or identifiable natural person. Includes name, ID numbers, location data, online identifiers, biometric, genetic, health data.
  • Data protection principles (GDPR / similar laws):
    • Lawfulness, fairness, and transparency
    • Purpose limitation — collect for specified, explicit, legitimate purposes
    • Data minimization — adequate, relevant, limited to what is necessary
    • Accuracy — kept up to date; rectify inaccuracies
    • Storage limitation — kept no longer than necessary
    • Integrity and confidentiality — secure processing
    • Accountability — controller responsible for compliance
  • Individual rights: Right to be informed, access, rectification, erasure ("right to be forgotten"), restrict processing, data portability, object, rights related to automated decision-making.
  • Consent: Must be freely given, specific, informed, and unambiguous. Pre-ticked boxes are invalid.

2.2 Cybercrime & Legislation

  • Hacking: Unauthorized access to computer systems. Motivations: financial gain, espionage, activism (hacktivism), sabotage.
  • Identity theft: Stealing personal information to impersonate someone, often for financial fraud.
  • Computer Misuse Act 1990 (UK):
    • Section 1: Unauthorized access to computer material (basic hacking)
    • Section 2: Unauthorized access with intent to commit further offences
    • Section 3: Unauthorized acts with intent to impair operation of computer (malware, DDoS)
    • Section 3ZA: Unauthorized acts causing serious damage (national security, life-threatening)
  • Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988: Protects original works. Software is literary work. Prohibits copying, distributing, adapting without license. Fair dealing exceptions limited.
  • Other laws: Fraud Act, Data Protection Act, Regulation of Investigatory Powers Act, Digital Economy Act.

2.3 Ethical Issues

  • Surveillance: CCTV, facial recognition, phone tracking, internet monitoring by governments and corporations. Security vs privacy tension. Snowden revelations highlighted mass surveillance programs.
  • Censorship: Government blocking of websites, content removal, social media bans. Protects citizens from harm but can suppress free speech and dissent.
  • Artificial Intelligence bias: Algorithms trained on biased data produce discriminatory outcomes in hiring, lending, policing, criminal sentencing.
  • Automation & unemployment: AI and robotics replacing workers. Need for reskilling, universal basic income debates, new economic models.
  • Digital footprints: Every online action leaves traces. Permanent, searchable, potentially damaging to reputation. Right to be forgotten debates.

2.4 Intellectual Property

  • Copyright: Automatic protection for original creative works. No registration needed in most jurisdictions. Duration: author's life + 70 years.
  • Patents: Protect inventions. Must be novel, non-obvious, useful. Granted by patent office. Duration: typically 20 years. Software patents controversial.
  • Trademarks: Protect brands, logos, slogans. Distinctive signs of origin. Renewable indefinitely.
  • Trade secrets: Confidential business information (Coca-Cola formula). Protected by non-disclosure agreements.
  • Open source & Creative Commons: Alternative licensing allowing reuse with conditions (attribution, share-alike, non-commercial).

📙 A2 Level Content

3.1 Globalisation & ICT

ICT has accelerated globalisation by enabling instant communication, remote collaboration, and global supply chains.

  • Offshoring & outsourcing: Companies relocate operations to lower-cost countries enabled by reliable internet and communication tools. Benefits: cost reduction, 24-hour operations. Drawbacks: job losses in home country, quality control issues, exploitation concerns.
  • E-commerce: Global marketplaces (Amazon, Alibaba, eBay) connect buyers and sellers worldwide. Benefits: wider choice, lower prices, convenience. Challenges: counterfeit goods, consumer protection across borders, tax avoidance.
  • Digital currencies: Cryptocurrencies (Bitcoin, Ethereum) enable borderless transactions without intermediaries. Challenges: volatility, regulatory uncertainty, environmental cost of proof-of-work mining, use in illicit activities.

3.2 AI, Automation & the Future of Work

  • Industry 4.0: Smart factories using IoT, AI, robotics, big data analytics. Cyber-physical systems communicate and make decisions autonomously.
  • Job displacement: McKinsey estimates 800 million jobs could be automated by 2030. Most at risk: routine physical and cognitive tasks. Least at risk: creative, emotional intelligence, complex social interaction.
  • Job creation: New roles: AI trainers, data ethicists, drone operators, sustainability consultants, human-machine interaction designers.
  • Reskilling: Lifelong learning essential. Governments and companies invest in retraining programs. Micro-credentials and online certifications gain value.
  • UBI (Universal Basic Income): Proposed solution to technological unemployment. Regular unconditional payment to all citizens. Pilots in Finland, Kenya, Canada show mixed results.

3.3 Misinformation & Media Literacy

  • Fake news: Deliberately false information spread via social media and messaging apps. Motivations: political manipulation, financial gain (clickbait), entertainment.
  • Deepfakes: AI-generated realistic but fake video/audio. Threats to reputation, democracy, evidence integrity. Detection tools emerging but arms race continues.
  • Echo chambers & filter bubbles: Algorithms show content reinforcing existing beliefs. Polarization, radicalization risks.
  • Countermeasures: Fact-checking organizations, media literacy education, platform content moderation, algorithmic transparency, watermarking authentic content.

3.4 ICT in Development

  • M-Pesa (Kenya): Mobile money transforming financial inclusion in East Africa. No bank account needed. Model replicated globally.
  • Telemedicine in rural areas: Remote diagnosis via smartphones, connecting patients with specialists in cities.
  • Precision agriculture: Drones, sensors, and AI optimize irrigation and fertilizer use, increasing yields and conserving resources.
  • Challenges: Infrastructure gaps, electricity access, literacy barriers, local language support, affordability, sustainability of donor-funded projects.

3.5 Evaluating ICT Solutions

When assessing ICT systems or policies, consider:

  • Technical feasibility: Can it be built with current technology? Scalability?
  • Economic viability: Cost-benefit analysis, ROI, total cost of ownership (TCO).
  • Social impact: Who benefits? Who is excluded? Effect on communities and culture?
  • Ethical implications: Privacy, autonomy, fairness, transparency, accountability.
  • Legal compliance: Data protection, accessibility standards, industry regulations.
  • Environmental impact: Energy use, e-waste, lifecycle assessment.
  • Security: Risk assessment, threat modelling, resilience.

💻 Worked Example

Question: Convert denary 157 to 8-bit binary.

Solution:
128 + 16 + 8 + 4 + 1 = 157
1 0 0 1 1 1 0 1
Answer: 10011101
Confusing bits and bytes. 1 byte = 8 bits. File sizes in bytes; transfer speeds in bits.
Fetch-decode-execute: 1) Fetch instruction from memory, 2) Decode operation, 3) Execute. Repeats millions of times/second.
