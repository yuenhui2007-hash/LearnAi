Stalin's Russia, 1924–1953

history-h9

📘 IGCSE Content

Power Struggle (1924–29)

  • Lenin dies (1924): Testament criticised Stalin; not published
  • Trotsky: Brilliant orator; Red Army founder; "permanent revolution"
  • Stalin: General Secretary; built power base in party apparatus
  • Strategies: Allied with Zinoviev and Kamenev against Trotsky; then against them
  • 1929: Trotsky expelled; Stalin sole leader

Collectivisation

  • 1929: Forced merger of small farms into collective farms (kolkhoz)
  • Kulaks: Wealthy peasants; deported or killed (5 million deaths)
  • Consequences: Famine 1932–33 (Ukraine — Holodomor); 7+ million dead

Five-Year Plans

  • 1st Plan (1928–32): Heavy industry; Magnitogorsk, Dnieprostroi
  • 2nd Plan (1933–37): Stakhanovite movement; continued industrial growth
  • Results: USSR became industrial power; consumer goods neglected

📗 AS Level Content

Great Terror (1936–38)

  • Show trials: Old Bolsheviks confessed to fabricated crimes (Zinoviev, Kamenev, Bukharin)
  • Purges: 700,000 executed; millions sent to Gulag
  • Reasons: Eliminate rivals; create scapegoats; maintain fear

Propaganda & Cult of Personality

  • Stalin portrayed as: Father of the nation, genius, Lenin's true heir
  • Control of media: All newspapers, radio, film state-controlled
  • Education: Marxist-Leninist curriculum; history rewritten

📙 A2 Level Content

World War II & Post-War

  • Great Patriotic War (1941–45): 27 million Soviet deaths; Stalin's leadership questioned after early defeats
  • Post-war: USSR emerges as superpower; Eastern Europe under Soviet control
  • Cold War begins: Stalin's suspicion of West; Iron Curtain

Evaluation of Stalinism

  • Achievements: Industrialisation, victory in WWII, superpower status
  • Costs: Famine, terror, Gulag, environmental destruction, loss of freedoms
  • Comparison: Stalin vs Hitler — totalitarian parallels and differences

📝 Last-Minute Summary

  • 1924–29: Power struggle; Stalin defeats Trotsky
  • 1929: Collectivisation begins; kulaks eliminated
  • 1928–37: Five-Year Plans; rapid industrialisation
  • 1932–33: Famine (Holodomor) — 7+ million dead
  • 1936–38: Great Terror; show trials; purges
  • 1941–45: Great Patriotic War; 27 million deaths
  • Evaluation: Industrial superpower at enormous human cost
