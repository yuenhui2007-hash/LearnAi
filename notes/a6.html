0452 / 9706 — Income Statement & Statement of Financial Position

IGCSE Content

Two Main Financial Statements

  • Income Statement (Profit & Loss Account): Shows financial performance over a period. Revenue minus expenses = profit or loss.
  • Statement of Financial Position (Balance Sheet): Shows financial position at a point in time. Assets, liabilities, and capital.

AS Level Content

Adjustments Before Preparing Statements

  • Accruals: Expenses used but not yet paid → add to expense, create liability
  • Prepayments: Expenses paid but not yet used → deduct from expense, create asset
  • Depreciation: Reduce asset value, charge to expenses
  • Irrecoverable debts: Remove from receivables, charge as expense
  • Allowance for doubtful debts: Estimate future bad debts → expense and contra-asset
  • Closing inventory: Count and value at lower of cost and NRV

Cost of Sales — Retail vs Manufacturing

For a retailer: Cost of Sales = Opening Inventory + Purchases − Purchases Returns − Closing Inventory

For a manufacturer: Cost of Sales = Opening Inventory of Finished Goods + Cost of Production − Closing Inventory of Finished Goods

Cost of Production = Direct Materials + Direct Labour + Direct Expenses + Production Overheads + Opening WIP − Closing WIP

A2 Level Content

IAS 1 Presentation of Financial Statements

  • Financial statements must present fairly the financial position and performance
  • Items must be classified as current or non-current
  • Comparative prior period figures must be shown
  • Disclosure notes required for accounting policies, contingencies, commitments

Revenue Recognition (IFRS 15)

Revenue is recognised when control of goods/services transfers to the customer:

  • Identify the contract with customer
  • Identify performance obligations
  • Determine transaction price
  • Allocate price to obligations
  • Recognise revenue when obligation is satisfied

Capital vs Revenue Expenditure Revisited

Misclassification affects:

  • Profit (revenue expenditure hits P&L immediately; capital expenditure is depreciated over time)
  • Asset values on SOFP
  • Future period profits (through depreciation)

Last-Minute Summary

  • Income Statement: Revenue − Cost of Sales = Gross Profit; Gross Profit − Expenses + Other Income = Net Profit
  • SOFP: Non-Current Assets + Current Assets = Total Assets; Capital + Liabilities = Total Equity & Liabilities
  • Cost of Sales = Opening Inventory + Net Purchases − Closing Inventory
  • Capital at end = Opening Capital + Profit − Drawings + Capital Introduced
  • Current assets: inventory, receivables (less allowance), prepayments, cash
  • Current liabilities: payables, accruals, bank overdraft, short-term loans
  • Adjustments: accruals (+expense/+liability), prepayments (−expense/+asset), depreciation (+expense/−NCA)
  • Inventory valued at lower of cost and NRV
  • Irrecoverable debts = expense; allowance for doubtful debts = contra-asset
  • Revenue recognised when control transfers to customer (IFRS 15)
