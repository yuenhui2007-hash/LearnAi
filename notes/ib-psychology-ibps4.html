Abnormal Psychology

IB DP Psychology HL/SL — Option 1

Overview

Abnormal Psychology (Option 1) examines psychological disorders, their causes, and treatments. It is a popular option topic examined in Paper 2. Students must understand etiological explanations from biological, cognitive, and sociocultural perspectives, as well as treatment efficacy and ethical considerations.

4.1 Defining Abnormality

  • Statistical infrequency: behaviour rare in population. Limitation: some rare behaviours are not disorders (e.g., high IQ).
  • Deviation from social norms: behaviour violating cultural expectations. Limitation: norms change over time and across cultures.
  • Failure to function adequately: inability to cope with daily life. Limitation: some functional people may still have disorders.
  • Deviation from ideal mental health (Jahoda): failure to meet criteria for positive well-being. Limitation: criteria are culturally biased toward Western individualism.

4.2 Depression

Biological Etiology

  • Serotonin and norepinephrine imbalances.
  • Genetic predisposition (twin studies show moderate heritability).
  • HPA axis dysregulation — elevated cortisol.

Cognitive Etiology

  • Beck's negative triad: negative views of self, world, and future.
  • Learned helplessness (Seligman): attributing negative events to internal, stable, global causes.

Sociocultural Etiology

  • Social support deficits.
  • Gender differences (women report depression at higher rates — possible due to reporting bias or social stressors).
  • SES and life stressors.

Treatments

  • Biological: SSRIs (e.g., fluoxetine/Prozac), SNRIs. ECT for severe, treatment-resistant cases.
  • Cognitive: CBT — challenge negative thoughts, behavioural activation.
  • Sociocultural: group therapy, family therapy, community-based interventions.

4.3 PTSD

  • Develops after exposure to traumatic event.
  • Symptoms: re-experiencing (flashbacks), avoidance, negative cognitions, hyperarousal.
  • Etiology: biological (amygdala hyperactivity, hippocampus shrinkage), cognitive (memory fragmentation), sociocultural (social support buffers risk).
  • Treatments: CBT with exposure therapy, EMDR, SSRIs.

4.4 Stigma

  • Social disapproval and discrimination toward those with mental disorders.
  • Reduces help-seeking behaviour.
  • Anti-stigma campaigns (e.g., Time to Change) aim to improve attitudes through education and contact.

Common Misconceptions

  • Depression = sadness: Clinical depression involves persistent symptoms affecting functioning, not just feeling sad.
  • Biological determinism: Biological factors increase risk but do not guarantee disorder — diathesis-stress model applies.
  • ECT: Modern ECT is safe and effective for severe depression — misconceptions come from outdated portrayals.

Practice Questions

  • Discuss two definitions of abnormality and evaluate one.
  • Evaluate cognitive explanations of one disorder.
  • Compare the effectiveness of biological and psychological treatments for depression.
  • To what extent can mental disorders be explained by biological factors alone?

Last-Minute Summary

  • Definitions of abnormality: statistical, social norms, failure to function, ideal mental health.
  • Depression: biological (serotonin, genetics), cognitive (Beck's triad, learned helplessness), sociocultural (SES, gender).
  • PTSD: trauma-related; biological, cognitive, and sociocultural factors.
  • Treatments: SSRIs, CBT, ECT, EMDR, group/family therapy.
  • Stigma reduces help-seeking; anti-stigma campaigns use education and contact.

Exam Tips

  • Use named studies (e.g., Beck, Seligman, Brown & Harris) for evidence.
  • Always consider the interaction of biological, cognitive, and sociocultural factors.
  • For treatment questions, evaluate efficacy and ethical issues.
  • Include cross-cultural evidence where relevant.
