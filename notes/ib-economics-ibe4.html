The Global Economy

IB DP Economics HL/SL — Topic 4

Overview

The Global Economy (Topic 4) examines international trade, exchange rates, the balance of payments, and development economics. It connects micro and macro concepts to the global context and is tested across all papers. Understanding exchange rate mechanisms and trade theory is essential.

4.1 International Trade

Comparative Advantage

A country should specialise in producing goods where it has a lower opportunity cost.

Opportunity cost of good X = quantity of good Y given up / quantity of good X gained
Countries gain from trade by specialising and trading at a rate between their opportunity costs.

Trade Protection

  • Tariffs: taxes on imports → higher domestic price, reduced quantity imported.
  • Quotas: physical limit on imports → higher price, reduced quantity.
  • Subsidies: domestic producers receive payments → lower costs, more competitive.

Arguments for protection: infant industries, national security, diversification, retaliation against dumping.

Arguments against: higher prices for consumers, reduced efficiency, risk of retaliation.

4.2 Exchange Rates

Determination of Exchange Rates

  • Floating: determined by demand and supply in foreign exchange markets.
  • Fixed: government/pegged to another currency or basket.
  • Managed float: mostly market-determined with occasional government intervention.

Factors Affecting Exchange Rates

  • Interest rate differentials.
  • Inflation differentials.
  • Current account balance.
  • Speculation and confidence.

Effects of Exchange Rate Changes

  • Appreciation: exports more expensive, imports cheaper → current account may worsen (Marshall-Lerner condition).
  • Depreciation: exports cheaper, imports more expensive → current account may improve.

4.3 Balance of Payments

Current account = trade in goods + trade in services + income + transfers
Capital account + financial account = capital transfers + investment flows
Current account + capital account + financial account = 0 (with statistical discrepancy)

Causes of Current Account Deficits

  • Loss of competitiveness (high inflation, low productivity).
  • Strong currency.
  • High marginal propensity to import.
  • Reliance on imports for energy/raw materials.

Policies to Correct Deficits

  • Expenditure-reducing (contractionary fiscal/monetary).
  • Expenditure-switching (depreciation, protectionism, supply-side policies).

4.4 Economic Development

  • Economic growth: increase in real GDP.
  • Economic development: broader improvement in living standards, including health, education, and equality.

Development Indicators

  • GDP per capita (PPP adjusted).
  • Human Development Index (HDI) — combines income, education, life expectancy.
  • Multidimensional Poverty Index (MPI).
  • Gini coefficient — income inequality (0 = perfect equality, 1 = perfect inequality).

Barriers to Development

  • Poor infrastructure, education, and healthcare.
  • Political instability and corruption.
  • Geographic disadvantages (landlocked, tropical diseases).
  • Debt burdens and unequal trade terms.

Development Strategies

  • Import substitution vs. export-led growth.
  • Microfinance and fair trade.
  • Foreign direct investment (FDI).
  • Structural adjustment programmes (controversial).

4.5 Sustainability

  • Sustainable development: meeting present needs without compromising future generations.
  • Trade-off between economic growth and environmental protection.
  • Renewable energy, carbon pricing, and international agreements (Paris Agreement).

Common Misconceptions

  • Absolute vs. comparative advantage: Comparative advantage is about opportunity cost, not absolute productivity.
  • Appreciation and exports: Currency appreciation makes exports more expensive in foreign currency terms, not domestic terms.
  • Current account deficit: A deficit is not always bad — it may reflect investment and growth.
  • Growth vs. development: Growth is quantitative (GDP); development is qualitative (HDI, living standards).

Exam-Specific Information

  • Paper 1 (Part a): 10-mark questions on comparative advantage, exchange rates, and trade protection.
  • Paper 1 (Part b): 15-mark evaluations of development strategies or trade policies.
  • Paper 2: Data response with exchange rate calculations, balance of payments data, and development indicators.
  • IA: Global economy commentaries on exchange rates, trade, or development are common.

Practice Questions

  • Country A can produce 100 cars or 200 computers. Country B can produce 80 cars or 160 computers. Explain whether there is a basis for trade.
  • Explain how a tariff affects domestic consumers, producers, and government revenue.
  • Distinguish between a current account deficit and a capital account surplus.
  • Evaluate the use of GDP per capita as a measure of economic development.

Last-Minute Summary

  • Comparative advantage: specialise where opportunity cost is lowest.
  • Trade protection: tariffs, quotas, subsidies — know effects on welfare.
  • Exchange rates: floating (market), fixed (pegged), managed.
  • Current account = trade + services + income + transfers.
  • Development: broader than growth; measured by HDI, MPI, Gini.
  • Sustainability: balancing present and future needs.

Exam Tips

  • Always define key terms in your opening sentence.
  • Draw accurate diagrams for tariffs and exchange rates.
  • Use real-world examples (e.g., China's export-led growth, EU tariffs).
  • In evaluation, consider short-run vs. long-run and different stakeholders.
  • For development questions, use data from the World Bank or UN.
