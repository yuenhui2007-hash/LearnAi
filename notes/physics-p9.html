IGCSE AS Level

D.C. Circuits

9702 (A-Level) · 0625 (IGCSE)

Detailed Notes

1. Circuit Symbols

Know standard circuit symbols: cell, battery, resistor, variable resistor, lamp, switch, ammeter, voltmeter, fuse, diode, LED, thermistor, LDR.

2. Series Circuits

  • Current same through all components.
  • Total p.d. = sum of individual p.d.s.
  • Total resistance: R_total = R₁ + R₂ + R₃ + ...

3. Parallel Circuits

  • P.d. same across all branches.
  • Total current = sum of branch currents.
  • Total resistance: 1/R_total = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + 1/R₃ + ...

4. Potential Divider

V_out = V_in × R₂/(R₁ + R₂)

5. Kirchhoff's Laws

  • First Law (Current): Total current entering a junction = total current leaving.
  • Second Law (Voltage): Sum of e.m.f.s = sum of p.d.s around any closed loop.

6. Internal Resistance

E = V + Ir = I(R + r)

Where E = e.m.f., V = terminal p.d., I = current, R = external resistance, r = internal resistance.

7. Sensors

  • Thermistor: resistance decreases as temperature increases.
  • LDR (Light Dependent Resistor): resistance decreases as light intensity increases.

Additional Applications

  • Real-world context: Engineering applications, medical physics, environmental monitoring
  • Experimental methods: Common apparatus, measurement techniques, error analysis
  • SI units in practice: Prefix usage in scientific and everyday contexts

Last-Minute Summary

  • Series: I same, V adds, R_total = R₁+R₂+R₃...
  • Parallel: V same, I adds, 1/R_total = 1/R₁+1/R₂+1/R₃...
  • Potential divider: V_out = V_in × R₂/(R₁+R₂)
  • Kirchhoff 1st: ΣI_in = ΣI_out at junction
  • Kirchhoff 2nd: Σe.m.f. = Σp.d. around loop
  • E = V + Ir = I(R+r)
  • Thermistor: R↓ as T↑; LDR: R↓ as light↑

📐 Worked Example

Question: A ball is thrown vertically upwards at 20 m/s. Calculate the maximum height reached. (g = 9.81 m/s²)

Solution:
At max height, v = 0. Using v² = u² + 2as:
0 = 20² + 2(−9.81)s
0 = 400 − 19.62s
s = 400 / 19.62 = 20.4 m
Answer: 20.4 m
Using g = +9.81 m/s² when the object is moving upwards — g always acts downwards, so use −9.81 m/s² for upward motion.
Always draw a diagram and define your positive direction before solving kinematics problems.

⚡ Quick Recall

  • v = u + at (no displacement)
  • s = ut + ½at² (no final velocity)
  • v² = u² + 2as (no time)
  • s = ½(u+v)t (no acceleration)
  • All apply only for constant acceleration
