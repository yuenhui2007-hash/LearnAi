Control Accounts

0452 / 9706 — Sales and Purchases Ledger Controls

IGCSE Content

Purpose of Control Accounts

Control accounts are summary accounts in the General Ledger that mirror the total of all individual accounts in the subsidiary (sales/purchases) ledgers. They serve as a check on the accuracy of the ledger system and help detect errors and fraud.

  • Sales Ledger Control Account: Total amount owed by ALL credit customers
  • Purchases Ledger Control Account: Total amount owed TO ALL credit suppliers

Sales Ledger Control Account Format

Sales Ledger Control Account
-----------------------------------------------
Dr Side (Increases receivables):
Balance b/d (opening debit)
Credit sales (from Sales Day Book)
Dishonoured cheques
Interest charged on overdue accounts

Cr Side (Decreases receivables):
Cash/bank received from customers
Sales returns (from Sales Returns Day Book)
Discount allowed
Irrecoverable debts written off
Set-offs (contra with purchases ledger)
Balance c/d (closing debit)

Purchases Ledger Control Account Format

Purchases Ledger Control Account
-----------------------------------------------
Dr Side (Decreases payables):
Cash/bank paid to suppliers
Purchases returns (from Purchases Returns Day Book)
Discount received
Set-offs (contra with sales ledger)
Balance c/d (closing credit)

Cr Side (Increases payables):
Balance b/d (opening credit)
Credit purchases (from Purchases Day Book)
Interest charged by suppliers

AS Level Content

Reconciling the Control Account

The balance on the control account should equal the sum of all individual balances in the subsidiary ledger:

  1. Extract list of individual balances from sales/purchases ledger
  2. Calculate total
  3. Compare with control account balance
  4. If different, investigate errors in either the individual accounts or the control account

Common Errors Found

  • Casting errors: Day book totals added incorrectly
  • Omission: Transaction recorded in individual account but not control (or vice versa)
  • Wrong amount: Different figures in individual and control account
  • Transposition: Digits reversed (e.g., $540 recorded as $450)
  • Contra entry omitted: Set-off between sales and purchases ledger not recorded in both

Contra Entries (Set-offs)

When a business is both a customer and supplier of the same party:

Set-off $500: Business owes supplier $500 and customer owes business $500
Journal entry:
Dr Purchases Ledger Control $500
Cr Sales Ledger Control $500
(Also post to individual accounts in each subsidiary ledger)

A2 Level Content

Self-Balancing Ledgers

A self-balancing ledger system means each ledger (general, sales, purchases) can be checked independently:

  • General ledger control accounts for sales and purchases ledgers
  • Adjustment accounts in the subsidiary ledgers for the general ledger
  • Each ledger balances on its own — errors localised faster

Advantages of Control Accounts

  • Fraud prevention: Separation of duties; one person maintains control, another the subsidiary ledger
  • Error detection: Discrepancies highlight where to investigate
  • Quick totals: Total receivables/payables known without listing all accounts
  • Efficiency: Trial balance extracted from general ledger only; subsidiary ledgers not needed

Limitations

  • Compensating errors may hide problems
  • Errors in both control and subsidiary may cancel out
  • Doesn't detect errors where the same wrong entry appears in both

  • Sales Ledger Control = total owed by ALL customers (Dr balance normally)
  • Purchases Ledger Control = total owed TO ALL suppliers (Cr balance normally)
  • SLC Dr: opening balance, credit sales, dishonoured cheques, interest
