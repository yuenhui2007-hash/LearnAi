Current Electricity

9702 (A-Level)

Last-Minute Revision Summary

  • I = Q/t (A) -- rate of flow of charge; conventional current: + to -
  • Metals: free electrons; Electrolytes: ions; Semiconductors: electrons and holes
  • Drift velocity: I = nAve (very small, ~10^-4 m/s, because n is enormous)
  • V = W/Q (V) -- energy transferred per unit charge
  • R = V/I (Ohm); Ohm's Law: V proportional to I at constant temperature (metallic conductors only)
  • Resistivity: R = rho L/A -- material property, independent of dimensions
  • Power: P = VI = I^2 R = V^2/R
  • Resistance increases with temperature for metals; decreases for semiconductors and thermistors

Key Formulas

I = Q/t
V = W/Q
R = V/I
R = rho L/A
P = VI = I^2 R = V^2/R
I = nAve

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Applying Ohm's Law to non-ohmic components (filament lamps, diodes, thermistors)
  • Forgetting to convert mm^2 to m^2 (1 mm^2 = 10^-6 m^2)
  • Confusing drift velocity with speed of electric signal/propagation
  • Using total length instead of length of conductor in resistivity formula

Quick Exam Tips

  • Always check whether a component is ohmic before applying V = IR
  • Convert all lengths to metres and areas to m^2 before calculating resistivity
  • Drift velocity is typically very small -- if answer is m/s, check calculation
  • For power, choose the formula using quantities you know
  • Conventional current flows + to -; electron flow is - to +
  • In resistivity experiments, measure diameter at several points and average
