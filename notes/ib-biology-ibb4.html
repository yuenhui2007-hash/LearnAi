Ecology

IB DP Biology HL/SL — Topic 4

Overview

Ecology (Topic 4) studies interactions between organisms and their environment. It is highly relevant to current global issues and is frequently tested with data-based questions in Paper 3. Understanding energy flow, nutrient cycles, and conservation strategies is essential.

4.1 Species, Communities & Ecosystems

  • Species: group of organisms that can interbreed to produce fertile offspring.
  • Population: group of organisms of the same species living in the same area.
  • Community: all populations of different species living and interacting in an area.
  • Ecosystem: community plus its abiotic environment.
  • Habitat: the environment in which a species normally lives.
  • Niche: the role of a species within an ecosystem (biotic and abiotic interactions).

4.2 Energy Flow

  • Energy enters ecosystems as light (photosynthesis).
  • Energy is lost at each trophic level through respiration, heat, and incomplete consumption.
Gross primary production (GPP) = total energy fixed by producers
Net primary production (NPP) = GPP − respiration (R)
NPP = energy available to next trophic level

Ecological efficiency = (energy at trophic level n+1 / energy at trophic level n) × 100%
Typically 10% transfer between levels

4.3 Carbon Cycle

  • Photosynthesis: CO₂ → organic compounds (removes CO₂ from atmosphere).
  • Respiration: organic compounds → CO₂ (returns CO₂ to atmosphere).
  • Combustion: fossil fuels → CO₂.
  • Decomposition: dead organic matter broken down by detritivores and decomposers.
  • Diffusion: CO₂ dissolves in oceans and is used by marine organisms.

4.4 Climate Change

  • Increased atmospheric CO₂ from fossil fuel combustion and deforestation.
  • Greenhouse effect: CO₂, CH₄, and N₂O trap infrared radiation, warming the Earth.
  • Consequences: rising sea levels, more extreme weather, biodiversity loss, ocean acidification.
Ocean acidification: CO₂ + H₂O → H₂CO₃ → H⁺ + HCO₃⁻
Increased H⁺ reduces carbonate ion availability, affecting shell-forming organisms.

4.5 Conservation

  • In situ conservation: protecting species in their natural habitat (national parks, reserves).
  • Ex situ conservation: protecting species outside their natural habitat (zoos, seed banks, captive breeding).
  • Rewilding: restoring ecosystems to their natural state.
  • Sustainable development: meeting present needs without compromising future generations.

Common Misconceptions

  • Food chains vs. food webs: Food chains are linear; food webs show interconnected chains and are more stable.
  • Energy recycling: Energy is NOT recycled in ecosystems — it flows through and is lost as heat. Nutrients (matter) are recycled.
  • Carbon sink: Oceans and forests absorb CO₂, but their capacity is limited and affected by temperature.
  • Biodiversity: Includes genetic, species, and ecosystem diversity — not just the number of species.

Exam-Specific Information

  • Paper 1: 3–4 MCQs on ecological terminology, energy transfer, and carbon cycle processes.
  • Paper 2: Structured questions on energy flow calculations, climate change, and conservation strategies.
  • Paper 3: Data analysis of population data, ecological surveys, and climate data.
  • IA: Ecosystem surveys, population modelling, and carbon footprint investigations are common.

Practice Questions

  • Calculate the NPP of a forest if GPP = 25 000 kJ m⁻² yr⁻¹ and respiration = 15 000 kJ m⁻² yr⁻¹.
  • Explain why food chains rarely have more than five trophic levels.
  • Describe two ways in which human activity has altered the carbon cycle.
  • Evaluate the advantages and disadvantages of in situ versus ex situ conservation.

Last-Minute Summary

  • Ecosystem = community + abiotic factors.
  • Energy flows; nutrients cycle.
  • NPP = GPP − R; ecological efficiency ≈ 10%.
  • Carbon cycle: photosynthesis, respiration, combustion, decomposition, ocean diffusion.
  • Climate change: increased greenhouse gases → global warming → sea level rise, extreme weather, ocean acidification.
  • Conservation: in situ (natural habitat) and ex situ (outside natural habitat).

Exam Tips

  • Read each question carefully and identify the command term before answering.
  • Use correct ecological terminology (e.g., "trophic level," "net primary production").
  • For "evaluate" questions, give both advantages and disadvantages with justification.
  • In "explain" questions, use the 10% rule and refer to energy losses.
  • Draw clear, labelled diagrams for food chains and carbon cycles.
