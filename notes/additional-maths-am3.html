Indices & Surds

Laws of Indices

aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ
aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ
(aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ
a⁰ = 1
a⁻ⁿ = 1aⁿ
am/n = (n√a)ᵐ

Surds (Irrational Roots)

√a × √b = √(ab)
√a√b = √(a/b)
√a + √b cannot be simplified (unless a = b)
Rationalising: 1√a = √aa
Worked Example

Simplify: 3√2 + √8√2

√8 = 2√2, so numerator = 3√2 + 2√2 = 5√2

= 5√2√2 = 5

Worked Example — Rationalising

Rationalise: 23 − √5

= 2(3 + √5)(3−√5)(3+√5) = 2(3 + √5)9 − 5 = 3 + √52

Key Point: When rationalising denominators with binomial surds, multiply numerator and denominator by the conjugate (change the sign of the surd term).

📝 Quick Summary

  • aᵐ × aⁿ = aᵐ⁺ⁿ; aᵐ ÷ aⁿ = aᵐ⁻ⁿ; (aᵐ)ⁿ = aᵐⁿ
  • √a × √b = √(ab)
  • Rationalise by multiplying by the conjugate
  • a⁻ⁿ = 1/aⁿ; am/n = (n√a)ᵐ

💡 Exam Tips

  • Learn the laws of indices thoroughly — they appear in many topics.
  • Always rationalise denominators containing surds.
  • Simplify surds by extracting square factors (e.g., √12 = 2√3).
  • Be careful with negative and fractional indices.
