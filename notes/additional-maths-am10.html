Permutations & Combinations

Factorial Notation

n! = n × (n−1) × (n−2) × ... × 2 × 1
0! = 1

Permutations (order matters)

ⁿPᵣ = n!(n−r)!

Combinations (order doesn't matter)

ⁿCᵣ = n!r!(n−r)!
Worked Example

How many ways can 4 people be chosen from 10?

¹⁰C₄ = 10!4!6! = 10×9×8×724 = 210

Worked Example

How many 3-letter arrangements from 'MATHS'?

⁵P₃ = 5!2! = 5 × 4 × 3 = 60

Worked Example — With Restrictions

How many 4-digit numbers can be formed from 1,2,3,4,5 with no repetition?

⁵P₄ = 120

How many are even? Last digit must be 2 or 4 (2 choices). First 3 digits from remaining 4: ⁴P₃ = 24. Total = 2 × 24 = 48

Key Point: Use permutations when arranging or ordering matters. Use combinations when selecting groups where order is irrelevant.

📝 Quick Summary

  • ⁿPᵣ = n!/(n−r)! (order matters)
  • ⁿCᵣ = n!/[r!(n−r)!] (order doesn't matter)
  • Deal with restrictions first
  • ⁿCᵣ = ⁿCₙ₋ᵣ

💡 Exam Tips

  • Identify whether order matters before choosing permutation or combination.
  • For problems with restrictions, deal with the restriction first.
  • ⁿCᵣ = ⁿCₙ₋ᵣ — use this to simplify calculations.
  • Always show your working clearly in counting problems.
